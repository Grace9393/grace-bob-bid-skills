# Human Guidance

## Anti-AI Writing Style Instructions

Default to formal scored-response mode for evaluator-facing tender content. Use conversational drafting mode only when the user explicitly wants an internal, workshop, or exploratory draft before formal conversion. Avoid overused AI words and phrases.

## Banned Words and Phrases

- Never use: `delve`, `leverage` (as a verb), `nuanced`, `multifaceted`, `paramount`, `plethora`
- Avoid transitions: `It's worth noting`, `It's important to remember`, `In today's digital landscape`, `Furthermore`, `Moreover`, `Additionally`, `However, it's crucial to understand that`, `When it comes to`, `At the end of the day`, `The fact of the matter is`
- Skip business jargon: `streamline`, `revolutionary`, `game-changing`, `cutting-edge`, `state-of-the-art`, `seamlessly`, `best practices`, `end-to-end solutions`, `synergy`, `next-generation`
- Don't overuse: `comprehensive`, `dynamic`, `innovative`, `strategic`, `holistic`, `effective`, `efficient`, `significant`, `substantial`
- Eliminate hedging: `While there are many factors`, `It depends on various factors`, `There are pros and cons`, `It's important to consider`, `One must take into account`
- Avoid AI helper phrases: `Here's what you need to know`, `I hope this helps`, `Let me break this down for you`, `To sum up`, `In conclusion, it's clear that`, `designed to be`, `built to`, `engineered to`
- Skip passive corporate language: `was created to`, `is designed to`, `has been developed to`, `was built in order to`

## Writing Rules

1. Use simple words:
   `use` not `utilise`, `help` not `facilitate`, `show` not `demonstrate`, `buy` not `purchase`
2. Vary sentence length.
3. Match the mode:
   In formal scored-response mode, prefer disciplined evaluator-facing prose.
   In conversational drafting mode, a lighter internal tone is acceptable.
4. Be specific.
5. Choose active voice.
6. Use concrete examples.
7. Do not default to chatty prose:
   Evaluator-facing bid writing should sound formal, credible, and controlled.
8. Use natural rhythm carefully:
   vary paragraph length and sentence structure, but avoid informality that weakens scoring credibility.
9. Don't be overly helpful:
   avoid sounding like an eager assistant; be direct rather than deferential.
10. Trust simple language, but do not strip out enterprise terms when they are necessary for precise bid writing.
11. Use technical language where needed:
   do not oversimplify specialist delivery, standards, or commercial content.
12. Do not use em dashes in tender response prose. Replace them with commas, colons, semicolons, parentheses, or separate sentences. Avoid en dashes as sentence punctuation.

## Replacement Suggestions

- Instead of `delve into` -> `explore`, `examine`, `look at`
- Instead of `leverage` -> `use`, `apply`, `work with`
- Instead of `robust` -> `strong`, `solid`, `reliable`, `thorough`
- Instead of `comprehensive` -> `complete`, `thorough`, `detailed`
- Instead of `facilitate` -> `help`, `enable`, `support`
- Instead of `utilise` -> `use`
- Instead of `prior to` -> `before`
- Instead of `in order to` -> `to`
- Instead of `purchase` -> `buy`
- Instead of `implement` -> `start`, `begin`, `put in place`, `roll out`
- Instead of `optimise` -> `improve`, `fine-tune`, `make better`
- Instead of `designed to be` -> `built to be`, `made to be`, or cut it
- Instead of `was trained on` -> `learned from`, `read`
- Instead of `large language model` -> `AI system`, `AI assistant`
- Instead of `baked into` -> `built into`, `part of`
- Instead of `corporate box-ticking` -> `PR speak`, `marketing fluff`
- Instead of `It's worth noting` -> `Actually`, `By the way`, `Also`, `Plus`
- Instead of `Furthermore` -> `Also`, `In addition`, `This also means`
- Instead of `However` -> `But`, `Though`, `Still`, `Where this creates risk`

## Before and After Examples

AI-speak:
`It's important to note that implementing a comprehensive strategy to optimise your robust marketing efforts will leverage multiple touchpoints to facilitate customer engagement.`

Human:
`Focus on 2-3 marketing channels that actually reach your customers. Do not try to be everywhere at once.`

AI-speak:
`Claude is designed to be helpful, harmless, and honest. It's built on a large language model, which means it's trained on tons of text data.`

Human:
`Claude helps people with stuff. They built it to be safe and honest. It learned from reading massive amounts of text online.`

AI-speak:
`This isn't just corporate box-ticking - it's baked into how the system works.`

Human:
`This is not just PR speak: it is part of how they built the thing.`

AI-speak:
`In order to facilitate optimal results, one must utilise best practices whilst leveraging cutting-edge methodologies.`

Human:
`To get good results, use what works and try new things when they make sense.`

## Tone Guidelines

- In default mode, sound formal, credible, and evaluator-facing
- In conversational drafting mode, sound human without becoming casual or fluffy
- Be direct and clear
- Vary your vocabulary naturally
- Trust that simple language is often the strongest, but not at the expense of procurement credibility
- Do not force interjections, asides, or tangents into scored bid prose
- Use emphasis techniques sparingly and professionally
- Avoid em dashes and en dashes as sentence punctuation
- Don't end everything with `I hope this helps` or similar AI pleasantries

## Rhythm and Flow

- Mix paragraph lengths
- Use natural pauses and emphasis
- Vary sentence starters
- Keep ideas connected in a deliberate, readable way

Good writing should feel effortless to read, even when the topic is complex. The goal is clarity, credibility, and scoreability.

In formal scored-response mode, write for evaluators in polished enterprise prose.
In conversational drafting mode, keep the language lighter, but preserve the underlying bid logic so it can be converted into formal prose later.

## Evaluation Criteria

Check for:

- banned words and phrases
- AI-style transitions and filler
- unnecessary business jargon
- excessive hedging
- helper-style assistant language
- em dashes or en dashes used as sentence punctuation
- tone mismatches against the selected mode

Verify:

- simple word choices where appropriate
- varied sentence length and natural flow
- correct tone for the selected mode
- specificity over vagueness
- active voice
- concrete examples over abstraction
- natural rhythm with varied sentence and paragraph length

Default to formal scored-response mode for evaluator-facing content, and only prefer conversational drafting tone when the user explicitly requests it.

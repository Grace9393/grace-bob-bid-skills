IBM considers a CI/CD pipe reference architecture for microservices and serverless as part of a DevOps lifecycle of (continuous) workflows: planning, development, integration, deployment, operations, and learning.
Key structural and operational components of a CI/CD pipe of reference:
- Git-based version control with strong pull-request support. Multiple Git repos manage changes to source code and artefact management handles supporting DevOps assets.
- Automated testing in separate pipes due to their long-running nature.
- DevOps engineering and management to build, enhance and maintain CI/CD pipes and design, implement, and deploy scalable solutions. Solutions are evaluated for integration capability, scalability, performance, and strategic product fit. Multidisciplinary squads apply agile ways of working.
- Effective pipe governance with clear purpose, boundaries, and priorities, and semi-autonomous teams empowered through the guardrails of blueprints and best practices to ensure alignment with organisational policies.
- KPIs based on the DevOps Research and Assessment (DORA) framework to track software delivery and pipe performance. These are deployment frequency, lead time for changes, change failure rate and time to restore service. Traceability and auditability of CI/CD should be provided through systematic collection of evidence with an instrumented toolchain.
- A culture of continuous communication, collaboration, and shared responsibility pervading all software delivery stakeholders: development, operations, product owners, security, compliance, governance, risk, and line-of-business teams.
Serverless architectures simplify CI/CD pipe flow as developers spend no time defining infrastructure required to integrate, test, deliver, and deploy code builds into production. Teams focus fully on writing and pushing code, freeing up time to innovate and optimise front-end application business logic. Rolling deployment patterns such as Blue/Green and Canary enable smoother code releases. Rollouts proceed under a bake time where compute resources are monitored for threshold breaches.
Microservices enable and require a CI/CD pipe. Due to the increase in complexity, moving parts and dependencies that come with microservices, we advise investing in deployment, monitoring and lifecycle automation. Runtime models for building and deploying fault-tolerant cloud-native applications (e.g., Istio and Knative) tackle scaling challenges by reducing interdependencies between teams, enabling them to develop, deploy, and run services independently. CI/CD pipes integrate with Istio traffic routing between microservices in the service mesh, resulting in security by design, the ability to innovate faster and continually, and minimalised manual effort.
CI/CD evolution is part of an everything-as-code philosophy where pipes are instantiated for application development, provisioning infrastructure (IaC) and deploying machine-learning models (Figures 1 and 3).
We innovate to bring greater levels of security and compliance to conventional DevOps approaches as well as open-source contributions to Istio (e.g., code, blog posts, documentation, and leading technical workstreams). Our reference architecture has evolved into a secure, compliant DevOps model driven by NIST800-53 controls (based on our experience with financial sector clients) that is extendable to other regulatory contexts, e.g., EU policymaking. A shift-left mindset leads to automated scanning/testing during development to mitigate security risks and compliance violations pre-deployment. The creation of templates for DevOps practices (Figures 2 and 3) and customised toolchains results in a continuous compliance CI/CD pipe, accelerating time to market for cloud-native digital apps compliant with EU policy direction.
IBM can also help in setting up a new DevOps platform from scratch using our Service DevOps Commander tool, which enables the self-service ability to design, build and operate a DevOps platform with toolchain integration of user choice on any target cloud.

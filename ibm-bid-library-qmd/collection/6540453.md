We will work with you to drive continuous improvement for three core groups: 1) the clinical and administrative end-users who directly use the Clinicals products (e.g. SCRa) or use ISV apps that are connected to Clinicals & EPS APIs; 2) the ISV community whose products connect to Spine Clinicals APIs; and 3) our cross-functional delivery squads who deliver the Clinicals products, together with the broader Spine teams. For all 3 groups: 
We will proactively collect feedback and data to understand their business processes, pain points and opportunities for improvement. We will gather both qualitive and quantitative insights. 
Using our understanding to inform the current baseline, we estimate then measure the impact of proposed ideas. We use a value-driven approach, identifying the impact of ideas and prioritising them through an impartial scorecard tailored to the programme’s strategic objectives that removes biases  
Having made changes, we will reflect on our progress by comparing the calculated impact to the actual value created. This keeps us aligned to addressing the programme’s strategic objective: “To better inform clinical decision making across all health and care settings, by enabling authorised Health and Care professionals to access all relevant patient information at the point of care” (Sch. 20 Section 2.8). 
All candidate improvements will be assessed, filtered and prioritised for impact using these factors: problem statement addressed, users impacted, measurable impact, alignment of objectives and key results (OKRs), solution ideas, assumptions, and questions. How they are handled will depend on their type:  
Service and product opportunities: We will prioritise continuous improvement ideas that impact end users as part of the general roadmapping and product backlog process. All items will be reviewed consistently in a comparable manner. We will prioritise opportunities using value orchestration (co-defined with you) to maximise business and user benefits for our team capacity. 
Squad-level ways of working opportunities: These opportunities will be managed in delivery squad retrospectives. Squads will have autonomy in implementing ideas to improve their own metrics (velocity, cadence, technical debt, and engagement), while maintaining co-ordination. Changes measurable by story points will be prioritised in the same way as service and product opportunities.  
Programme-level ways of working opportunities: Senior team members from across our squads will review and prioritise ideas to improve ways of working at programme level in governance reviews. Depending on the effort required, they will work closely with Delivery and Product Managers to plan the required capacity into future sprints. 
We used this approach at the Disclosure and Barring Service, where IBM worked in blended teams to deliver digital services. We drove the product management and engineering approach, using agile sprints, in parallel with other DBS activities before integrating at completion. Using Design Thinking with User Personas, and Journey Maps as unifying artefacts, the service was evolved in a product-centric continuous-improvement manner, with streamlined DevOps CI/CD pipelines to automate and improve delivery of services. This focus on continuous improvement led to DBS winning the ‘Best Use of Customer Insight’ award at the 2020 UK Customer Satisfaction Awards.

A)	Methods, processes and tooling you will use, to deliver advice, design, and delivery support for Architecture and Design
 
Our approach brings together traditional complex programme discipline with the flexibility of agile to achieve the best from both approaches and is fully aligned with the Open Group’s TOGAF. It supports the delivery of advice, design and delivery support, using our flexible, proven, enterprise grade methods, tailored and optimised for both Agile based development and complex integration delivery and have been used in projects with government departments including NHS & HMRC. It comprises:
•	IBM Garage Methodology: a holistic and integrated set of practices and workflows to design, deliver and operate solutions. It encompasses best practices like Enterprise Design Thinking, Agile development, Lean Start-up, and Test Driven Development to build cloud-native and cloud-enabled applications, and
•	IBM Mosaic Method (Fig 1): orientated around complex integration, to bring together diverse solution components, requirements and teams and unifying various approaches and techniques into an integrated, iterative programme of work. Mosaic includes an extensive set of guidance on all aspects of project and programme governance, how to structure and deliver complex programmes, & manage risks, issues, assumptions & dependencies both within and externally to the projects. It aligns well with other large-scale agile approaches such as Scaled Agile Framework (SAFe). 
 
Our IBM teams are proficient at working in customer environments where we collaborate to understand and deliver within customer strategic and governance frameworks. We are skilled in tailoring methods and practices to support the Buyer’s decision-making process. Through a proven approach of method adoption workshops, we have previously aligned our architecture methods to HMRC’s architecture practices.
 
How our Processes are aligned to a selected method to deliver design and delivery support
 
Our high-level design process is to move from a blueprint into design, development and test:
•	Blueprint: IBM will produce architecture documents that detail the proposed solution. We will include this in our internal review process of deliverables (work products) and check they align against your policies/standards and nominate the required skilled resources within the team to provide representation in the required governance forums
•	Development: We will document the system configuration. This begins at the start of the iteration with documenting how the user stories will be implemented in the system and will then be further elaborated as part of the iteration
•	Final Test: We will update the documents based on the defect fixes during testing
 
On all our projects:
 
•	Architect and PM work closely to enable Work Breakdown structures to support Delivery planning. This ensures better technical support feeding into the plan shaping and delivery management processes (e.g. Risk, Issue and Dependency management).
•	All work is underpinned by a consistent set of tooling to manage, store and track the delivery of content.  We use a variety of tools to do this, including Confluence, Jira, Cognitive Architect and Archi (for ArchiMate models), based on client need.
 
On CDS, IBM adapted SAFe to use mixed agile/waterfall delivery processes and SAFe concepts, enhanced by IBM method content to align better with internal HMRC processes.
As shown in Fig 1, we developed E2E architecture documents against which each workstream iterated through the required application features in blueprint. This was done to enough level of to provide to the development teams for delivery and configuration documentation before final testing against a release. Jira was used to document and track the tasks that needed to be completed and to raise defects, with Confluence used to document the designs we produced.
 
We used our IBM Garage methodology and processes to deliver advice to support decision making for key CDS Day 1 No Deal Brexit decisions. We focused on rapid collaboration with the client infusing technical expertise, with strategy and policy specialists to develop potential solutions. We then used our Architecture Decision process to:
 
•	Document assumptions, constraints and motivation
•	Outline potential solution options
•	Evaluate the pros and cons of those options
•	Develop recommendations supporting the Buyer’s need including justification and implications.
 
Standard Architecture Work Products were used to smooth turning of recommended option into delivery through further elaboration rather than starting from scratch 
B) Quality assurance/ensuring quality deliverables
Our Overall approach to Architecture and Design Quality Assurance is multi levelled using a Design Authority (DA) scaled appropriately. This DA will be accountable for ensuring quality deliverables are provided to the Buyer and will ideally be jointly Buyer/IBM staffed to ensure decisions are bought into in a timely manner.
 
As demonstrated in work on numerous Government projects including HMRC CDS and NHS, we will ensure quality in work product creation by (see Fig 2): 
1.	Guidance and knowledge sharing: Technical Working Group (TWG) sessions with SMEs to ensure early alignment and provide the opportunities for design challenges to be resolved quickly with guidance provided. Knowledge gained here will be shared across the function
2.	Formal project review: Formal Project governance will agree solution outline early before agreeing complete designs. Peer review of the work products prior to DA submission, scored against our QA checklist, will ensure a quality threshold prior to wider review and enable quality improvements to be tracked
3.	Enterprise DA submission: The DA will ensure that quality deliverables are provided to the Buyer by managing reviews by an appropriately selected wider group. Where appropriate these reviewers will have been part of the TWG to understand solution context and provide early solution shaping. Buyer attendance at this final QA step before submission to Buyer enterprise DA will help streamline approval processes
 
This multi layered support model improves deliverable quality with peer reviews by Senior architects using regularly updated checklists. Feedback from these reviews will be used to track quality improvements and drive further improvements delivered by the service. Capturing this feedback centrally in the DA, for assessment and sharing across the service, will drive improvements by educating the team, based on lessons learned across the programme, reducing reoccurrence.
 
On CDS, we used an adaption of this process to ensure feature designs were ready for development:
1.	Architects work with feature stakeholders to agree approach
2.	Acceptance criteria agreed through Design forum (Solution Outline level / medium maturity)
3.	Further design detail discussed with stakeholders in TWG before design reviewed by lead architect and submitted to DA
 
This method allowed CDS architecture to adapt quickly to iterative change, such as scaling out the system to meet EU Exit requirements. Expected volumes were roughly 10x the original system, and significant functional change was needed to meet the complex requirements around Northern Ireland alignment with EU customs policy while creating flexibility in the rest of the system to diverge.
 
Measuring/managing A&D performance
The DA function will be responsible for measuring the performance of the function with feedback from each stage of the design/delivery process. Examples of this on CDS include defects in test being assigned to designs to ensure that the artefacts are corrected appropriately to reflect delivery reality. The DA will collect, analyse and track a set of metrics eg Defect severity, stage defect found and time taken for resubmission to assure the quality of our deliverables.
 
C) Continuous improvement of A&D
We have extensive experience delivering continuous improvement to its Architecture and Design Services, using a repeatable approach which has allowed us to deliver benefits to our clients.
 
Shown in Fig 3, this approach comprises:
•	Agree the dimensions that are key targets and how we will measure them
•	Monitor our delivery of architecture and design services against these metrics
•	Review achievements and lessons learned 
•	Define improvements; update processes and educate team and client in new ways of working
•	Repeat
 
At HMRC SoTF, IBM were responsible for managing the architecture governance process, which acted as a gateway between the design and implementation phases.
 
Using our approach, the joint team were able to improve the process significantly:
•	The 5-3-2 review structure was often significantly delayed. The governance team created a tracker to monitor progress and stage status for every review with stakeholders involved to observe performance
•	The tracker enabled the team to identify blockers preventing the reviews from completing on time eg delayed responses from reviewers; significant amount of feedback to be addressed by project team
•	Findings were reported to senior leadership, resulting in top-down decisions on prioritisation. Jira was implemented to communicate status of reviews and to shift ownership onto project teams to progress reviews and reduce pressure on the governance team. This resulted in reviews completing faster and the quality of documents significantly improving
•	Governance processes were updated to ensure there was increased reviewer engagement at the beginning of the design lifecycle to reduce pressure on the formal review process. Multiple workshops were delivered to ensure this new process was understood and approved by stakeholders
 
This process has enabled IBM to deliver improved quality deliverables, target priority areas of importance and secure quicker and more reliable design acceptance

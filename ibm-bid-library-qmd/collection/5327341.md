The IBM Risk Management process is tightly woven into our service delivery methodology to facilitate the identification and mitigation of any risks that might arise. Our Risk Management approach is consistent across the different areas of our services and is aimed at identifying potential risk factors that might jeopardize the project success. 
This process has five major steps: risk identification, risk analysis, risk response planning, risk tracking and control, risk reaction. Our collaborative approach to risk management provides ongoing and proactive management of risk to facilitate successful delivery. IBM takes a proactive approach to risk management and considers it as an ongoing aspect of project management. A critical element of project success will be the formal identification and evaluation of risks and their impact on the Sims business environment and achievement of project goals.
Risk includes anything that may impede the project from achieving its objectives. Although risk is commonly considered as a hazard, it also involves uncertainty regarding achievements of benefits and results. Risk is defined in the IBM Programme and IBM Project Management Method as both the threat that something negative will happen and the possibility that something positive will not happen. In addition to the defensive action of minimizing the incidence or impact of a hazard, the IBM Risk Management Method also focuses on an offensive strategy to increase the likelihood the project goals, objectives, and benefits will be achieved.
IBM’s risk management reporting assigns a weighted dollar value and schedule impact to each category. Through consistent monitoring and aggressive management, IBM can assess exposure and action the highest impact items first.
Identifying and quantifying project risks and developing and executing appropriate mitigation plans is critical for the project’s success. Anticipating events, either opportunistic or unfavourable, creates the possibility of capturing opportunities or mitigating unfavourable events. Below is an explanation of six most common key project risk categories and mitigation actions.
Based on IBM’s experience with large, complex, fast moving programmes, the following provides a deeper look into mitigation activities by risk area that are relevant to Sims.
- Executive Risk: Lack of executive support often results in the perception that the project is not of high priority. Hence, the organization’s desire to support the effort disintegrates in terms of resource commitment and effective and timely decision making
- Project Risk: A project that is poorly planned will also be poorly executed. Lack of up-front planning and inadequate project controls can lead to a runaway scope, missed activities, poor quality deliverables, and a less than desirable end product
- Functional Risk: Functional risk stems from ambiguous definition of business processes and requirements. Lack of early and ongoing end user involvement often results in a system that does not adequately meet user needs or expectations
- Operational Risk: An organization must be ready, willing, and able to design, build, test, and deploy a new system. Lack of appropriate training and minimal communications often result in a system that is not adopted or used.
- Technical Risk: An unstable technology environment can result in poor system performance and reduced data integrity and control. Ultimately, usage of the system will deteriorate if the environment is not reliable

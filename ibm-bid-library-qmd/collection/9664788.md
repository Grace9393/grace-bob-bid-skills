A)	How we provide support for multiple Buyers/different organisations
 
We provide high-quality services to meet the needs of multiple large Government Buyers, eg HMRC, DWP, Scottish Government, Defra, Home Office, and MOD. 
 
We will build on this by creating a DALAS Operating Model to support and ensure quality across multiple Buyers (see Fig E1.1), which comprises:
•	A DALAS Cross Framework Board to drive consistency, best practice and innovation across all our engagements 
•	Dedicated Buyer Account Teams with deep business and technical understanding of the Buyer’s context, goals and constraints, who understand the outcomes that need to be achieved 
•	An established interlock with relevant experts within our IBM capability teams (eg. Salesforce, MS, Pega) and within our DALAS-specific supplier ecosystem (eg, PA Team, Red Kite), who can access our methods/experience and help define solutions that will deliver the Buyer’s outcomes
•	A collaborative process to co-create requirements/outcomes. We will drive collaboration between IBM and Buyer experts early in the commissioning lifecycle to jointly define clear outcomes, solution strategies and high-level requirements for future work packages
•	Effective governance and reporting to ensure quality and timeliness. We will review our performance with the Buyers monthly, eg average time to respond to requests, capacity fulfilment and value delivered
 
Ensuring quality service 
 
We have rigorous internal governance procedures/tools that independently measure, monitor and report quality scores across all our engagements, including Net Promoter Score.  
 
Every solution we issue will have completed our assurance process, proven over 1,000’s of government projects, where we have an excellent track record of quality delivery on time and budget. This process includes:
•	Technical Design Review, completed by a certified architect who will validate the solution, assumptions, and dependencies 
•	Solution Readiness Review, to validate robustness of the solution, deliverability, and resourcing considerations
•	Deal Review, ensuring we bring innovative commercial models and value
 
During delivery our assurance process is further underpinned by our E2E test assurance activities supported by our Test centre of competence.
 
We will also track, measure, and report SLAs/agreed KPIs monthly on our performance. We've successfully implemented this approach at DWP, handling 100s development requests annually with a 100% success rate in meeting our 10-day response KPI for the past 5 years.
 
 
B)	How we scale across multiple Buyers
 
We have the scale/breadth of capability to serve multiple large Government Buyers and will meet capacity requirements by drawing on resources available in IBM and our wider Partners (see Fig E1.2). We have c14,000 professionals in the UK across IBM/our DALAS ecosystem, 2,000 having pre-requisite security clearance.
 
Working with each Buyer, we will maintain a DALAS-specific rolling 12-month view of demand and match this with our Skills Matrix, which holds IBM and our DALAS ecosystem capability/capacity with a skill maturity assessment per role (eg professional certifications & experience). This will enable us to keep ahead of demand and work with our partners to train/hire in advance to avoid capacity or capability gaps. 
 
As demand firms up, our resourcing specialists will use our proven resource management process and tooling, enabling us to quickly search through resource skillsets, work history and availability to rapidly find resources that best match Buyer needs at scale.
 
We demonstrated this capability when addressing pandemic needs by mobilising 70 people in 4 weeks for NHS Test and Trace and, in parallel, scaling agile teams at the Scottish Government by 100 people in 2 months.
 
 
Utilising our supply chain 
 
We will augment and complement IBMers with resources from our ecosystem of SMEs, each bringing specialist expertise in the relevant low/no code technology. Each SME will contribute quarterly updates to our Skills Matrix, enabling rapid assessment of capabilities to determine the best fit against future Buyer demands.
 
Our approach to apportioning workshare will draw on specific expertise, not putting SMEs in competition, enabling us to foster collaboration and rapidly respond to Buyers. We commit to transparently and equitably sharing a minimum 20% of work with this ecosystem based on the specific low/no code technology needs and will encourage them to develop skills in high-demand areas.  
 
Fig E1.3 shows how we utilise our supply chain to apportion work with ecosystem partners based on expertise and meeting agreed selection criteria. If we need additional capabilities, we will include other trusted suppliers we work with in the Public Sector and/or identify new suppliers through an open procurement process. 
 
When Ireland's Health Service Executive urgently needed to build a national COVID-19 vaccine management system, we leveraged our ecosystem partner, Salesforce, to rapidly provide expert skills in key functional areas. 
 
C)	Quality Decision making to ensure delivery of commercially viable outputs
 
Our Account Manager will work with you to maintain a view of your future requirements and ensure that our capability teams and ecosystem partners have the context to enable us to develop solutions to ensure the delivery of commercially viable outputs. 
 
As soon as a requirement is shared, either for a direct award or a mini-competition, our Account Manager will set up a proposal team to lead the response. This team will comprise client-specific knowledge, relevant capability team expertise, input from our supply chain, and platform-specific input.
 
In many cases, the requirement is likely to be related to an existing strategic platform (eg case management using Pega, here we will bring in experts with skills/experience in the required platform type to provide best practice outputs. 
 
If the Buyer is open to using different low-code platforms, we'll start by assessing their technical feasibility. Drawing on our UK/international experience to identify similar projects and learn from their challenges/successes will help us choose the best platform and reduce delivery time and cost. For instance, we have developed industry-specific patterns/accelerators which we could reuse, reducing delivery timeline/costs. 
 
In all cases, our proposal team will adopt the following approach:
•	Impact the requirement to determine the service type (eg Discovery, Alpha, Beta, DevOps)
•	Submit clarifications if required
•	Develop the proposed solution and roadmap
•	Capture assumptions & dependencies
•	Estimate the effort required
•	Determine the appropriate commercial model
 
Our choice of commercial model will be informed by several factors, including the nature of the service (eg Discovery vs Build), our level of understanding of the requirement, and the degree to which we can jointly bind the scope vs need to build in flexibility to meet changing requirements. 
 
For example, in one contract for HMRC, we were paid fixed price/outcome based on the technical solution meeting a number of transactions/second for customs declarations as the scope and requirement were well known. Whilst on DWP Apply for NINO service, the Discovery was T&M to cater for assessing several options and then switched to Fixed price/Fixed capacity for delivery of the Alpha and Beta phases, successfully delivering an MVP within 8 weeks and ongoing iterative releases to improve the service based on prioritised requirements. 
 
 
D)	Internal governance and maintaining control of all activities across multiple Buyers
 
Delivery Control
Our Integrated Project Planning and Forecasting tool stores delivery quality information for each engagement and is used by our Delivery Managers to report progress and by our Operations teams to track delivery, quality, and performance. Engagements are tracked against 7 dimensions and given a RAG rating per dimension and overall: 
1.	Stakeholders are committed
2.	Client Business Benefits are being realised
3.	Work and schedule are predictable
4.	Scope is realistic and managed
5.	Team is high-performing
6.	Risks are being mitigated
7.	Delivery benefits are being realised
 
We have a dedicated Delivery Partner for each project who conducts monthly reviews to ensure the project is on track for timeline, quality, and budget. We also gather feedback through annual Net Promoter Score surveys and track progress through formal supplier relationship boards.
 
Regular Delivery Excellence reviews are held across all our Public Sector engagements involving assigned independent Quality Partners to provide overall assurance, address any risks/issues and independently measure, monitor, and validate quality metrics across all our engagements.
 
This approach is used to deliver/assure 1000s of our engagements. For example, it is being used today to successfully manage c750 individuals working on 20+ contracts for HMRC, DWP and the Scottish Government.
 
Managing Opportunities
We will meet with each Buyer monthly (along with our SME ecosystem) to understand their demand roadmap and identify areas we can support. For each project, we will discuss potential start dates, T-shirt size efforts and unique characteristics that may require specialist skills. We will also engage with Buyers around planned procurement events. 
 
We will use these to maintain a DALAS-specific rolling 12-month forward view of the demand for functional/technical skills, which we will share with the Buyers and our ecosystem partners. This will enable us to identify any potential capacity constraints or capability gaps and work in advance to mitigate them.
 
We have used this approach successfully with the Scottish Government to forward plan demand and support the scaling of resources from 50 to 300 over an 18-months.
 
Reporting
Our Contract manager will use the combined information from our internal governance controls to provide regular monthly reporting to the CCS Framework manager in accordance with Framework Schedule 4.

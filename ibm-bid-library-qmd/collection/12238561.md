We are proud to have supported the NHSE CSOC since 2020, utilising our List X Facility Security Clearance (FSC) Hursley certified site (the ‘SOC’). Hence, we understand the criticality of your service to the NHS and the UK at large, and we recognise that timely, flexible and scalable ‘Surge Support’ is a key capability enabler for the NHSE CSOC. Over our last five years with you, we have augmented your team to provide Tier 1 24/7 protective monitoring and fulfilled 100% of all surge requests during the life of the contract.
 
The SOC has been supporting our public sector and critical national infrastructure clients for the last 14 years, success demonstrated by our confirmed expansion of List-X sites in Leicester and Farnborough (in 2025). This experience positions us as a reliable partner for NHSE, capable of meeting your Surge Support needs. Below, we outline how we satisfy all 'must' and ‘should’ supplier Appendix B3 requirements relevant to this question (with references in square brackets).

1. Confirmation the additional support required can be requested

IBM confirms that we can provide 120 surge call-off hours (plus an additional 30 hours for Senior Analysts) during each 12-month period at short notice (e.g., 24 hours [32]). This Surge Support will deliver additional capacity using our existing SOC resources, augmenting the standard service offering. Our approach for doing so is provided below
 
2. Approach to putting additional Surge Support in place at short notice

Our approach to delivering additional Surge Support at short notice is based on proven processes that we have developed with you; our experience providing Surge Support to you and other public sector clients; and our SOC’s demonstrable scale compared to other List X SOCs in the UK.
 
To provide the scale and rapid response required for any Surge Support request, we will leverage our existing SOC services, which supports multiple clients with Critical National Infrastructure (CNI). The SOC currently operates as a shared service model consisting of a minimum of 16 operational full-time analysts (i.e., 4 shift teams with 4 people per shift). This does not include our staff who are rostered off, on stand-by, on personal/medical leave, attending training etc. This additional capacity enables us to accommodate rapid short term Surge Support to assist you in any event, incident, or crisis.

To ensure the SOC is always available to provide Surge Support, we operate a detailed demand forecasting process taking a short-term and long-term view through monthly, weekly and daily forecasting exercises. Our dedicated Service Manager, Laura Martin, is responsible for providing the necessary inputs into the SOC, such as the capacity estimate required to deliver the contractual obligations of the CSOC contract. We then add on a percentage uplift to this capacity to cater for short-term surge requirements. This additional capacity is reserved for NHSE, ensuring we maintain a level of capacity over and above the expected industry baselines.

Requests for Surge Support are often time critical. Laura (augmented by the duty Service Manager out of hours to provide you 24/7 availability of this role) receives/actions all NHSE requests. For significant escalations, our CSOC Service Manager will contact the IBM SOC Manager and IBM Public Sector Security Centre Manager, liaising directly with NHSE’s Security Manager throughout.
 
Team awareness and communication of the current activity tempo and threat environment is key to effective capacity management and surge demands. This is achieved through:
• Daily NHSE standups: Our dedicated IBM Service Manager ensures that our SOC team stays updated on the NHSE CSOC team's current priorities, activities, and threat environment, providing situational awareness and forewarning as required.
• Weekly (Monday) operational calls: These meetings involve NHSE Service Managers, SOC Leadership, our IBM Service Manager, and our Focal analyst to discuss the previous week's performance, challenges, priorities, and incident resolution paths. They also cover upcoming challenges, including resource coverage, thereby allowing further planning of required support
• IBM Focal analyst meetings with SOC leads, twice per week: Working with NHSE Area leads, focusing on operational challenges, service improvements, and technical alignment.
• Monthly service reviews: Both the IBM Service Manager and Focal analyst attend these meetings, with NHSE CSOC leadership, therebY allowing further support analysis.
 
Our established communication and resource allocation process is built to ensure detailed tracking and management of the day to day and Surge related activity provided by the SOC, with complete transparency to NHSE, detailing any pain points, reviewing and extending the support if needed, and continually refining processes to support the analyst team.

From receiving your request for Surge Support, we assess our SOC resource coverage for the requested time-period and determine the optimum resource alignment to fulfil the Surge Support demand. This includes enacting the following options:
• Future shift resources: We will backfill future shifts to cover surge requests
• Daytime engineers: Temporary re-assignment of experienced and qualified analyst resources
• Focal analyst daytime coverage and additional resources available beyond regular 9-5 hours.
 
Our proven approach and demonstrable experience of working with you to provide Surge Support gives us the confidence to commit to providing 150 total surge call-off hours during each 12- month period at short notice
 
3. Confirmation our resource profile can increase capacity incrementally

• IBM can confirm that we can provide an increase of 0.25 FTE within 28 days from the date of the written request received from you.
• IBM can also confirm that we can provide an increase of 0.5 FTE within 56 days from the date of the written request received from you.
• IBM confirms that we will work collaboratively with you on requests over 0.5 FTE.

4. Approach to increasing the capacity of the service incrementally

IBM’s UK SOC capability is managed centrally through our Cybersecurity Services Business Unit. With visibility over our wider client and account demands, our Executive and Delivery Management teams have the organisational awareness and authority to appropriately right-size and implement future personnel planning to meet our, and our clients’, workforce needs. This has seen IBM grow our List X/FSC capability from 1 site in Hursley, to 3 sites total by 2025, without stretching current client resources or over-investing in excess capacity (thereby avoiding increases to service cost).

To incrementally provide additional capacity to the service throughout the contract, we will leverage our shared service model, described above in section 2. This model enables us to support you in a variety of incremental capacity increases including 0.25, 0.5 and over 0.5 FTE [35] within the timeframes set out, through altering shift patterns to match demand rather than hiring new resources for every request. This approach has been tried, tested and proven over the last 5 years of our engagement with you, and over the last decade with other clients such as National Grid and NHS BSA (ESR), where we have incrementally increased demand as required.

When we receive a capacity increase request from NHSE, our SOC shift lead initiates a resource reallocation process. This standard process will engage our central Cybersecurity Services Business Unit and SOC lead (Sam Butler) to fulfil the resource request. Sam will review the resource allocation and demand forecasting models and use this analysis to determine the best approach to fulfilling the demand. If the demand can be absorbed into the existing 20+ full time operational analysts in the SOC, then we will alter the shift patterns to match demand. This approach is standard across our clients within the shared service and enables us to be flexible and provide rapid increase in demand as required. If required, the number of analysts in a shift can also be increased, leveraging our additional capacity of SOC resources (including our engineers and senior analysts who typically engage in the triage/response stages for other clients).
 
If additional resource is required outside of our current pool of SOC resources, we will:
• Leverage strategic resource pools located in Leicester and Farnborough
• Leverage skilled resources from the wider IBM UK Cybersecurity teams
• Draw upon our wider SOC-experienced capability performing in other roles such as SIEM Engineering, Threat Hunting, security consultancy, etc.,
• Go to market to recruit highly skilled individuals to join our team which takes 4-16 weeks (due to SC requirements). We will achieve this through utilising our strategic partnerships, such as Grayce, and continuous recruitment processes to fulfil demand

Testing and release management are critical to the successful delivery of projects and the increasing move to automation of these activities and adoption of DevOps practices such as small batch sizes allows for much more control and predictability around releasing to production than with traditional manual approaches.
 
For example, for the NHS test and trace solution we needed to be able to quickly develop and deliver new functionality to respond to the ever-changing nature of the pandemic, whilst providing confidence in the releases of new functionality being successful. Our continuous delivery pipeline provided the test automation that the delivery met the specification of the design. 
At Lloyds Bank, IBM runs a DevOps and test driven development environment, focusing on Business Intelligence service delivery and improving operational efficiency. The approach is Continuous Integration, advocated through Minimal Viable Products defined in behaviour-driven development (BDD) statements, with high-levels of automated testing coverage. Together with business owners, Cucumber is used to define BDD outcomes, which are referenced throughout the pipeline using workflows that manage static and unit testing, deployment, and multi-level automated regression tests for UI, integration and performance. Benefits include accelerated development and quality improvements. The established confidence in deployment, with releases linked to the original BDDs, has halved manual testing effort required.
We anticipate employing similar approaches to those we delivered at NHS and Lloyds Bank with DSA, building on the DevOps approaches that you already use. Some legacy systems may not make it possible, but wherever we can, all tests will be defined up front, automated and integrated into DevOps release management pipelines to drive automation, consistency, flexibility and above all confidence into releasing to live.

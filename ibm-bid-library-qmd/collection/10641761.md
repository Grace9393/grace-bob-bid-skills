Overall approach to programme and delivery management
To ensure support of your APHIDS service continues uninterrupted, IBM is intending to partner with Naimuri, subject to contract; APHIDS will be maintained by people you already know and trust from Naimuri, backed up by IBM’s experience of supporting and hardening systems across UK government. In terms of our approach to programme and delivery management, this means that we start from a baseline of already knowing your stakeholders and being able to develop those relationships rather than starting afresh.
To ensure that we have answered your question fully, we have addressed each of your guidance bullet points in the sections that follow. As our response progresses, we specifically call out examples of IBM and Naimuri’s collective experience of delivering stakeholder engagement. We also confirm both of the notes in the question and highlight that we will:
- Work collaboratively as part of a multi-supplier environment delivering the end-to-end APHIDS Support Services.
- Act in good faith towards each other, adopt and maintain a genuine non-defensive stance in our dealings with each other and commit to making our relationships with each Party mutually successful. 
Organising business priorities and outcomes into a programme of activities and deliverables / Establishing and delivering operational service and support / Managing a successful delivery
Naimuri and IBM propose running your service much like an agile software delivery project. We believe that this way of working will naturally enable organisation of priorities into programmes of activities and deliverables.
Much like in an agile system delivery, we will run a refinement session on a regular basis with all stakeholders to review a backlog of incidents and issues requiring resolution, which we will create if it doesn’t exist already. Ordinarily this refinement session would be weekly or bi-weekly; we will determine a suitable cadence once we are embedded as your partner and have a clearer view of the cycle of incident fixes, service requests, and changes.
Each item on the backlog would include, as a minimum; a description, a priority, an assessment of systems and stakeholders being impacted, the deliverables required on delivery of the fix, and, in the case of changes, a quantified assessment of benefits (for example, the ability to process a pass application 50% faster) and documented business outcomes. Ideally, this backlog would be hosted on collaboration tooling such that all stakeholders can access it, and view or add items at any time. If it is not possible to provide backlog access to a stakeholder, IBM and Naimuri will act as a conduit and update the backlog on a stakeholder’s behalf.
The regular refinement sessions will be chaired by your Product Owners (PO) with our support. The output of each session will be a freshly prioritised backlog, reflecting a collective view of which items need to be addressed as soon as possible, and which are of lesser importance. The prioritised backlog is thus an organisation of business priorities, and will subsequently inform a project roadmap, which will envisage being our programme of activities. The project roadmap will show when each item on the backlog will be addressed, likely in two-week chunks. Items on the plan can be cross-referenced to the backlog which will list which deliverables will be produced as completion criteria for each item.
The APHIDS operational service and support will be established quickly yet thoroughly for a system of importance.  We have minimised the risk of transition with our plan to work with Naimuri and with the integration into our wider Secure Shared Delivery (SSD) capability which provides operational services and support for many Home Office applications. We will update existing runbooks and operational procedures and continually manage these for accuracy and relevance.   
Combined with this is our approach to managing successful delivery. We have strong ITIL based Service Management processes, aligned to Home Office ways of working that will guide our teams along with strong Project Management and Governance skills that our Service Manager lead will demonstrate. We have successfully used the 7 keys reporting method on other projects which provides you with an easy to digest status of the overall success of our delivery. We will deliver this report to you on a monthly basis which includes the following keys:
- Work and Schedule are predictable.
- Business benefits are being realised.
- Stakeholders and committed. 
- Scope is realistic and managed.
- Team is high performing.
- Risk are being mitigated.
- Delivery Organisation benefits are being realised.
 
 
Delivering software development and infrastructure changes, and assuring quality
Building on our overall approach described above, IBM and Naimuri will partner to bring the best of both organisations and meet the APHIDS Service Requirements, which will be achieved through the continued provision of Naimuri’s Site Reliability Engineering (SRE) Team, complimented with IBM Service Management. Naimuri’s SREs will continue to work closely with the existing APHIDS project delivery team, ensuring that the SRE team has a detailed knowledge and a deep understanding of the environments, tooling and working practices of APHIDS. We will bring thought leadership from our software development practices, refined from engagements with clients worldwide, to continually upskill the Support team and civil servants working on APHIDS.
Our software development process is designed to deliver high-quality, scalable, and secure solutions. Our team of experienced developers, testers, and service managers work collaboratively to design, develop, test, and deploy software in an iterative way. We will employ continuous automated approaches to software delivery, harnessing our in-depth knowledge of Continuous Integration and Continuous Delivery tools such as Drone, GitLab and Jenkins. Our approach to infrastructure changes is focussed around minimising disruption and ensuring business continuity. We use automated pipelines to build, quality assure, report, and deploy software (including infrastructure) into a range of environments from on-premises to Kubernetes and the cloud. Using Infrastructure-as-Code allows us to deliver changes to the infrastructure in the same way we do for software. Proactive monitoring allows us to be ahead of infrastructure incidents, allowing changes to be planned where possible rather than reactive. Software and infrastructure changes will be planned ahead of time where possible, according to the process outlined above; the project roadmap will communicate when changes will be implemented. The support service will follow the same best practice DevSecOps approach that underpin all of Naimuri and IBM’s work, ensuring all development and support is secure by default, following industry best practice for coding, testing and deployment.
Our service will have quality built in. As fixes and changes are documented, we will collectively agree a ‘definition of done’; in other words, what criteria do we have to meet to formally accept a change. This could include automated testing, peer review and documentation updates, ensuring high quality test coverage of all changes is undertaken. Quality assurance benefits from automated reporting in an accessible manner and we will provide test reports with all deliveries, along with detailed release information for any changes made to the system.
Delivering technical and operational rollout across multiple organisations
Our intention is that the approach outlined above will bring representatives from all impacted stakeholders together from the very start of the change and incident resolution cycle. Well ahead of implementation and on a frequent basis, the project roadmap will be published which shows when items are planned to be addressed and which development and support teams will be involved. As also described above, this roadmap is created from a backlog which is prioritised collectively. Working closely with the project team will enable the support service to have a clear understanding of the project roadmap. This means support considerations and required changes are considered at the earliest point of the development lifecycle, so that releases across the teams minimise disruption to users.

AWS testing strategy
We will bring an automation-first strategy for AWS testing. It will cover the various types of testing such as functional, performance, security, and compliance as well as testing for scalability and reliability. We will run our automated tests regularly, including unit tests, integration tests and acceptance tests, to ensure that changes we make to code and infrastructure do not introduce bugs or issues. Manual testing should also be performed to validate the functionality and performance of critical workloads when necessary. We will use our experience as Well Architected Framework reviewers to help test the overall solution as well as the software / infrastructure. We will bring thought leadership from our IBM Quality Assurance and Testing (QAT) practice who work closely with AWS on a regular basis to embrace new techniques and approaches. As described above, our testing is also based on ways of working regardless of deployment architecture, this includes high quality test case coverage of new changes and thorough documentation, namely test reports and release notes.
Future support of APHIDS
We have worked on many critical systems across different Government stakeholder groups and successfully worked with you to manage risk; working to the Home Office best practice, as recognised in the delivery review boards we attend for projects we deliver with you.
Our APHIDS strategy will identify potential risks, assess their likelihood and impact, and importantly, implement mitigation strategies to minimise where possible. It will also include threat modelling, risk assessment, risk mitigation, compliance management, continuous monitoring, incident response, training and awareness, and continuous improvement.
Collaboration with the government agencies, industry partners, and other stakeholders is crucial to share information, best practices, and threat intelligence as both the solution evolves going forward and the threat landscape changes.
Working with Home Office Portfolio and Programme management
We bring great experience of building joint organisations, and developing strategy, delivery plans and management processes with the Home Office. As your SAS Delivery Partner, we established the new operating model for the Home Office and helped you move to this model across the various Portfolios and Programmes. We will replicate our learnings from this to help APHIDS. Our approach will include:

- Co-create the strategy in a clear and comprehensive way that aligns with the mission and vision.
- Create a detailed delivery plan and management processes that outlines the steps to and methods to achieve the goal.
- Embrace common tooling and communication approaches, ensuring things are open and transparent.
- Regular monitoring and evaluation to allow ongoing adjustments.

We are conscious that a multitude of stakeholders input to APHIDS. Considering this, early in our engagement we will carry out interviews with all stakeholders to establish points-of-contact, agree ways of working (see above) and communication preferences. We will document these in a stakeholder engagement plan and a communications plan and publish these so that they are available for anyone to consume. Our team will know many of the stakeholders and we encourage a ‘leave badges at the door’ culture. We have great success when our teams are all aligned to a common goal and can bring project charters which help with this and overall joint working effectiveness.
We will hold regular retrospective sessions to review our joint working. If joint working is not deemed to be as efficient as possible, we will collectively agree on improvement actions and document them in the stakeholder engagement plan and communications plan.
Evidence: rapidly course correcting to ensure involvement from all stakeholders in a law enforcement project
While working with a law enforcement client, our regular retrospective sessions highlighted that regional stakeholder groups felt less represented than their London counterparts. During the retrospective, which included representation from all stakeholder groups, we collectively agreed that running subsequent demonstration and retrospective sessions on a road-show basis, touring the regional units would be a great step to increasing inclusivity. The plan was put into action at the next sprint and resulted in an increase in interaction from regional units for the remainder of the programme. The regional units told us, through our mechanisms of continuous communication that they felt more represented, consulted and involved.

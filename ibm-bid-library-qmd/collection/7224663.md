The roll-out approach and its execution are critical to success of the project, and it is key that we ensure this is treated as a critical requirement in all the phases during the project and not included as an afterthought during the later phases of the project.   We have proposed to deploy the solution in incremental stages to allow adaption of Maximo WMS and also understand what changes or enhancements are most critical for WPD.
Below we describe our approach that incorporates thinking on roll-out and support for that into all phases of the project, 
- Discovery
- Proof of Concept (PoC)
- Design for Minimum Viable product (MVP)
- Build & Test (MVP)
- Training & cutover (MVP)
- Enhancements post MVP
Discovery phase
During the discovery phase, IBM will work with WPD stakeholders to understand the field force structure in terms of their geography, roles, skills, overlaps etc. and will draw out the opportunities and constraints identifying the grouping for roll-out.
 At a leading Electricity transmission business, we were able to group the workforce into self-contained geographical areas for the purpose of roll-out and we believe we can work with WPD to achieve a similar set of grouping to support a staged rollout.
During this phase, we will work with WPD to agree: 
- Deployment Strategy – including areas such as approach to cutover of users, cutover principles, timing, pace, parallel run, work in progress, engagement and technical enablers
- User group definition – Target audience, skills groupings, work types, Risk considerations
- Define core functionality to be trialled (cut down functionality to gain feedback from the end users).  This will need to align with roll out strategy, so reporting is accurate
- Deployment plan including phasing over ‘X’ Period of time
- Identification of super users to support in deployment planning and delivery
- Business engagement approach
The field force structures, timing of deployment around key events e.g. winter storm periods, Customer impacts, work types, use cases and past experience of rolling out change to user groups, will all influence the approach.  
Our approach typically recommends that a user can only be in one system at a time, so that users do not face complexity of using two solutions.   It also recommends that the smallest / least complex groups are included in the initial releases, this will enable the project to mitigate any issues or challenges to smooth the deployment processes.  Lessons learnt from these initial deployment groups will then be fed in to iterate and evolve the approach, with the more complex user groups planned later in the deployment plan. This ensures that business risk is mitigated through the resolution of any initial issues and concerns and inputs from the business. This approach has worked well in several large utility deployments with complex user group and regional diversity.
In a large Gas utility, we deployed Maximo technology into production in one cutover, the business deployment was then phased in with maintenance, repair job types and associated users going first, followed by Metering and emergency job types whose users had the experience and ability to complete multiple order types.  This approach was defined for two reasons: 1. Reducing impact to customer facing work by settling the solution in prior to introducing those and 2. Identifying user groupings that were on more restricted work types, prior to deploying to users who required a higher diversity of work types and hence wider system exposure
Proof of Concept
During this phase, we will perform the base configuration of Maximo and perform system testing. We will also load configuration data for the target workgroups in scope for the PoC and test this configuration. We will create ‘dummy’ interfaces where we can take data extracts from your existing systems and push the information into Maximo through its integration framework simulating the future interface. Our functional consultants will run a familiarisation session for users on the project and access to the system will be provided to your project team so they can use the system to understand the system processes and capabilities.  This information will be fed into the design phase which will create the MVP for the regional roll out that has been outlined in the discovery phase.

Design phase
Out of the PoC phase, the project team (WPD and IBM) will collaborate to define the MVP solution.  At this point in the project, we suggest a decision point of how to move forward.  This will also allow both parties to realign scope (priorities) and resourcing if needed.  IBM will confirm the fix price still stands or if a new price will need to be submitted based on the change in scope or the project will proceed as is. At this point, we do not feel new pricing will need to be submitted based on the scope that has been outlined in this proposal. The WMS solution must consider five (5) key areas to enable a staged roll-out across WPD. 
- Updates to legacy solution
- Integration
- New application
- Support 
- Business process and data
IBM will work with WPD and stakeholders supporting existing solution to design an optimal solution, that will support a staged/iterative roll-out, while also not creating enduring constraints into the design for the future. Below are a few considerations for each of the technical areas:
- Updates to legacy solution: To enable an iterative roll-out the overall design should enable parallel running of the legacy and the new solution for a period of time. With work/ jobs able to be routed to one system or another and to one user or another.  This may potentially need updates to the legacy solutions to stop the legacy applications from scheduling and dispatching work that has been rolled out into the new solution. We have seen this achieved through manual intervention / updates to the legacy solutions or introducing changes into them to support switching off groups when they have been rolled out into the new solution. IBM during the design phase will work with WPD to agree an approach and design to support this.  
- Integration: During a iterative roll-out, there will be a period of parallel run that needs the integration to feed both the legacy and new solutions. The design phase will review the options to achieve this and incorporate this into technology and the process design.  If a middleware is used, then this would be the ideal area to look in order to achieve parallel running.  
- New application: The design for the new application must consider approaches to achieve an iterative roll-out; we have achieved this by executing selective data migration (migration by groups and roll-out) or by having ability in the application to selectively schedule and dispatch work in groups. The design of the legacy applications, the integration and the groupings will also influence this approach. During the design phase, IBM will analyse the options for WPD and incorporate the agreed approach into the WPD design.
- Support: The design needs to consider how the solution will be packaged up and deployed to users and how that will be created, released, deployed and managed.  Engagement of WPD support teams regarding any changes identified in legacy solutions or integrations to impact assess and gain agreement through routes such as your business and / or technical design authority to those changes. Lastly, the impact and planning of a longer period of transition for your BAU support teams today driven by the deployment pace and approach.
- Business process: IBM will work with you to review existing business processes and data implications of the changes (e.g. how to handle work in progress, criteria for deployment readiness of a user and data statuses through the lifecycle of work types. The process review should consider Maximo WMS functionality and the process its drives from out of the box features.  This will ensure the processes and systems are aligned and minimal customisation is needed to deploy the system. 
Build and Test
The design that has incorporated a phased roll-out will be included into our build and test phases. IBM will work closely with the WPD legacy and Integration teams as necessary during this phase to ensure functionality and consistency are achieved. This phase will lead to the new applications being ready for cutover.  Any interim solution requirements to handle user and work type migration to the new application will be developed and tested in this phase as well as the core WMS application suite.
Training and Cutover
Based on our experience, IBM have found that it is important to align the training and cutover activities in line with the roll-out plan. Our response to training is covered in question 29 and will be started prior to user acceptance testing so users are educated on the solution prior to completed test cases. 
Any work with the user in the current system in our experience is better to complete in the existing solution, especially with regulatory reporting requirements on some job types. This creates a new clean system for regulatory reporting and also understand what enhancements are needed moving forward.  
Enhancements post the MVP
Keeping with the phased rollout approach, IBM has included an enhancement phase that allows for new features and functions to be included in the enduring solution plus to rollout to new regions of WPD.  Again, at this stage scope will be baselined and a decision point will be considered if resources need to be adjusted, which may lead to another reprice.  On other projects, this is usually the time period the client finalises their priorities based on the initial uptake of the system and makes a decision to continue on the journey or move in another direction which will ultimately change scope. 
Support
Post go live support for the solution needs to consider not only the normal warranty period but how that support will transition into BAU support post go-live through what could be an extended and phased period of roll-out.  IBM will provide resources for level 2 support for the Maximo WMS solution.  It is assumed that IBM will use the existing helpdesk processes and systems to manage WMS tickets.  Any large enhancements will be considered change requests, but existing defects, bugs with the core product or issues with the deployed solution will be addressed through support. Full details of the support can be found in question 27.

Our model for incident management and problem resolution is ITIL-aligned and leverages next-gen AMS capabilities (automation, AI, real-time monitoring, proactive problem management and continuous improvement) to prevent incidents, enhance service quality, improve user experience, and reduce costs. For incident management, our priority is to restore service ASAP. Once service is restored, major and recurring incidents are moved to problem management for root cause analysis and to apply a permanent fix.
 
We’ll reduce risk for UKHSA by building on our proven delivery model and trusted relationship we have with you. We plan to optimise your service further and establish a solid foundation enabling UKHSA to pivot quickly towards new challenges as they emerge.
 
We’ll meet all service requirements without additional support from UKHSA by providing a highly skilled UK-based L2 and L3 team knowledgeable in your systems. We'll develop a prioritised roadmap for UKHSA in the first three months, including next-gen AMS initiatives eg self-service, automation, AI, and chatbots, balancing speed and value. Our roadmap exceeds your requirements and will leverage existing UKHSA investments, eg ServiceNow, target appropriate use cases. See Figure TC1.1 for our incident and problem handling future vision.
 
 
Incident handling and problem management for NHS Test & Trace (T&T):
 
Situation: 
In 2020, IBM partnered with T&T to support its Trace Services platform. 
 
Tasks: 
We provide 24x7, L2, and L3 support. We deliver incident response and resolution, root cause analysis, platform maintenance, patching, configuration changes, change and problem management, system monitoring, tuning and service performance reporting across COTS and custom applications, escalating to product vendors as required.
 
Actions: 
We established a clear process for handling incidents and problems. We’re unique in our proven flexibility and ability to adapt/scale to UKHSA’s needs. We tailored the service to align with UKHSA working methods and industry best practices. We follow a mix of ITIL v3 and v4 processes. Our approach is designed to adapt to the constantly evolving T&T environment. 
 
We continuously review incidents in our queue and hold daily calls with UKHSA to agree on priorities. Our flexibility means we work on what's important for UKHSA. We embrace a DevOps culture and prioritise resolving tickets at L2, minimising L3 handoffs. We liaise directly with L3 for more complex issues, maintaining control and ensuring quicker resolution. L2 controls production environment access for a clear separation of duties, reduced risk, and improved security posture. 
 
We maintain a knowledge base in Confluence, regularly updating documentation after incident resolution for faster future resolution. 
 
Results: 
•	Robust processes adapted to UKHSA needs
•	Agile and flexible ways of working; prioritising T&T's needs whilst maintaining excellent SLA performance, and •	A detailed knowledge base for common incidents and problems, reducing fix times and increasing transparency.
Below is an example of what is achievable with next-gen AMS capabilities.
 
Situation: 
A large Bank needed to modernise its legacy application estate to lower its service cost. 
 
Tasks: 
IBM delivered next-gen AMS, modernised production operations and migrated applications to cloud. AMS scope includes incident, knowledge, change and certificate management, requests, deployment services, DR testing, daily health checks, monitoring and alerting. 
 
Actions: 
We modernised service delivery, applying three principles: 
 
1.	Automation-first mindset
2.	Shift-left, and 
3.	Engineering for resiliency. 
We introduced advanced AMS capabilities such as automated monitoring, automated ticket triage using ServiceNow, test automation, self-healing and automatic ticket resolution, runbook automation, and cognitive assistance through an AI chatbot for self-help support. Additionally, we’ve created a common AD/AMS backlog to streamline prioritisation and facilitate rapid/agile delivery.
 
Results:
•	Zero P1s in the last 3.5 years 
•	35% incident/issue avoidance
•	25% faster patch deployment
•	40% faster incident resolution using SRE principles, and
•	6,000+ hours saved annually.

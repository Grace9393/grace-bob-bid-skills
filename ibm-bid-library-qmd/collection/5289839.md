IBM are providing a co-located Agile design and delivery service to support the Scottish Government’s flagship Social Security programme which has the core aims of implementing a devolved welfare benefit system and establishing a new agency called Social Security Scotland to administer the new benefits.
 
At the heart of this programme is a major digital transformational change in how these services are delivered, designed with the people of Scotland in mind, based on evidence of their needs and to a core principle of treating everyone with dignity, fairness and respect. This required a fundamentally different approach to defining the organisation, processes and technology which was centred on the needs of the citizens but also to do so at pace and with flexibility to support the growth of benefits in the future. 
 
The Scottish Government engaged IBM as key delivery partner, bringing industry experience in delivering social security benefit platforms through enterprise user-centred design, Agile delivery methods and “cloud first” DevOps driven development process. Through this highly-collaborative partnership, Scottish Government have established the new agency and successfully launched the first benefits on the platform, paying out over £190 million to nearly 100,000 households, establishing a modern, secure, flexible and scalable platform for the future and most importantly making a material difference to people’s lives.
 
As part of the programme, the Scottish Government set out an ambitious schedule to launch the new agency with the first wave of benefits for low income families to go live from late 2018 through to early 2020. To deliver at this pace, the programme was delivered iteratively, with IBM establishing the overall ways of working using our ‘Agile at Scale’ experience, focussing on how the programme could enable maximum flexibility to be able to engage with citizens, respond to research, deliver incrementally and run multiple squads in parallel.
 
This approach has been highly successful and enabled early testing with users and citizens, learning from experience and the opportunity to tune the technology and processes before fully rolling-out new services. It has promoted continuous improvement, providing clear visibility and transparency of how the digital services were evolving, helping collaboratively working across organisational development, policy definition, technical delivery and communications. It also allowed the joint IBM and client teams to follow the Discovery, Alpha, Beta delivery phases ensuring the Digital First Service Standards could be demonstrated at each stage and built confidence that services fulfilled the needs of Scottish citizens.
 
To deliver a truly user centred design approach, the programme required engagement with large numbers of users and stakeholder groups across all stages of the delivery. IBM worked closely with the Scottish Government teams to establish the ongoing detailed research process which connected with over 1350 people, including individual citizens, agency advisors and charities. This was delivered across 40 locations, hosting more than 480 research sessions including one-on-one interviews, focus groups, group workshops, usability testing sessions and ethnographic field studies. Research was performed sensitively, working with vulnerable groups including parents that have suffered a stillborn or neonatal death, survivors of domestic abuse, Black, Asian and Minority Ethnic Groups, young parents and users with disabilities. This identified core needs of diverse groups and allowed concepts to be tested with different communities across all areas of Scotland. 
 
Once this data was collected, the joint teams used IBM’s Design Thinking Framework as the basis for aligning around the different user needs, consolidating detailed user stories and creating clear goals for each development sprint to enable the service to be developed iteratively. Feedback was continuous, with every iteration validated with users and citizens to ensure we were on the right track.
 
The ability to have a robust, flexible set of platforms which can be easily configured and scaled, was crucial to the rapid delivery of digital social security services and to enable the exceptional user experience required. Using the Social Security Digital and Technology Strategy of “Reuse, before Buy, before Build” IBM worked closely with the Scottish Government Chief Digital Officer (CDO) to develop the overall architecture based on three key domains:
- The proportionate and appropriate reuse of existing services in UK government
- Deploying a set of flexible package solutions to provide the core services, and 
- Building a secure hosting platform using highly automated processes.
 
The CDO and IBM technical teams then built upon this architectural direction to deliver an end to end benefits platform which was built on the AWS public cloud using DevOps driven approaches. The teams followed a Secure by Design set of principles to ensure that security was baked into every element of the technical delivery and allowed the deployment of the solutions into AWS public cloud, a first for any digital application in Scottish Government. 
 
Together, IBM and the Scottish Government have delivered a solution which: 
- Can scale to meet the digital demands of low-income benefits and other future Scottish public services
- Deals with large scale disaster events to quickly recover through automation, and 
- Provides the foundations to iteratively build on in the future as the application and business process landscape changes.

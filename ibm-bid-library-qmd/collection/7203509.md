The “discovery” phase is a core element of IBM’s delivery methodology and previous engagements at the likes of the large UK gas transmission company, Southern Water and Irish Water.  
Getting this right will set the project up for success by, not only laying the foundations for successful delivery but also bringing certainty and clarity for the future phases of the project.  Discovery will provide the opportunity for our joint WPD-IBM to “form, norm and storm”.  Building this one-team ethos will be critical to ensuring that this Work Management programme is an exemplar programme for both organisations.  
Our design principle is “keep it simple, keep it standard”.  This means that we will use the industry-standard business processes in Maximo, rather than re-create your legacy business processes.  We will leverage IBM’s depth of experience in Utilities (electricity, gas and water) processes, business capabilities and target architectures to accelerate this engagement.  In developing this proposal, we have built example “to-be” process flows mapped to common industry personas that bring to life the value of Maximo for WPD.

As detailed above, IBM used this Discovery methodology during the implementation of Maximo at Southern Water.  We supported this utility to design a new set of streamlined business processes, aligned to Maximo, based on the IBM UK Water Process Model.  These processes were used to deliver business benefits, help define the new target operating model, drive business change and establish new, better ways of working.  
The Discovery phase at Southern Water lasted for 4 months, during which we jointly worked to achieve the following objectives
- Refine functional requirements and develop user stories
- Technical design for Maximo deployment
- Planning and Mobilisation 
- Establish governance and common working
 
Refine functional requirements and develop user stories
 
We worked with representatives from across Southern Water’s business and IT teams to map out detailed “to-be” requirements for a new Maximo based Work & Asset Management platform.  These required formed the prioritised backlog for the implementation phase.  
 
We held a series of process workshops enabled by design thinking to map out business needs, pain points, challenges and opportunities.  The output of these “brown paper” workshops were contrasted with out-of-the-box Maximo demos and process documentation.  This helped the business users to build confidence in the Maximo tool.  
 
Identifying user personas, their roles and how they would interact with the application was key to the design and configuration of the application. We leveraged pre-defined persona (an IBM accelerator) and mapped them against the Southern Water user base. 
 
Technical design for Maximo deployment
 
During the discovery phase we worked with the business and IT user groups to document and so understand the infrastructure and application landscape.  This enabled IBM to design the Conceptual Solution Architecture, Logical Technology Model (LTM)and Physical Technology Model (PTM) for the solution. 
Planning and Mobilisation
During the discovery phase, we drove planning and mobilisation in two stages, 
- planning and mobilisation for the discovery phase itself – this will be done upfront at the start of the engagement, and we have also done this during the contracting phase in order to accelerate the project where required
- planning and mobilisation for subsequent phases – this was one of the key outcomes from the Discovery phase and was jointly developed with Northumbrian, with input from stakeholders across all areas that includes: 
- IT (downstream applications, Integration, Infrastructure, Networks, Security)
- Business 
- Any other third party
 
Establish governance and common working: 
Governance and a setting up the right working culture is critical to programme delivery. Establishing this upfront and setting out required training and induction for any resources joining the project and adopting best practices will set this project for success and we aim to do this during the discovery phase.

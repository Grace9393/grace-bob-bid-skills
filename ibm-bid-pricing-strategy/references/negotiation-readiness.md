# Pricing Negotiation Readiness

Use this reference when the pricing strategy is likely to be negotiated, challenged, discounted, compared against a cheaper competitor, or converted into final commercial terms.

This is not a general negotiation playbook. Use only the parts that directly affect price, margin, scope, risk, terms, and value defence.

## Core Rule

Do not enter pricing negotiation with only a proposed price.

Define:

- Opening position
- Target position
- Walkaway point
- BATNA
- WATNA
- ZOPA
- Conditional concessions
- Tradeables
- Price objection responses
- Escalation thresholds

## BATNA And WATNA

BATNA is IBM's best alternative if the deal is not agreed. WATNA is the downside scenario if negotiation fails or if IBM accepts a poor deal.

Use BATNA/WATNA to set pricing discipline:

- Identify IBM's best fallback, such as pursuing higher-value work, delaying start, reducing scope, or reallocating scarce delivery capacity.
- Identify IBM's worst-case exposure, such as revenue loss, reputational impact, idle team cost, delivery disruption, or strategic account impact.
- Estimate the client's BATNA, such as selecting a lower-cost competitor, delaying procurement, reducing scope, or doing nothing.
- Estimate the client's WATNA, such as higher TCO, delayed transformation, integration failure, quality risk, operational disruption, or inability to realise outcomes.

Guardrail:

- BATNA is a safeguard, not the target. Aim for a deal better than BATNA.
- Do not reveal detailed BATNA information. Signal confidence through alternatives and value, not through explicit threats.
- If the deal falls below IBM's profitability, risk, or strategic threshold, recommend walking away or restructuring.

## ZOPA And Negotiating Range

ZOPA is the range where IBM's acceptable terms and the client's acceptable terms overlap.

Define:

| Item | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Opening position | IBM's initial price and terms, anchored to value |
| Target position | IBM's preferred agreed price and terms |
| Reservation / walkaway | Minimum acceptable economics and risk position |
| Client reservation estimate | Highest price or terms the client may accept |
| ZOPA | Plausible overlap between IBM and client ranges |

Use ZOPA to:

- Avoid negotiating below minimum acceptable margin.
- Decide whether a price gap should be bridged with scope, timing, payment terms, risk allocation, or value-based mechanisms.
- Identify when there is no rational agreement unless the commercial model changes.

## Concession Discipline

Concessions should be conditional. Do not give without getting.

Use this pattern:

```markdown
If the client needs [concession], IBM can consider [offer] provided the client agrees to [client give].
```

Examples:

| Client request | Avoid | Better response |
|---|---|---|
| 10-20% discount | Immediate price cut | Phased scope, extended term, faster approval, reduced risk exposure, or volume commitment |
| Faster timeline | Same scope / same price | Phased delivery, priority scope, added governance, or adjusted acceptance criteria |
| More features | Free additions | Trade against timeline, budget, future phase, or lower-priority scope |
| Longer support | Free extension | Link to multi-year managed service, renewal, or reduced customisation |
| Competitor price match | Price match by default | Compare TCO, risk, scope, quality, and transformation value; offer value-optimised scope if needed |

Before offering any concession, check:

- Value to client
- Cost to IBM
- Margin impact
- Delivery risk
- Precedent risk
- Approval requirement
- Client commitment received in return

## Tradeable Impact Grid

Use tradeables before discounting. Categorise each possible tradeable by client value and IBM cost.

| Category | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| High client value / low IBM cost | Strong tradeable | Use early to preserve price |
| High client value / high IBM cost | Value-driven tradeable | Use only with meaningful client give |
| Low client value / low IBM cost | Low-priority item | Use sparingly; may not move decision |
| Low client value / high IBM cost | Bad trade | Avoid |

Potential tradeables:

- Phased payment plan
- Phased deployment
- Option years
- Volume commitment
- Scope prioritisation
- Reduced customisation
- Extended support tied to term extension
- Innovation fund funded by gain-share
- Service credit cap adjustment
- Implementation start date or resource reservation
- Governance cadence adjustment
- Training package or knowledge transfer
- Pilot or proof-of-value stage

## Price Objection Handling

When the client says IBM is too expensive:

1. Listen and clarify the objection.
2. Identify the root cause: budget, ROI, procurement pressure, competitor anchor, timing, affordability, risk perception, or missing value case.
3. Reframe from price to value, TCO, risk, outcomes, and delivery assurance.
4. Offer tradeables before discounting.
5. If a discount is unavoidable, make it conditional.

Useful response patterns:

```markdown
I understand the budget concern. Before we reduce price, let's separate affordability from value. The current model is designed to protect [outcome/risk] and avoid [cost/risk]. If affordability is the issue, we can explore phasing, payment profile, or scope sequencing while preserving the outcomes that matter most.
```

```markdown
If the priority is to match a lower upfront price, IBM can model a value-optimised option. We should compare it against the full proposal on total cost of ownership, delivery risk, integration risk, and the outcomes that would be deferred or removed.
```

```markdown
We can consider movement on [commercial term] if the client can commit to [scope lock / multi-year term / faster decision / volume / reduced change exposure]. That preserves the value case while addressing the constraint.
```

## Competitor Undercut Response

When a competitor offers a lower price, avoid reflexive price matching.

Check:

- Is the competitor scope equivalent?
- Are transition, governance, reporting, quality, and support included?
- Are service levels, credits, and risk transfer equivalent?
- Is the delivery model credible?
- Does the competitor price create hidden TCO or change-control exposure?
- Does the competitor model preserve innovation and continuous improvement?

Possible strategies:

- Defend premium with proof of value and risk reduction.
- Offer a phased option that preserves future scalability.
- Create a value-optimised scope at a comparable price without pretending it is the same solution.
- Use gain-share or outcome payments to reduce client risk while protecting IBM upside.
- Adjust payment profile rather than headline price.

## Scenario Planning

Prepare responses for likely pricing scenarios.

| Scenario | Response strategy |
|---|---|
| Client asks for a discount | Use tradeables first; if discounting, require a client give |
| Client delays decision | Quantify cost of delay and offer decision-tied terms |
| Client compresses timeline | Phase delivery or adjust scope; price acceleration risk |
| Competitor undercuts | Compare TCO, risk, outcomes, and included scope |
| Procurement challenges rate cards | Move discussion to value, blended economics, and outcomes |
| Delivery raises risk after negotiation starts | Reprice, add contingency, change scope, or escalate |
| Client wants more scope for same price | Trade scope against timeline, acceptance criteria, or future phase |

For each scenario, define:

- Likelihood
- Impact
- IBM response
- Required approvals
- Message to client
- Walkaway trigger

## Stakeholder-Specific Pricing Messages

Tailor price defence by stakeholder priority.

| Stakeholder | Likely concern | Pricing message |
|---|---|---|
| CFO | Cost, ROI, affordability, TCO | Quantified ROI, cumulative returns, total investment, phased payments, gain-share |
| CIO | Integration, reliability, technical risk | Risk-adjusted delivery, resilience, architecture assurance, technical debt reduction |
| COO | Speed, continuity, operational impact | Phased delivery, quick wins, reduced disruption, operational resilience |
| Procurement | Comparability, benchmark, discount | Clear assumptions, scope equivalence, service levels, benchmark, conditional concessions |
| Legal / Risk | Liability, compliance, service credits | Risk allocation, caps, service credit exposure, auditability, governance |
| Delivery | Feasibility, resource risk, scope | Scope boundaries, assumptions, change control, realistic timeline |

## Negotiation Readiness Checklist

- [ ] Opening price and target price defined
- [ ] Walkaway price and minimum margin defined
- [ ] IBM BATNA and WATNA documented
- [ ] Client BATNA and WATNA estimated
- [ ] ZOPA estimated
- [ ] Concession list approved with give/get conditions
- [ ] Tradeable impact grid completed
- [ ] Competitor undercut response prepared
- [ ] Price objection responses prepared by stakeholder
- [ ] Scope, timeline, and payment-term alternatives modelled
- [ ] Approval thresholds known for discount, margin, legal, and delivery risk
- [ ] Sales, delivery, finance, legal, and procurement aligned on boundaries

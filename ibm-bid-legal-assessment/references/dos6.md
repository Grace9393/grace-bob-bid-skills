# DOS6 Framework Assessment Criteria

Digital Outcomes and Specialists 6 framework-specific legal risk assessment questions.

## 1. Mandatory Eligibility and Exclusion Grounds

### Q1: PCR 2015 Regulation 57 Mandatory Exclusions
What are the specific mandatory exclusion grounds under PCR 2015 Regulation 57 that apply to this tender, and has IBM or any relevant connected entity been subject to convictions for corruption, fraud, terrorist offences, money laundering, or other criminal activities within the relevant time periods?

**DOS6 Context**: DOS6 incorporates standard PCR 2015 mandatory exclusions. IBM must confirm no relevant convictions within 5-year look-back period for connected entities including IBM UK, IBM Europe, IBM Corporation.

**IBM Position**: Absolute requirement - no deviation accepted. Declare any investigations or proceedings even if not yet concluded.

---

### Q2: Discretionary Exclusion Grounds
What are the discretionary exclusion grounds under PCR 2015 Regulation 57 that the contracting authority has chosen to apply in this tender, including breaches of tax, environmental, social, and labour law obligations?

**DOS6 Context**: Typically includes tax compliance (HMRC), insolvency events, professional misconduct, prior contract breaches. Check if specific PPNs referenced (modern slavery, prompt payment).

**IBM Position**: Tax compliance requires current certificates. Professional misconduct provisions require specific incidents within defined timeframes. Reject vague "reputational harm" exclusions.

---

### Q3: Self-Cleaning Provisions
Does the tender specify any self-cleaning provisions under Regulation 57(13)-(16), and if IBM has past issues that could trigger exclusion grounds, what evidence of remedial measures would be required to demonstrate rehabilitation?

**DOS6 Context**: Self-cleaning allowed but rarely invoked in DOS6. Requires demonstration of: remedial action taken, compensation paid, clarification by payment/transparency, compliance programme implementation.

**IBM Position**: If required, prepare detailed evidence pack. Escalate to IBM Legal EMEA for guidance on disclosure approach.

---

### Q4: Economic and Financial Standing
What are the economic and financial standing requirements under Regulation 58, including minimum turnover thresholds, insurance requirements, and financial ratios that IBM must demonstrate to be eligible?

**DOS6 Context**: Typical requirements - turnover threshold 2x contract value over 3 years, specific professional indemnity (£5-10M), public liability (£5-10M), employer's liability (£10M minimum). May require audited accounts or credit ratings.

**IBM Position**: IBM UK meets standard thresholds. Confirm insurance cover meets or exceeds requirements. If parent company guarantee required, escalate to IBM Legal for PCG terms review.

---

### Q5: Technical and Professional Capability
What technical and professional capability requirements are specified as eligibility criteria, including minimum relevant contract experience, certifications (ISO, security clearances), or staff qualification requirements?

**DOS6 Context**: Typically requires 2-3 comparable contracts of similar value/scope in past 3 years. Common certifications: ISO 9001, ISO 27001, Cyber Essentials Plus. Security clearances (SC, DV) may be call-off specific.

**IBM Position**: IBM UK capability generally satisfies DOS6 requirements. Verify specific contract references available. Security clearance requirements may create timeline/cost implications - assess per opportunity.

---

### Q6: Organisational Eligibility
Are there any organisational eligibility requirements regarding corporate structure (e.g., restrictions on subsidiaries bidding, consortium arrangements, or requirements for UK-registered entities) that could affect IBM UK's ability to bid or necessitate specific corporate vehicles?

**DOS6 Context**: No restrictions on subsidiaries or parent companies bidding. Consortia and subcontracting permitted. UK registration not mandatory but UK establishment required for framework access.

**IBM Position**: IBM UK is appropriate contracting entity for DOS6. Consortium arrangements permitted but require named partners at call-off stage.

---

### Q7: Pass/Fail vs Scored Criteria
What are the pass/fail criteria versus scored criteria, and which eligibility requirements represent absolute barriers to entry versus factors that affect competitive positioning?

**DOS6 Context**: Eligibility and exclusion grounds are pass/fail at framework access stage. Individual call-offs typically scored on technical capability (60-70%) and commercial (30-40%). Some minimum capability thresholds may be pass/fail.

**IBM Position**: Ensure absolute clarity on mandatory vs desirable requirements. Challenge any call-off specific pass/fail criteria not established in framework rules.

---

### Q8: Sector-Specific Exclusions
Does the tender impose any sector-specific exclusions or restrictions (e.g., conflicts of interest from previous contracts, restrictions on companies providing certain other services, or limitations based on market dominance)?

**DOS6 Context**: Organisational conflict of interest (OCI) provisions may apply where supplier has advised on requirements or provided assurance services. Market dominance restrictions rare but may appear in specific call-offs.

**IBM Position**: Declare potential conflicts proactively. OCI can often be managed through information barriers. Reject vague conflict provisions that don't specify concrete circumstances.

---

### Q9: Declarations and Evidence Requirements
What declarations, certifications, or third-party evidence must be submitted at the Selection Questionnaire (SQ) stage versus contract award stage, and what are the consequences of non-compliance or late submission?

**DOS6 Context**: Framework access requires SQ with self-declarations. Call-offs may require updated declarations plus evidence (accounts, insurance certificates, references, certifications). Late submission typically results in exclusion.

**IBM Position**: Maintain current evidence pack (insurance, ISO certs, tax compliance, references). Ensure declaration signatories have authority. Late submission rarely recoverable - prioritise deadline compliance.

---

### Q10: Supply Chain Eligibility Requirements
Are there supply chain-related eligibility requirements that extend exclusion grounds to subcontractors or key suppliers, and how might this affect IBM's proposed delivery model or partner ecosystem?

**DOS6 Context**: Exclusion grounds typically apply to subcontractors performing more than 10% of contract value. Buyer may require subcontractor due diligence at call-off stage.

**IBM Position**: Flow-down of eligibility requirements to key subcontractors acceptable. Maintain approved supplier list with current compliance status. Red flag if unlimited right to reject subcontractors.

---

### Q11: Time Limitations on Exclusion Grounds
What time limitations apply to exclusion grounds (e.g., spent convictions provisions, time-barred violations), and are there any specific look-back periods defined in the tender that differ from standard PCR provisions?

**DOS6 Context**: Standard 5-year look-back for most discretionary grounds. Mandatory grounds have no time limit unless conviction spent under Rehabilitation of Offenders Act.

**IBM Position**: Apply UK rehabilitation periods. Disputes/litigation typically 3-year look-back. Challenge any indefinite or excessive look-back periods.

---

### Q12: Cabinet Office PPNs and Supplementary Policies
Does the tender reference the Cabinet Office Procurement Policy Notes (PPNs) or other supplementary policies that impose additional exclusion grounds beyond PCR 2015 (e.g., modern slavery, prompt payment, cyber security requirements)?

**DOS6 Context**: Typically incorporates PPN 01/21 (prompt payment), PPN 03/19 (cyber security), modern slavery declarations. May include defence/security specific restrictions if call-off is protectively marked.

**IBM Position**: IBM complies with prompt payment requirements for supply chain. Cyber Essentials Plus certification standard. Modern slavery statement published annually. Verify no additional sector-specific exclusions.

---

### Q13: Prior Contract Performance Grounds
Are there any grounds related to prior contract performance that could result in exclusion, such as significant contract terminations, performance notices, or litigation with public sector clients within a specified period?

**DOS6 Context**: Serious performance failures within 3 years may trigger discretionary exclusion. Typically requires contract termination for default or significant damages awarded. Performance notices alone rarely sufficient.

**IBM Position**: Declare any public sector contract terminations or major disputes. Performance issues resolved by negotiated exit typically not declarable. Escalate borderline cases to IBM Legal.

---

### Q14: Remediation or Waiver Mechanisms
What remediation or waiver mechanisms exist if IBM identifies potential eligibility concerns, and does the tender allow for clarification questions or discretionary consideration by the contracting authority?

**DOS6 Context**: Contracting authority has discretion on discretionary grounds but not mandatory grounds. Clarification window typically 5-10 days after document publication. Self-cleaning provisions available but require substantial evidence.

**IBM Position**: Use clarification process for ambiguous requirements. Request waiver for disproportionate requirements. Self-cleaning requires Legal sign-off due to disclosure implications.

---

### Q15: Eligibility Requirements by Bidding Role
How do the eligibility and exclusion grounds interact with IBM's intended bidding structure (sole bidder, prime contractor with subcontractors, consortium member), and are there different requirements based on the role IBM intends to fulfil?

**DOS6 Context**: Same eligibility requirements apply regardless of role. Consortium members assessed individually. Prime contractor responsible for subcontractor due diligence.

**IBM Position**: Establish consortium structure early if pursuing joint bid. Ensure all consortium members meet eligibility requirements. Document subcontractor compliance processes.

---

## 2. Liability Caps, Indemnification, IP Ownership

### Q1: IBM Corporate Liability Policy Thresholds
What are IBM's global and UK-specific corporate policies on maximum acceptable liability caps (as multiples of contract value or absolute amounts), and how do these vary by contract type, duration, and service category?

**DOS6 Context**: Standard DOS6 framework terms establish 125% total contract value liability cap. Call-off contracts may vary. UK public sector typically expects 1-2x annual contract value for services, higher for product sales.

**IBM Position**: IBM standard position 1x annual charges for services, uncapped for death/personal injury and IP infringement. Caps below 1x annual charges require escalation to IBM Legal and Risk Management.

---

### Q2: Tender Liability Cap Provisions Below IBM Thresholds
Which specific liability cap provisions in the tender are below IBM's minimum acceptable thresholds, and what is the financial exposure gap between the tender terms and IBM's risk appetite?

**DOS6 Context**: DOS6 framework provides 125% total liability cap across contract life. Some call-offs propose lower caps (e.g., 100% or fixed amount below contract value).

**IBM Position**: RED FLAG if liability cap below 1x annual charges. Quantify exposure gap. Required mitigation: scope reduction, price increase, or cap negotiation. Escalate any cap below £1M regardless of contract value.

---

### Q3: Uncapped Liability Categories
What categories of liability does the tender propose to exclude from caps (e.g., data breaches, IP infringement, wilful misconduct, regulatory penalties), and how does this compare to IBM's standard carve-outs?

**DOS6 Context**: UK government standard uncapped categories - death/personal injury, fraud, wilful misconduct. Increasingly see data breaches, IP infringement, bribery uncapped.

**IBM Position**: Accept uncapped liability for death/personal injury, fraud, wilful default. Data breach uncapped liability is RED FLAG unless defined as wilful/grossly negligent acts only. IP infringement should be capped for third-party IP embodied in standard products.

---

### Q4: Indemnification Obligations for Third-Party Claims
Does the tender impose uncapped or disproportionate indemnification obligations on IBM for third-party claims, customer losses, or consequential damages that exceed standard commercial practice?

**DOS6 Context**: UK government typically requires indemnity for third-party IP infringement claims. May see broader indemnities for data breaches, security incidents, regulatory penalties.

**IBM Position**: Accept IP infringement indemnity subject to standard exclusions (compliance with customer specifications, customer misuse, modification). REJECT indemnities for customer's own acts/omissions, consequential losses, unlimited scope.

---

### Q5: Intellectual Property Ownership Requirements
What are the intellectual property ownership requirements—does the customer demand full ownership of foreground IP, background IP, or pre-existing IBM tools and methodologies that would compromise IBM's reusable assets?

**DOS6 Context**: UK government default position - Crown ownership of foreground IP created specifically for the project. DOS6 framework allows suppliers to retain IP in reusable tools/methods with licence grant to customer.

**IBM Position**: IBM retains background IP (pre-existing tools, methods, platforms). Grant perpetual, irrevocable licence to customer for foreground IP incorporated in deliverables. REJECT: transfer of IBM pre-existing IP, unlimited licence to IBM confidential materials, assignment of IP in reusable components.

---

### Q6: Flow-Down Clauses to Subcontractors
Are there "flow-down" clauses requiring IBM to pass identical liability and indemnification terms to subcontractors, and is this commercially feasible with IBM's supply chain?

**DOS6 Context**: DOS6 typically requires "back-to-back" terms with subcontractors. May be limited to key/named subcontractors or those providing >10% of contract value.

**IBM Position**: Accept proportionate flow-down (subcontractor liable for own scope). REJECT: requirement to impose terms more onerous than IBM's own exposure (e.g., uncapped subcontractor liability where IBM's is capped). Assess subcontractor acceptance before committing.

---

### Q7: Comparison Against Standard Frameworks
How do the proposed liability and indemnity terms compare against standard UK government frameworks (G-Cloud, Crown Commercial Service templates, NEC contracts) and where are the material deviations?

**DOS6 Context**: DOS6 framework establishes baseline terms. Call-off contracts often reference CCS Model Services Contract or NEC4 framework. Check which standard terms apply and what amendments made.

**IBM Position**: DOS6 framework terms are generally acceptable baseline. Material deviations in call-offs require legal review. Track changes document essential - don't accept "standard terms" without verification.

---

### Q8: Insurance Requirements
What insurance requirements are mandated (professional indemnity, cyber liability, public liability levels), and can IBM's existing coverage meet these without additional cost or are they commercially unreasonable?

**DOS6 Context**: Professional indemnity £5-10M standard for IT services. Public liability £5-10M. Employer's liability £10M (statutory minimum). Cyber liability increasingly required (£5-10M).

**IBM Position**: IBM UK maintains £50M professional indemnity, £50M public liability. Cyber liability covered under professional indemnity. Requirements above £50M require specific underwriting - escalate to IBM Risk Management.

---

### Q9: Audit Rights and Data Protection Liability Exposure
Are there audit rights, data protection obligations, or security breach notification requirements that carry unlimited liability exposure or onerous obligations that cannot be operationally delivered?

**DOS6 Context**: Extensive audit rights standard in government contracts (NAO, departmental audit, ICO). GDPR obligations typically impose joint/several liability. Breach notification within 24-72 hours common.

**IBM Position**: Accept audit rights during term + 7 years post-termination, at reasonable times, with confidentiality. Data protection liability should be several, not joint, and subject to overall cap except for wilful breaches. Notification timelines must be operationally achievable (suggest 72 hours from detection).

---

### Q10: Most Favoured Customer and Benchmarking Clauses
Does the contract contain "most favoured customer" clauses, benchmarking rights, or price reduction mechanisms that would create precedent-setting commercial disadvantages across IBM's portfolio?

**DOS6 Context**: MFN clauses uncommon in DOS6 but may appear in large contracts. Benchmarking rights typically triggered at specified intervals (annually or at extension options).

**IBM Position**: REJECT MFN clauses - incompatible with IBM's commercial model. Accept benchmarking at extension decision points only, with defined methodology, comparable contracts, and mutual agreement on adjustments. Reject automatic price reductions.

---

### Q11: Liability for Cloud Services and AI/ML Outputs
What is the tender's position on limitation of liability for cloud services, AI/ML outputs, or emerging technology where liability frameworks are evolving and IBM requires protective language?

**DOS6 Context**: Increasingly seeing AI/ML workloads in DOS6. Government position evolving - may seek to impose traditional software liability to AI outputs. Cloud SLAs typically tied to supplier's upstream provider.

**IBM Position**: AI/ML outputs provided "as-is" with no warranty of accuracy, fitness for specific purpose. Liability limited to re-performance or refund. Cloud services limited to IBM's remedies from underlying providers (e.g., AWS, Azure). Reject liability for customer's use of AI outputs.

---

### Q12: Asymmetric Termination Rights
Are there termination provisions that allow the customer to exit without cause while imposing penalty regimes, IP forfeiture, or transition obligations that create asymmetric risk for IBM?

**DOS6 Context**: DOS6 framework allows termination for convenience with notice (typically 30-90 days). May see termination rights at extension points. Rare to see termination fees payable to supplier.

**IBM Position**: Accept termination for convenience with notice period matching mobilisation duration (minimum 90 days). Require payment for committed resources, abortive work. REJECT: IP forfeiture on termination, free transition services exceeding 90 days, termination without payment of earned amounts.

## Risk Classification Guide

**RED (Bid Blocker)**
- Uncapped liability below £5M contract value
- IP assignment of IBM background/platform IP
- Unlimited parent company guarantee
- TUPE obligations with undefined workforce
- Exclusion grounds IBM cannot satisfy
- Mandatory security clearances undeliverable in tender timeline

**AMBER (Significant Risk - Mitigation Required)**
- Liability caps <1x annual charges but >0.5x
- Data sovereignty with undefined "UK soil" requirement
- Benchmarking without defined methodology
- Parent company guarantee exceeding contract value
- Exit obligations >90 days unpaid
- Performance bonds >10% contract value

**GREEN (Acceptable or Manageable)**
- Liability caps ≥1x annual charges
- Standard UK government audit rights
- IPR with background IP retention and licence grant
- Named subcontractor approval process
- Security clearances with reasonable timelines
- TUPE with defined workforce numbers

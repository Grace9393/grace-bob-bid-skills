# Strategic Positioning Framework

## Level 3 Analysis: Strategic Decision-Making

### Executive Decision Drivers

Understanding what drives procurement decisions at executive level is critical for response positioning.

**Career risk mitigation:**
- Safe choice indicators (reference requirements, case studies from similar orgs)
- Risk-averse language (proven, established, track record)
- Steering committee involvement → multiple stakeholders to satisfy
- Detailed governance requirements → need for defensible decision

**What this means for response:**
- Emphasize proven solutions with strong references
- Highlight similar organizations who made same choice successfully
- Provide clear audit trail and decision justification
- Show how solution reduces personal/organizational risk

**Organisational credibility:**
Different sectors have different credibility requirements:

*Public sector:*
- Audit trail and transparency (decisions must be defensible publicly)
- Value for money (not just cost, but outcomes per pound spent)
- Public accountability (visible delivery of citizen benefits)

*Private sector:*
- ROI and bottom-line impact (financial returns)
- Competitive advantage (market positioning benefits)
- Innovation and differentiation (ahead of competitors)

*Third sector:*
- Mission impact (outcomes for beneficiaries)
- Stakeholder engagement (trustees, funders, service users)
- Resource stewardship (maximize impact per pound)

**Political coverage:**
Indicators of internal politics requiring careful navigation:
- Multiple approval layers → contentious procurement with competing interests
- Stakeholder engagement requirements → need to satisfy diverse groups
- Extensive documentation requirements → covering decision trail against future challenge
- Reference to "business case" or "options appraisal" → justification scrutiny expected

**What this means for response:**
- Acknowledge stakeholder diversity and engagement needs
- Provide materials for internal selling (slides, FAQs, comparison matrices)
- Emphasize consensus-building approach and change management
- Show how solution addresses concerns of different stakeholder groups

### Capability Building vs Problem Solving

Most tenders contain elements of both. The balance determines response emphasis.

**Immediate problem solving signals:**
- Tight timelines (less than 12 months to delivery)
- Operational pain emphasis ("currently unable to...", "system failures")
- Quick wins required ("demonstrate value within X months")
- BAU maintenance focus ("sustain current operations while...")
- Fix broken processes ("replace failing system", "address inefficiencies")

**Future capability building signals:**
- Transformation language ("modernize", "transform", "reimagine")
- Platform thinking ("foundation for future", "scalable architecture")
- Skills transfer requirements ("embed capability", "knowledge transfer")
- Innovation emphasis ("leading edge", "best practice", "future-proof")
- Build new capabilities ("enable us to...", "position us for...")

**Analysis approach:**
1. Count instances of each signal type in tender
2. Weight by evaluation criteria (if capability building gets 40% weighting, it's genuinely important)
3. Identify any contradiction (e.g., tight timeline but transformation language)
4. Recommend response balance (e.g., 60% problem solving, 40% capability building)

**Response implications:**

*If predominantly problem solving:*
- Emphasize rapid deployment and quick wins
- Show proven, low-risk solutions
- Focus on operational metrics and immediate ROI
- Highlight support and handholding during transition

*If predominantly capability building:*
- Emphasize innovation and future-proofing
- Show platform thinking and scalability
- Focus on strategic outcomes and long-term value
- Highlight skills transfer and knowledge embedding

*If balanced (common):*
- Structure response in phases: Stabilize → Optimize → Transform
- Show how solution addresses immediate pain while building future capability
- Use "foundation" framing: solid base for current needs, platform for future growth

### Win Theme Identification

Win themes are not generic value propositions. They are specific arguments for why we should win this specific deal.

**Primary themes from analysis:**
1. **Match highest weighted evaluation criteria to client pain points**
   - If quality/technical is 60%, win themes must address technical differentiation
   - If social value is 20%, one win theme should be local/social impact

2. **Identify gaps where client lacks confidence**
   - Detailed requirements in one area = uncertainty or past failure
   - Vague requirements = don't know what's possible (opportunity to educate)

3. **Find risks client hasn't articulated but sector/context suggests**
   - Healthcare: clinical safety, IG compliance (even if not explicit)
   - Government: transparency, audit (assumed but often understated)

4. **Locate areas where competitors likely to use generic approaches**
   - Integration complexity often underestimated by product vendors
   - Change management often underestimated by tech-focused bidders
   - Sector-specific nuances missed by generalist consultancies

**Differentiation opportunities:**
- Where client requirements suggest they don't know what's possible (educate)
- Areas with vague requirements (define the art of the possible)
- Integration challenges underestimated in RFP (highlight complexity we manage)
- Change management complexity not fully appreciated (show our experience)
- Sector-specific requirements others will treat generically (demonstrate deep context)

**Win theme structure (for ibm-bid-win-themes skill):**
For each potential theme, document:
- **Customer issue**: Specific pain point or need from tender
- **Evaluation criteria link**: Which scoring dimension this addresses
- **Competitor weakness**: Why likely bidders will miss or underdeliver on this
- **Our proof point**: Specific evidence we can deliver (for ibm-bid-customer-stories)

### Competitive Positioning

Position against each likely bidder type with specific counter-strategies:

**Against large consultancies (Accenture, Capgemini, Deloitte):**

*Their likely play:*
- Emphasize scale and global delivery capability
- Highlight methodology and frameworks (proprietary approaches)
- Show breadth across technology and business transformation
- Leverage offshore delivery for cost competitiveness

*Our counter positioning:*
- Agility: Faster decision-making, less bureaucracy, more responsive
- Senior access: Direct access to senior leaders, not just account managers
- Contextual expertise: Deeper sector knowledge, not generic approach
- Lower risk: Fewer layers, clearer accountability, proven delivery
- Integration depth: Technology+consulting, not consultants integrating others' tech

**Against pure tech vendors (Salesforce, Microsoft, Oracle):**

*Their likely play:*
- Focus on product capabilities and features
- Highlight product roadmap and R&D investment
- Show market share and customer base
- Emphasize ecosystem and partner network

*Our counter positioning:*
- Implementation experience: We've seen what goes wrong, not just what works
- Sector knowledge: Industry-specific challenges, not generic deployment
- Outcome focus: Business results, not just technical deployment
- Integration breadth: Multi-vendor landscape, not single-platform dependency
- Change management: Adoption and benefits realization, not just installation

**Against specialists (sector-specific or niche tech firms):**

*Their likely play:*
- Emphasize deep domain expertise in specific area
- Highlight focus and specialization
- Show detailed understanding of sector nuances
- Position as experts vs. generalists

*Our counter positioning:*
- Enterprise scale: Ability to deliver across multiple locations/functions
- Integration capability: Connect specialist solution to wider enterprise architecture
- Governance and risk: Enterprise-grade controls and compliance
- Longevity: Long-term partnership, not point solution dependency
- IBM backing: Resources and investment beyond small specialist firm

**Client Zero/transformation stories:**
IBM's own transformation is powerful competitive positioning:
- Your own transformation = credibility ("we've lived this journey")
- Lessons learned = risk mitigation ("we know what goes wrong")
- Current state → future state journey = relatability ("we understand where you are")

Use when:
- Client facing similar transformation (legacy to cloud, waterfall to agile)
- Client concerned about transformation risk (show we've de-risked through own experience)
- Client needs executive-level confidence (CIO to CIO conversations)

### Sales Strategy Selection Framework

**Critical context:**
- At ITT stage, procurement criteria are usually locked
- Flanking (reset criteria) requires client internal support - unlikely if we weren't consulted pre-ITT
- Delay (postpone action) is not an option once ITT is issued
- Most ITT-stage strategies are Attack strategies

**Selection criteria:**

*Choose FRONTAL when:*
- We have clear technical superiority or unique capability
- Our price is competitive or we can justify premium
- Requirements play to our strengths
- We have strong incumbent position or client relationship
- Risk: Must be genuinely superior; frontal attacks fail if claims don't hold up

*Choose FLANKING when (rare at ITT stage):*
- We're at parity or slightly disadvantaged on stated criteria
- We have strong internal client advocates who can influence
- There's flexibility to add or re-weight criteria
- We can demonstrate alternative approach that's clearly better
- Risk: Requires client support; if they won't adjust criteria, this fails

*Choose FRAGMENT when:*
- We're disadvantaged overall but have one major strength
- Tender can be split into components (technical, support, training, etc.)
- We can target specific department, function, or geography
- Our goal is foothold for future expansion
- Risk: May win small piece but struggle to expand; client may prefer single supplier

*Choose DEFEND when (incumbent protection):*
- We're incumbent with dominant position
- Tender is refresh/renewal of existing contract
- Requirements favor our existing solution
- Our goal is to discourage challengers and maintain position
- Risk: Complacency; challengers may fragment or flank if we don't strengthen

*Choose DELAY when (incumbent, pre-ITT only):*
- We're incumbent but current solution is weak
- We need time for new technology/solution release
- We can influence client to postpone procurement
- Risk: Only works pre-ITT; once ITT issued, delay not possible

**Strategic combinations:**
- Primary strategy: Overall approach (usually Frontal or Fragment)
- Secondary tactics: Support primary (e.g., Frontal attack on technical, Fragment on specific geography)

### Price to Win Psychology

**Why clients don't trust traditional pricing:**
- Bottom-up pricing leads to "gold-plating" (vendors add everything possible)
- Late-stage cuts feel desperate and reduce confidence
- Lack of transparency around what drives cost

**Top-down approach builds confidence:**
- Start from what client can afford/expects to pay
- Solution within that envelope (shows constraint management)
- Transparent about trade-offs and options
- Demonstrates we understand their business reality

**Common mistakes to avoid:**
1. **"Rolls Royce" syndrome**: Over-engineering because we can, not because client needs
2. **Late-stage panic cutting**: Reduces features/quality to hit price, increases delivery risk
3. **Margin-first thinking**: Starting from our margin target, not client budget reality
4. **Single viewpoint**: Only considering one perspective (e.g., just competitive)

**Best practice approach:**
- Start Price to Win analysis in Phase 0 (now)
- Update as new information emerges through clarifications
- Use as constraint for solution design (not afterthought)
- Treat as range, not single number (e.g., £2-2.5M, not £2.3M)
- Document assumptions clearly for deal team review

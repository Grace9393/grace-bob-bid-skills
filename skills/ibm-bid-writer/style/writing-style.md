# Writing Style

## Core Writing Principles for Tender Responses

### Storytelling in Tender Responses

Build a narrative that demonstrates understanding of the client's situation and showcases ability to deliver results. Effective tender storytelling should include:

1. Emotional connection through understanding of the client's situation and hot buttons
2. Appropriate attention to detail
3. Clear and memorable narrative structure
4. Passion and belief in the proposed solution
5. Clear journey to desired outcomes
6. Unique positioning and differentiation

Assess whether the story meets the S.T.A.R. approach:
`Something They'll Always Remember`.

### Active Voice Usage

Use human subjects demonstrating action and focus on how results are achieved for customers.
Avoid over-commitment words such as `ensures` or `guarantees` when the evidence cannot support certainty.

Poor:
`Design decisions on our products were made based on user's specifications.`

Good:
`Our engineers designed our products with your users in mind.`

### Mode-Appropriate Writing Style

The default for tender responses is formal scored-response mode:
accessible but formal, evaluator-facing, and commercially credible.

Conversational drafting mode is optional and should only be used for internal or exploratory drafts when explicitly requested.

Ensure sections have introductory sentences rather than just bullet points to maintain narrative flow, while recognising that bullet points are acceptable when they enhance clarity.

### Concise Writing

Check for:

1. Main idea stated upfront with supporting information
2. Short paragraphs
3. Unified paragraphs with connected sentences where helpful
4. Only necessary words used
5. Concrete images and precise measures
6. Consistent technical terminology
7. Positive descriptions of what the solution does
8. No redundancy or repetition
9. Use `while` instead of `whilst`
10. Always use British English spelling
11. Avoid American business terminology

### Informative Headings

Headings should clearly indicate section contents and benefits. They should highlight new topics, connect features to benefits, and often mention differentiators.

### Quantified Benefits with Baseline Context

All benefit claims should include specific, measurable data with baseline comparison.

Poor:
`Reduces response time by 40%`

Good:
`Reduces response time by 40% (from 10 minutes to 6 minutes) compared to manual processes`

Excellent:
`Reduces response time by 40% (from 10 minutes to 6 minutes) compared to manual processes, saving £500,000 annually in staffing costs`

## Question Response Strategy Assessment

### Client Language and Structure Mirroring

Check whether the response:

- uses the client's exact terminology and phrasing where possible
- follows the order of sub-questions as presented
- addresses all required parts

### Comprehensive Coverage

Complex questions should be broken down into all component parts and answered explicitly.
For example, verify whether a single question covers:

1. approach to implementation
2. approach to assurance
3. particular skills brought to the engagement
4. management across multiple tickets or workstreams
5. impact assessment
6. effort estimation
7. stakeholder engagement activities
8. sub-contractor or consortium delivery where relevant

### Innovation Assessment

Responses should demonstrate innovation in ways of working, commercial approaches, or methodologies. Generic approaches without innovative elements will not achieve higher marks.

### Assumption Avoidance

Use only information provided by the client or verified source material. Flag content that makes unsupported assumptions.

### Competitive Differentiation

Responses should clearly articulate competitive advantages, including unique capabilities, delivery methods, security features, or domain expertise.

### Stakeholder-Specific Value

Tailor value propositions accordingly:

- Technical teams: integration complexity, API capabilities
- Finance: TCO, budget impact, cost allocation
- Compliance: audit trails, regulatory alignment
- Operations: workflow impact, productivity metrics

## Response Structure and Format Evaluation

### Clear Organisation

The response should maintain:

- clear structure throughout
- section numbers aligned with question numbering where helpful
- sensible combination of prose and bullet points for structure and clarity
- strong preference for paragraphs and prose over bullets, with 80% prose and 20% bullets as a useful default balance

### Cross-Referencing Guidelines

- Cross-reference within a question and its associated attachments where appropriate
- Do not cross-reference between different questions
- Remember different evaluators may only see their assigned question

### Diagram Assessment

Diagrams should:

- balance detail with clarity and readability
- avoid being overly busy
- have correct and clear figure numbers, names, and cross-references
- not be reused without clear logic and explanation

### Generic Content Identification

Flag any generic answers or diagrams that are not specifically tailored to the client's requirements and situation.

### Executive Summary Requirements

When executive summaries are required, include:

- key metrics such as savings percentages
- time reductions
- typical ROI timeframe
- financial impact where possible

## Evidence and Substantiation Evaluation

### Ecosystem Partners Assessment

Partner references should:

- go beyond naming partners
- explain why partners are included
- explain what skills they provide
- explain what risks they mitigate
- focus on value and capability rather than name-dropping

For example, `A recent implementation` or `a manufacturing client` is too vague without scale, baseline, or magnitude.

### Benefits and Claims Verification

Support all claims with specific evidence and examples. Apply the `So What?` test so technical features connect to clear business outcomes.

Poor:
`Our GenAI tool can reduce discovery effort by 20%`

Good:
`Our GenAI tool reduced discovery effort by 20% on a similar recent project with ACME Co.`

Excellent:
`Our GenAI tool reduced discovery effort by 20% on a similar recent project with ACME Co, saving £100K`

### References and Examples Assessment

References should be:

- relevant
- detailed
- numerous enough to show repeatable capability

Example of insufficient detail:
`The bidder provides examples where they have supported 30 CNI services, but there was no detail on these to support the scale and magnitude.`

### Client Example Requirements

At least one real or anonymised example should include:

- industry or sector
- problem baseline
- measured outcome
- timeframe
- financial impact if available

Ensure all claims are based on actual information provided or publicly available verified data.
Ensure examples reference real, verifiable case studies rather than hypothetical scenarios.

## Precision and Clarity Assessment

### Risk Management Evaluation

Check whether responses:

- elaborate on key risks and mitigations
- include mitigation strategies with specific implementation steps
- address contingency planning
- provide governance frameworks where relevant

### Value-Added Services Clarity

If value-added services are proposed, state clearly if there is no additional cost to the client.

### Tooling and Approvals Recognition

If new tooling is proposed, acknowledge client approvals and relevant contingencies.
Commit to working with client departments to obtain necessary approvals where needed.

### Precise Metrics Assessment

Poor:
`20% of the team will be apprentices`

Good:
`The team will have five apprentices (representing 20% of the team)`

### Cost, ROI, and Time to Value

Include where available:

- deployment time
- expected payback period
- cost ranges
- TCO considerations

### Implementation Readiness

Cover:

- prerequisites and dependencies
- integration complexity
- change management considerations
- staff training requirements and duration

### Scalability and Future-Proofing

Address:

- performance metrics at different usage scales
- upgrade pathways and version compatibility
- long-term cost projections where relevant

## Quality Evaluation Checklist

Before finalising, verify that the tender response:

- addresses all required elements
- uses the client's language and terminology
- follows the question structure and order
- demonstrates innovation and unique value
- provides specific, relevant examples with quantified outcomes
- avoids generic content and unsupported assumptions
- maintains clear structure with informative headings
- uses active voice and the correct tone for the selected mode, with formal scored-response mode as default
- includes precise metrics and concrete details
- substantiates all claims with evidence
- employs British English spelling and avoids Americanisms
- uses no em dashes; replace them with commas, colons, semicolons, parentheses, or new sentences
- avoids en dashes as sentence punctuation
- quantifies benefits with baseline context
- includes competitive differentiation where appropriate
- addresses stakeholder-specific value propositions
- applies the `So What?` test to technical features
- covers implementation readiness and scalability
- flags fabricated information using `{{++REPLACE: text}}`
- avoids over-commitment words such as `ensures` or `guarantees`
- includes introductory sentences for sections rather than just bullet points

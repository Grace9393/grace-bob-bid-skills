# Contracting Models

Use this reference to select or compare commercial models. Prefer hybrid value-based models for complex services where operational stability, automation, measurable outcomes, and continuous improvement all matter.

## Model Selection Summary

| Model | Best for | Watch-outs |
|---|---|---|
| T&M with guardrails | Uncertain scope, discovery, early agile work | Can reward effort unless paired with value controls |
| Fixed price | Stable scope, clear deliverables, price certainty | Can drive scope disputes and underprice change |
| Outcome-based contracting | Measurable outcomes and clear data | Requires strong baselines and governance |
| Gain-share | Automation and efficiency savings | Requires transparent savings methodology |
| Managed service with outcome SLAs | Stable operations with high service assurance | Must price service credit exposure and demand volatility |
| Hybrid value-based model | Complex transformation plus BAU service | Needs clear model boundaries and governance |
| Performance-based partnering | Long-term partnership where behaviour matters | Requires credible behavioural scoring and transparency |

## Outcome-Based Contracting

Outcome-based contracting ties payment to the achievement of agreed outcomes rather than labour inputs.

Use when:

- Outcomes can be clearly defined and measured.
- The client wants improved quality, compliance, user experience, or digital adoption.
- Supplier should have flexibility over how outcomes are achieved.
- Automation and continuous improvement should be commercially rewarded.

Core structure:

- Base service fee for minimum operational capacity.
- Outcome payment pool released when thresholds are met.
- Stretch incentives for exceeding targets.
- Service credits or penalties for failing baseline outcomes.
- Innovation fund for continuous optimisation.

Example outcome domains:

- Accuracy and integrity
- Timeliness
- Digital adoption
- User experience and accessibility
- Operational efficiency
- Compliance and security
- Data quality
- Resilience and availability

Conditions for success:

- Stable KPI definitions
- Validated baselines
- Transparent data
- Strong governance
- Anti-gaming controls
- Clear statutory or regulatory constraints

## Gain-Share Contracting

Gain-share rewards supplier-led improvements that reduce cost, effort, risk, or waste.

Use when:

- There are measurable efficiency opportunities.
- Automation or process redesign can reduce manual effort.
- Both parties can agree a baseline and savings methodology.
- The client wants innovation without repeated business case approvals.

Core structure:

- Baseline measurement of cost, volumes, effort, error rates, or service performance.
- Defined improvement categories.
- Sharing ratio, often 50/50, 60/40, or 70/30 depending on investment and risk.
- Savings calculation method.
- Cap and measurement period.
- Reinvestment option for part of gains.
- Joint governance to validate gains.

Suitable gain categories:

- Reduced manual effort or FTE-equivalent work
- Reduced rework, errors, exceptions, or backlogs
- Reduced paper, scanning, postage, or storage
- Reduced call volumes or correspondence
- Faster processing or better SLA adherence
- Reduced technical debt or platform cost

Common controls:

- Exclude savings caused by demand collapse unless agreed.
- Require independent or jointly governed validation.
- Cap supplier upside where public-sector affordability is sensitive.
- Reinvest a proportion into further improvement where appropriate.

## Managed Service With Outcome SLAs

A managed service with outcome SLAs gives the supplier end-to-end responsibility for defined service outcomes.

Use when:

- Operational stability is critical.
- The client wants predictable performance and service assurance.
- The service has clear recurring responsibilities.
- The supplier can control enough of the service chain to be accountable.
- Failures have high operational, financial, legal, or reputational consequences.

Core structure:

- Base managed service fee.
- Outcome-aligned SLAs.
- Service credits for underperformance.
- Optional performance bonuses for stretch outcomes.
- Continuous improvement obligations.
- Governance and reporting commitments.

Example SLA categories:

- Processing timeliness
- Quality and accuracy
- Availability and resilience
- Reconciliation and financial integrity
- Security and fraud monitoring
- Digital channel performance
- Complaint resolution
- Accessibility and vulnerable user support

Pricing considerations:

- Demand volatility and volume bands
- Transition and dual-running cost
- Reporting and dashboard cost
- SLA credit exposure
- Continuous improvement obligations
- Technology refresh and security patching
- Third-party dependency assumptions

## Hybrid Value-Based Models

Hybrid value-based models combine multiple mechanisms into a single commercial architecture.

Use when:

- BAU needs stability but transformation needs incentives.
- Different service components need different commercial mechanics.
- Outcomes are measurable but transformation is still evolving.
- The client needs both price predictability and innovation.
- Risk and reward must be balanced over time.

Common patterns:

### Managed Service + Outcome Payments

- Base fee ensures service stability.
- SLAs enforce accuracy, timeliness, security, and availability.
- Outcome payments reward digital adoption, automation, or strategic metrics.

### Managed Service + Gain-Share + Innovation Fund

- Managed service covers BAU.
- Gain-share rewards reductions in manual work, rework, call volumes, or exceptions.
- Innovation fund finances AI, automation, UX, data quality, and accessibility improvements.

### Outcome-Based Contracting + Gain-Share

- Supplier is rewarded for achieving outcome targets.
- Additional gain-share rewards efficiency and reduced effort.
- Strong fit where automation and service quality are both strategic goals.

### Transformation + Managed Service Split

- Transformation delivered under milestone, fixed price, or outcome-based payments.
- BAU delivered as a managed service with outcome SLAs.
- Useful when build and run risks differ.

Hybrid design rules:

- Define which mechanism applies to which service component.
- Avoid double-paying for the same outcome.
- Make payment triggers simple enough to govern.
- Include anti-gaming controls.
- Keep statutory or critical service thresholds non-negotiable.
- Set review points for KPI evolution.

## Performance-Based Partnering

Performance-based partnering adds behavioural and collaboration incentives to delivery outcomes.

Use when:

- Long-term partnership matters.
- The supplier must proactively identify risk and improvement opportunities.
- The service needs transparency, joint governance, and continuous evolution.
- Transactional delivery would undermine transformation.

Core structure:

- Operational performance score.
- Transformation and innovation score.
- Partnership behaviour score.
- Outcome-linked payments or incentive pools.
- Gain-share eligibility tied to transparency and governance participation.

Example scorecard:

| Domain | Example metrics | Typical weight |
|---|---|---:|
| Operational performance | SLA adherence, accuracy, backlog control, resilience | 40-50% |
| Transformation outcomes | Automation delivered, digital adoption, process redesign | 20-30% |
| Partnership behaviour | Transparency, proactive risk management, governance quality | 20-30% |

Controls:

- Use evidence-based scoring, not subjective sentiment alone.
- Define behavioural expectations in contract language.
- Link severe transparency failures to loss of incentive eligibility.
- Ensure behaviours support, rather than replace, hard outcome thresholds.

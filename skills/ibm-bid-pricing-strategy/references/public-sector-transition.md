# Public-Sector Transition Guidance

Use this reference for high-integrity public-sector services, especially where statutory obligations, financial controls, vulnerable users, legacy technology, or data quality risks affect pricing strategy.

## Why Public-Sector Value-Based Pricing Needs Extra Controls

Public-sector services often require more than generic value-based pricing because:

- Legal or statutory duties cannot be transferred away from the authority.
- Vulnerable users may be harmed by service errors or delays.
- Manual legacy processes can make baselines unreliable.
- Third-party dependencies may be outside supplier control.
- Accessibility, fairness, and transparency are part of the value proposition.
- Over-automation can create risk where human judgement is required.
- Procurement scrutiny requires clear value-for-money evidence.

Price the model so it protects public value, not only IBM margin.

## Outcome Domains For High-Integrity Services

Consider these domains when designing outcome payments, SLAs, or performance scorecards:

- Statutory compliance
- Accuracy and financial integrity
- Reconciliation timeliness and completeness
- Error thresholds and critical incident tolerance
- Payment or transaction timeliness
- Data quality and audit trail completeness
- Security, fraud monitoring, and resilience
- Digital adoption and reduction of paper
- Accessibility and assisted digital support
- User satisfaction and complaint resolution
- Backlog reduction and demand management
- Automation coverage and reduced manual intervention
- Continuous improvement delivery

Treat statutory compliance and critical accuracy thresholds as guardrails, not optional stretch targets.

## Transition Roadmap

### Phase 1: Preparation And Baseline Establishment

Establish:

- Strategic objectives and priority outcomes.
- Current volumes, costs, FTE allocation, and manual effort.
- Error rates, rework loops, backlog, and processing times.
- SLA performance and service quality.
- Data quality and reporting maturity.
- Technology constraints and integration dependencies.
- Governance roles and Intelligent Client Function capability.

Do not finalise outcome payments until baselines are credible.

### Phase 2: Commercial Model Design

Select the model:

- Outcome-based contracting
- Gain-share
- Managed service with outcome SLAs
- Hybrid value-based model
- Performance-based partnering

Most complex statutory services fit a hybrid model that combines:

- Base managed service fee for stability
- Outcome SLAs for statutory and operational integrity
- Outcome payment pool for transformation priorities
- Gain-share for automation and efficiency
- Innovation fund for continuous improvement
- Performance-based partnering for transparency and governance behaviours

Define:

- Payment formulas
- Outcome payment pools
- Service credits and penalties
- Gain-share ratio and cap
- Innovation fund structure
- KPI definitions and data sources
- Escalation paths and review cycles

### Phase 3: Transition And Shadow Running

Before activating material value-based payments:

- Run old and new measures in parallel.
- Validate baseline accuracy.
- Stress-test KPIs and dashboards.
- Confirm supplier reporting capability.
- Test transition scenarios and service continuity.
- Train contract managers and operational teams.

Shadow running is especially important when current service data is fragmented, manual, or inconsistent.

### Phase 4: Full Adoption And Continuous Improvement

Activate payments and governance only when measurement is trusted.

Maintain:

- Quarterly performance reviews.
- Annual KPI recalibration.
- Automation and digital adoption baselines.
- Improvement backlog.
- Data quality remediation.
- Innovation fund governance.
- Independent assurance where statutory risk is high.

## Key Success Factors

- Clear, measurable outcomes.
- Accurate baselines and reliable data.
- Mature Intelligent Client Function.
- Strong supplier transparency and collaboration.
- Technology enablement and automation readiness.
- Contractual continuous improvement.
- Agile governance and rapid escalation.
- User-centred design and accessibility.
- Secure and compliant operations.
- Change management and staff enablement.

## Risk Mitigation Strategies

### Protect Statutory And Financial Integrity

- Embed statutory compliance into SLAs.
- Set zero-tolerance or near-zero thresholds for critical errors.
- Require audit-ready trails.
- Use mandatory escalation paths for high-risk events.
- Ensure incentives reinforce legal duties.

### Strengthen Data Quality And Reporting

- Baseline and remediate data quality before payment activation.
- Use automated validation where possible.
- Agree reporting formats and calculation methods.
- Provide real-time dashboards for material KPIs.
- Establish data governance with client oversight.

### Address Technology And Integration Constraints

- Assess technology readiness early.
- Use API-first integration principles where appropriate.
- Price legacy integration risk and third-party dependencies.
- Include cybersecurity hardening and resilience.
- Identify manual workarounds that could undermine KPI measurement.

### Maintain Operational Continuity

- Price transition and dual-running explicitly.
- Require knowledge transfer and onboarding.
- Use scenario-based readiness testing.
- Protect vulnerable or urgent user journeys.
- Avoid big-bang switching where failure impact is high.

### Prevent Perverse Behaviours

- Use balanced scorecards across performance, transformation, and behaviour.
- Monitor for KPI gaming.
- Review whether automation is being applied safely.
- Preserve human judgement where legal, ethical, or vulnerability concerns require it.
- Link incentives to how outcomes are achieved, not just whether a metric moved.

### Build Governance Capability

- Define decision rights.
- Create operations, design, transformation, data, and risk forums where needed.
- Keep change control lean but risk-tiered.
- Establish rapid escalation routes.
- Use independent assurance for high-risk areas.

## Pricing Implications

Include explicit pricing for:

- Transition and shadow running.
- Reporting, dashboarding, and data quality remediation.
- Governance forums and assurance.
- Accessibility and assisted digital channels.
- Security, fraud, and resilience controls.
- Innovation fund contribution.
- Technology roadmap and continuous improvement.
- Service credit exposure and demand volatility.
- Third-party integration assumptions.

Avoid hiding these costs in generic overhead; they are part of what makes the value-based model governable.

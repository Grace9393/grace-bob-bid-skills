# Departmental Procurement Assessment Criteria

Legal risk assessment for direct departmental procurements outside standard CCS/Crown frameworks (OJEU/Find a Tender, NEC contracts, bespoke departmental agreements).

## Overview

Departmental procurements vary significantly by contracting authority. This reference provides assessment approach where no standard framework terms apply.

## Common Departmental Approaches

- **OJEU/Find a Tender**: Open procedures, restricted procedures, competitive dialogue
- **NEC Contracts**: NEC3/NEC4 Professional Services Contract, Engineering and Construction Contract
- **Bespoke terms**: Department-specific contracts (MOD, NHS, HMRC variations)
- **Academic/research**: UKRI, university partnerships (TRAC methodology, IP complexities)

## Key Assessment Variations

### Ministry of Defence
- Security clearances mandatory (SC, DV, BPSS)
- List X registration for protectively marked contracts
- Defence-specific indemnities (Official Secrets Act breaches)
- UK content requirements may apply
- Long procurement cycles (12-24 months)

### NHS/Health
- NHS Standard Contract variants
- Clinical safety requirements (DCB0129, DCB0160)
- Data Protection Impact Assessments for patient data
- Integration with national programmes (Spine, NRL)
- Payment mechanisms tied to NHS financial years

### HMRC/Treasury
- Higher security requirements (often SC minimum)
- Tax affairs scrutiny more intensive
- IR35 compliance requirements for contract staff
- Specific audit rights (NAO, PAC)
- Data sovereignty particularly sensitive (taxpayer data)

### Home Office
- Counter-terrorism checks
- Vetting for immigration/police data
- International data transfers particularly restricted
- Biometric data special categories
- Public protection obligations

## NEC Contract Specifics

NEC contracts use fundamentally different structure from standard commercial contracts:

- **Compensation Events**: Variation mechanism very different from change control
- **Early Warning**: Proactive obligation to notify risks
- **Programme**: Delivery schedule is contract document
- **Key Performance Indicators**: Often linked to payment
- **Collaborative approach**: Good faith obligations more explicit

**IBM Position on NEC**: Acceptable framework but requires specialist input from Legal and Commercial teams with NEC experience. Payment mechanisms must not create unlimited downside risk.

## Risk Classification - Departmental Specific

**RED FLAGS for Departmental**
- Unlimited indemnities for department-specific risks (clinical harm, security breaches, data incidents)
- IP ownership extending to IBM background IP and tools
- Security clearance requirements for entire delivery team (cost/timeline prohibitive)
- NEC pain/gain share without caps on pain
- Open-ended TUPE obligations with undefined workforce
- Data processing terms incompatible with IBM global architecture
- Academic IP commercialisation rights without IBM revenue share
- Payment terms >90 days (despite government policy of 30 days)

**AMBER for Departmental**
- Sector-specific insurance requirements above IBM standard
- Elevated liability caps for safety/security critical systems (assess per contract value)
- Complex IP arrangements requiring case-by-case assessment
- TUPE with indicative numbers requiring due diligence
- Security clearances for named individuals (manageable with planning)
- Department-specific performance regimes not previously encountered
- Bespoke exit obligations requiring operational assessment

## Departmental Deep-Dive Requirements

For high-value or complex departmental procurements, conduct deep-dive including:

1. **Precedent review**: IBM's previous contracts with this department
2. **Framework comparison**: How does this differ from equivalent CCS framework?
3. **Peer benchmarking**: What terms have competitors accepted?
4. **Technical-legal alignment**: Do technical reqs create legal exposure?
5. **IBM sector expertise**: Engage relevant IBM sector legal specialists

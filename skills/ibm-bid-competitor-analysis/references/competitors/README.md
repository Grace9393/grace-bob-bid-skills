## How to Use These Profiles

When conducting competitor analysis for a bid:

1. **Identify likely competitors** based on:
   - Named competitors in RFP/ITT documents
   - Incumbent relationships mentioned
   - Deal size, complexity, and geography
   - Client industry and procurement route

2. **Load relevant profiles** to understand:
   - Typical positioning and messaging
   - Commercial model preferences
   - Pricing patterns by deal type and geography
   - Delivery location strategies
   - Negotiation behaviors
   - Strengths and vulnerabilities

3. **Apply to your analysis**:
   - Predict likely attack lines against IBM
   - Identify where competitors will be strong/weak
   - Develop counter-positioning strategies
   - Inform pricing and commercial decisions
   - Plan response surgery and proof point insertion

## Profile Structure

Each profile covers:
- **Positioning & Messaging** - How they present themselves
- **Commercial Approach** - Pricing models, negotiation style, rate patterns
- **Delivery Strategy** - Location mix, resource deployment, nearshore/offshore patterns
- **Deal Behavior** - Observed patterns in different opportunity types
- **Strengths & Vulnerabilities** - Where they excel and where they're exposed

## Evidence Base

These profiles are compiled from:
- Competitive intelligence from past bids
- Market observation and deal debriefs
- Public information about capabilities and presence
- Pricing benchmarks and commercial patterns

**Important**: Always distinguish between evidenced facts, reasonable inferences, and assumptions when using these profiles in bid analysis.

## Maintenance

Profiles should be updated as new competitive intelligence emerges from:
- Recent bid outcomes and debriefs
- Market moves (acquisitions, partnerships, new offerings)
- Client feedback and relationship intelligence
- Public announcements and analyst reports

How we’ll identify Customer Enablement customer needs 
We’ll use IBM’s Enterprise Design Thinking (EDT) framework to identify your customers’ needs. You’ve already adopted this framework, and we’ve helped many of your team gain EDT certification. We’ve included a summary of the main features of EDT in our response to 6.12. 
 
EDT is conceived to place your customers at the heart of everything we do by focusing on the outcomes that our actions and solution choices create. To own and progress the knowledge we identify for each application, we will ask you to appoint a Product Owner who will become specialist in your customers’ needs and will already be experienced in the use of EDT. 
 
To extend this customer focus further, we recommend working with you to identify Sponsor Users for each end to end service. These will be active participants that will work with us, alongside our delivery teams to help us deliver outcomes that meet their needs. We’ve started this process already by talking to some of your customers who work with us as IBM interns as they complete their student activities1. 
 
We think understanding your customers is an essential pre-requisite to understanding their needs. Our EDT method includes a two-stage process to gain this insight: 
- firstly, it is important to make sure that we know who your customers are as this may vary from application to application. For example, a potential student applying for a loan is an easily identified customer but there are others too such as the casework officer who must understand the legislative framework applicable to a decision to grant a loan or not. We’ll work with you to build on the personas that you have already given to us to capture the customers applicable to each application, and
- secondly, we’ll create customer empathy maps. These maps will rapidly put our teams in your customers’ shoes and align on pain points. In our experience, empathy is gained from the collaborative creation of the map and we would make sure that the Sponsor Users were active participants in this process.
 
With clear insight into who your customers are, we would then proceed to create ‘As Is’ and ‘To Be’ scenario maps for all the ways that they interact with the service. These maps are used for different purposes: 
- the ‘As Is’ scenario maps document collective understanding of user workflows and are best used as precursors to exploring new ideas, and
- the ‘To Be’ scenario maps tell the story of a better experience for your customers. It is these stories that will help us identify their needs2.
The gap between ‘As Is’ and ‘To Be’ process maps is essentially the definition of your customer’s future needs. The capabilities needed to close this gap will be documented as needs statements. An example of a needs statement for SLC could be, “the student needs a way of accessing their loan balance so they can understand how different payments affect how long it will take them to pay back their loan”. These needs statements will be placed into the application’s backlog so they can be considered within the overarching programme planning process (see our response to 6.2). 
 
When we consider the ‘To Be’, we will be sure to include accessibility requirements and other relevant standards such as GDS’ gov.uk design kits. For Defra, IBM delivered applications to Level AA of the W3C WCAG 2.0 standard. For DBS, our award-winning live service was the first ever to use the Gov APIs: Notify, Verify and Pay GOV.UK together. We have passed c15 Alpha/Beta GDS service assessments. Our solutions make use of the GDS Design System including patterns, toolkits, styles and components. 
 
Lastly, we’ve found that it’s important to consolidate all the activities described above into something that customers, stakeholders and delivery teams can easily understand, essentially to ‘keep them on the same page’. To realise this goal, we will create blueprints which communicate ideas and scenarios visually by telling user-centric stories. They create a narrative that focuses on people and their actions, thoughts, goals, emotions, and relationships. Storyboards are very visual and easily understood by everybody - some of our previously created storyboards now live on the walls of CEO offices and communal spaces used by Garage teams. 
 
 
Notes
- We asked our IBM interns, your customers, about their student loan pain points. They told us they would like:
- increased engagement through mobile channels
- greater visibility of repayment plans, and
- easier access to their loan balance.
- Working with Scottish Government, we delivered 7 services in 2 years and engaged with over 1350 users and obtained a 97% customer satisfaction rating.

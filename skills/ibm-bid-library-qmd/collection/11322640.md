We’ll support your Equality, Diversity & Inclusion (EDI) objectives, aligning to the Public Sector Equality Duty. 
 
Our approach to identifying and managing equality impacts 
 
Our approach under this contract aligns to IBM’s four strategic EDI priorities:
 
- Employee experience – We champion all diverse communities of IBMers and support every employee to thrive and bring their authentic selves to work. In-person workshops during mobilisation will be hosted in our inclusive London office which has all-gender bathrooms, reflection/prayer rooms, lactation rooms, increased aisle widths/turning radius, and tonal contrast for material.
- Advocacy – We work to drive systemic change that creates opportunity for diverse communities. We have Sustainability and Social Value (SSV) champions on the Defra account, promoting EDI initiatives.
- Allyship – We provide training and support to help every IBMer be an upstander through inclusive behaviours. For this contract, we’ll provide mandatory EDI training during onboarding, detailed later in this response.
- Accountability – We harness data transparency to enable accountability, action and outcomes for increased diversity representation and inclusion at every level. Our leadership team will publish their EDI goals annually to our team and yours
 
 
 
 
The processes to identifying equality impacts and how any negative impacts will be mitigated and opportunities realised
 
 
EDI will be a key agenda item in the mobilisation workshops, providing an opportunity for your team to meet our existing account’s SSV Lead, Alice Cantelo.
 
We’ll run an anonymous EDI survey, to feedback and identify EDI impacts and identify action areas to address negative impacts. In 2023, this initiative led us to recognise the necessity for more regular opportunities for our team to provide feedback and recognition. As a result, we established our monthly account team 'temperature check,' with the results being reviewed by our leadership team.
 
We’ll focus specifically on enabling accessibility of digital solutions by adhering to applicable accessibility guidelines and principles. ServiceNow’s 2024 accessibility roadmap outlines how they plan to achieve higher levels of Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) compliance across their products.
 
As well as complying with required legislation, we’ll provide our team with relevant tools and education to help them identify potential equality impacts including:
 
- ‘Advancing Accessibility’ training and accreditation, and
- IBM Accessibility Central (collection of tools and resources to better understand and deliver accessible solutions).
 
 
These resources mean our team will have the skills and tools to embed accessibility conversations and approaches throughout delivery:
 
- we’ll meet with your accessibility champions, during mobilisation workshops, in which we’ll discuss any specific accessibility requirements for each stage (eg testing, implementation etc)
- we’ll also share with you and agree any required accessibility features in ServiceNow (eg accessibility mode, high contrast theme, screen reader compatibility, etc), meaning the solution will be accessible for all users
- we’ll provide documentation in electronic formats supporting compatibility with screen readers (eg alternative text for images) meaning equitable experiences for all stakeholders and users, and
- we’ll offer accessible participation in meetings (eg closed captions, automated transcription, voice commands) during virtual meetings over Webex.
 
We’re committed to using inclusive language, as defined by the Equality Act, and you’ll benefit from our team’s access to relevant tools, standards, and expertise, including:
 
- IBM’s Inclusive Language Guide, which removes biased language. For this contract, for example, we can use this when sending out workshop invite agenda, ensuring that the content is inclusive for all potential attendees
- Business Conduct Guidelines (BCG) training, which all our team members will be required to complete annually. Modules include mitigating unconscious bias, empathy building, and using inclusive language
- our EDI events such as Words Matter Seminars, which we’ll invite our blended team members to, intended to drive thoughtful change toward inclusive language in technology, and
IBM Terminology database, which our team will have access to, holds 150,000+ terms (figure C2.1), providing recommendations on use and alternate suggestions

Additionally, ServiceNow’s platform is regularly updated to reflect best practice (eg a recent enhancement replaced non-inclusive terms such as “Master” and “Blacklist” with inclusive terminology, “primary” and “denylist”).
 
How we’ll work with Defra’s accessibility champions to ensure your ServiceNow instance is accessible
 
Our SSV Lead will work with your accessibility champions to enable your accessible ServiceNow solution. We’ll do this by:

 
- collaborating with your accessibility champions in the 'SSV activation session’ during mobilisation to understand accessibility considerations and opportunities for the MVP delivery
- configuring the product to ensure compliance with accessibility outcomes, and
- running a demo of ServiceNow’s accessibility features, including capabilities for the MVP and opportunities to enhance this over time.
 
How we’ll ensure that all staff working on this contract are skilled in recognising and addressing potential equality impacts
 
As well as the specific accessibility and inclusive language education and resources outlined above, we’re committed to skilling our team in recognising and addressing equality impacts. For example:
 
- require managers responsible for hiring to complete IBM’s ‘Licence to Hire’ training, gaining an accredited badge upon completion
- we’ll provide our IBM team members access to our Defra account’s dedicated, and regularly refreshed, Equality, Diversity, and Inclusion learning channel(figure C2.2), and
- we’ll provide our team with EDI expertise and mechanisms to raise concerns, making them better equipped to recognise and address equality impacts, eg:
- TalkItOver@IBM: provides IBMers with access to raise concerns with a dedicated team of HR professionals whose role is to listen, provide guidance and options on next steps, and
- IBM Employee Concerns: a mechanism for IBMers to formally, and confidentially, raise concerns about non-inclusive or inappropriate behaviours, BCG violations or give a point of view about broader IBM policies and practices

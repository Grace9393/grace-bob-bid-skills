T6.1.1. Our overall approach to the statement of work 
SOW 1 requires a multidisciplinary team to manage, scale, and enhance Digital Health Checks (DHC) using Tier3 DevOps. Our goal is to increase uptake, improve health outcomes, and reduce inequalities by delivering a reliable, scalable service. We'll leverage our experience from Digital Screening, NHS.uk, NHS App, FDP, focusing on innovation, user experience, interoperability, cost reduction, and compliance with Medical Device Qualification standards. To deliver SOW 1, we'll: 
- Leverage collective expertise and capability across IBM, BJSS, AxioLogik, Carnall Farrar, Rayo and Huma, to deploy highly skilled individuals experienced in Prevention and related services, to deliver value from day one.  
- Tailor our successful transition approach to keep DHC running smoothly and instil an effective ‘we build it, we run it’ DevOps culture. 
- Work closely with the DHC Senior Leadership Team, leveraging established approaches, relationships and innovations from other NHS portfolios and industries to optimise transition and innovate for product delivery. 
- Drive high performance and best practice approaches aligned to your standards, delivering to GDS/NHS Service Standards, Clinical Safety and Medical Device standards, with assurance embedded into our ways of working.  
- Follow best practice User Centred and Product-Led methodologies, meeting your adoption targets by involving end-users continuously, creating a comprehensive and quantified service blueprint for the end-to-end service. 
- Continuously measure & report on key success metrics (user/GP/clinical acceptability, cost, time to deliver), linked to our Value Measurement framework. 
We’ll bring together relevant proposal elements to support milestones:  
For MS01 – Mobilisation, we will: 
- Implement 3-week pre-mobilisation activity to identify and deploy resources, conduct training and knowledge transfer, establish transition responsibilities including document creation/upkeep, to deliver value from day one. 
- Collaborate to finalise the team structure so we can resource immediately. 
- Develop 8-week transition plan, to be agreed within 2 weeks of SoW signature. 
- Deploy SMEs to evaluate, safely transition and maintain accreditations such as accessibility and medical device certifications during transition. 
For MS02 – Complete Onboarding, we will:  
- Empower an experienced Transition Lead, to work with you to establish governance & reporting, and manage the transition plan. They’ll follow a structured Plan-Learn-Perform method to shadow and reverse-shadow exiting resources, evaluate (e.g. Software Engineering quality framework) and address knowledge gaps to safely transition DHC and play back, 8 weeks after SOW signing. 
- Run an engineering assessment (& Well Architected Review) to understand technical debt levels for DHC, enabling knowledge transfer from incumbent. 
- Set up onboarding sessions and bootcamps including deep dives (e.g. on assurance gates, medical device certification, and accessibility) and create a Team Charter to align rainbow teams on ways of working and team behaviours. 
- Carry out validation exercise of existing metrics and baselines for value, then establish/ iterate a value framework that aligns overall SOW objectives 
MS03/04 – Tier 3 DevOps Support, Incident Management & Resolution, we’ll: 
- Run MS03 and MS04 concurrently, to ensure robust support, proactive monitoring, and effective triage of incidents to minimise downtime & maximise quality, via DevSecOps and Site Reliability Engineering. 
- Monitor and continuously improve CI delivery process automation, CI/CD pipelines, IaC, and cloud services, to streamline issue resolution. 
- Adopt a shared run-maintain-transform backlog prioritised against our value framework, using a pre-mortem workshop to identify key risks and mitigations. 
We exemplified MS01-04 in transitioning the CSMS between BJSS and IBM where we successfully transitioned the service within 8 weeks, into Tier 3 Devops support.  
For MS05 – Service Innovation and Enhancement. DHC needs to deliver step-change in current adoption of 39%, while scaling to 738,000 checks in year 4 and potentially expanding from CVD to other conditions, against ongoing change in policy, clinical and interoperability requirements (e.g. versus wearables). We will: 
- Leverage our Strategic Leadership Team, working closely with DHSC/NHSE to identify and shape policy and clinical requirements, and identify scalable new technologies to enhance uptake and inclusion.
- Complete all work to deliver and roll out DHC nationally, following the latest healthcare interoperability standards (i.e. FHIR UK CORE 4), product-led approaches and NHS Service Manual Methods, to iterate the service journey for improved completion rates, adoption and service efficiency. 
- Prepare teams for Public Beta Assessment, using our experts in GDS and NHS standards to coach teams through mock assessments. 
- Use our value framework to gather real service data and deliver strategic, data driven improvements to increase DHC adoption. 
- Establish distinct reference groups of Trusts, GPs and Local Authorities to advise on the end-to-end service design and experience, to inform service and adoption improvements and build a culture of collaboration. 
Example: On NHS App, we identified weaknesses in the delivery lifecycle, benefits tracking and alignment of teams. We co-developed a new lifecycle informed by our experience which improved delivery reliability and enhanced benefits realisation.  
For MS06 – Accessibility Standards, we will:  
- Embed accessibility in definitions of done and ready, include accessibility training in onboarding, and provide SME support from IBM’s expert global accessibility team to exceed SOWs requirement. 
- Recruit diverse users with cognitive, motor, neurodivergence needs, to participate in research and testing, to make sure DHC works for all target users.  
- Leverage our relationship with the RNIB, involving them in user research and testing to assure accessibility approaches for users with visual impairments. 
- Embed accessibility in the user research & testing plan to ensure accessibility is prioritised, and arrange independent audits to attain WCAG status. 
- Nominate an accessibility champion in each squad to drive accessibility, and engage qualified DHSC Service Assessors to validate approaches, provide coaching and ensure alignment across DHC and DPSP. 
Example: On NHS App, we successfully delivered 300+ releases that met WCAG standards. We facilitated 4 external accessibility audits per annum to attain WCAG 2.2AA, bolstered by insights from our collaboration with RNIB. 
For MS07 – Medical Device Status: Huma’s experience of Software as Medical Device (SAMD) certification, will shape our approach to: 
- Deploy medical device specialists in the DHC team to transition, maintain and evolve the quality management system and compliance with MHRA Class 2A 
- Include tailored medical device training & content in onboarding to ensure teams are well-versed in medical device requirements and processes. 
- Manage Medical Device Certification as a separate but connected workstream to support DHSC/NHSE in navigating certification gates. 
- Identify a clinical safety and medical device champion in each squad to immerse documentation and audit compliance into squad ways of working. 
- Leverage our expertise in the NHS.uk team that wrote the NHS content on Medical Device regulations for digital services and AI, collaborating closely with NICE, MHRA, CQC, the HRA and NHS to advise and embed skills in squads. 
Example: On NHS.UK, we gained medical device status by supporting detailed quality assurance, technical assurance, and certification processes to deliver the Heart Age Tool and Child & Teenage BMI Calculator. 
T6.1.2 How the Statement of Work fits within the wider Call-Off Contract 
Strategic perspective: ECC is key to the Government’s 3 strategic shifts for the NHS, in particular Sickness to Prevention. DHC aims to complement in-person Health Checks, reducing premature mortality from England’s second biggest killer, CVD, by lowering risk of heart attack, stroke or dementia in people 40-74yrs. DHC needs to raise adoption from the current 39%, by offering a flexible, inclusive and cost-effective service. It can tackle health inequalities through inclusive design, and free up in-person capacity for those with limited digital skills and/or under-served populations. It can embed personalisation at the core of DPSP’s delivery, using NHSE’s personalisation service, behavioural science & biopsychosocial factors. 
Strategic technology perspective: This SOW will provide important opportunities to establish reuse, integration & modularisation across the DPSP portfolio, including reusing support services like appointment booking and Patient Data Management. We’re immersed in the wider Call-Off Contract’s technical environment, integrations and DHC strategy through our experience in DPS, CaaS, Notify, Patient Health Manager, MNS, FDP, CSMS and Screening.  
Metrics perspective: As the first service to move to Beta, DHC will establish an OKR measurement framework for ECC. This will help us to support the wider Call-Off by providing clear, evidenced accountability across progress and decisions made, benefits realised, and changes in adoption. 
Business Change Perspective: SOW01 requires extensive stakeholder management to achieve change across national, regional and local NHS and LA services, presenting an opportunity to forge strong relationships for future services.  
Ways of Working (WoW) perspective: As the first SOW, DHC will establish the baseline WoW for future SOWs, creating a re-usable framework for processes, roles and practices that will enable consistent collaboration across the Call-Off.  
T6.1.3 The capabilities we'll deploy on the Statement of Work
We’ve aligned our SOW01 team composition with the DPSP Pathway & Outcome team model; as early adopters of this model in Screening (IBM, BJSS), we have consulted our teams on optimisations to best deliver for DHC: our proposed DHC SOW01 team of 52 individuals will comprise of a Pathway Team (Lead Product Manager, Lead Service Designer, Senior Technical Architect, Senior User Researcher, Senior BA, Senior Delivery Manager) and three outcome teams. The Pathway Team will lead and shape activity, establishing shared governance structures, cross-service and pathway forums and feedback loops across SOWs.  
We’ll resource multidisciplinary people at various SFIA levels into outcome teams, with experience delivering outcomes for DPS (e.g. DTOS, CSMS, NHS App & Wayfinder). Our people will have the required skills across technical, delivery, UCD & data capabilities to provide Tier 3 DevOps support, deliver new, accessible, user-centred features, & ensure Medical Device Qualification. We will increase the leadership in Service Design across the pathway and cross support teams to help shape the end-to-end DHC service blueprint. These teams will be supported by our SOW2 Extended Strategy Team led by Carnall Farrar, which will support the broader strategic agenda, managing front door processes and cross-cutting initiatives; and Cross Support teams (All SOWs), which will split their time across the outcome teams and SOWs, providing flexibility and expertise in key roles.  
Our capabilities will be aligned and deliver best practice processes/methods, including: 
- GDS-aligned user-centred design: combining analytics tools, user research, CX, and UX to drive service improvements and increased service adoption.  
- Agile portfolio and programme management: using tailored scaling techniques such as Scrum of Scrums, Big Room Planning, and mid-range cycle planning activities, to inform decisions and manage complexity across SoWs.  
- Product-led agile: establishing a clear product vision, prioritising epics, and delivering measurable value using OKRs and data-driven insights.  
- DevSecOps: adopting a 'we build it, we run it' mentality, using automation, observability, and CI/CD tools to ensure a consistent, reliable, & scalable DHC. 
Example: We employ a similar model to Screening, to create multidisciplinary teams that solve E2E problems and meet your goals to improve DevOps release cycles, reduce siloes, successfully scale to a national service, and progress through assurance gates (GDS assessments / medical device certification) at pace. 
T6.1.4 Outline timescales / plans on how the capabilities will be deployed. 
For SOW 1, we’ll deploy all teams and capabilities within 4 weeks, maximising time with the incumbent and reducing the risk of knowledge gaps. The plan below details our approach for DHC, including a breakdown of the 3 months prior to BAU. Post 22/12 (current end of plan), teams will continue delivering DHC outcomes, working to 2-week sprint for maximum flexibility.
Preloading team mobilised, which will consist of Transition and resourcing leaders, from the 1st of September following contract award to kick start our mobilisation.  
Leadership roles will deploy into Pathways teams before the SOW begins. Conducting Lead and Shape activity throughout preloading and transition, to investigate as-is and establish to-be operating models, processes and ways of working. They’ll collaborate with outcome teams, define work priorities and shape improvements. 
Outcome team roles (covering technical, delivery, UCD and data capabilities) will deploy into DHC outcome teams, applying detail to plans, deep diving into technology and processes, mitigating risk, reporting progress and undertaking transition activities. They will learn and understand the products quickly, shadowing incumbent operations to prepare for reverse shadowing in week 4. 
Processes & Methods: recognising existing ways of working, we will work with you to identify those that remain or where new ways of working will better deliver the services. We will identify these during preloading.  
T6.1.5 Challenges, opportunities, mitigations & innovations for SoW delivery. 
Opportunities for delivery: (1) New cross-cutting/platform capabilities across ECC, starting with DHC, creating a more cost-effective, joined up DPS predicated on reuse. For example, further leveraging the CaaS to provide an initial patient cohort with fine grained filtering for eligibility against business rules for the invitation processor (2) Moving to Beta will enable focus on efficiency, adoption, cost reduction and cross-cutting service optimisation. NHS App reduced infrastructure costs by >30% (£38,000/month).
Opportunities for innovation: (1) Leverage the approach used for the HPV self-sampling PoC and GenAI to personalise DHC touchpoints for greater adoption in under-represented user groups, increased engagement and improved health outcomes. (2) Facilitate annual Futures Thinking workshop, scanning tech horizon for innovation roadmaps, e.g. remote biometric testing on blood pressure/wearables. We do this on the App and with DPSP directorate, hosting innovation days for personalisation and embedding new technology. (3) Leverage new tech (e.g. Healthtech integrations via integration layer), to conduct low-touch DHCs regularly and enable DHC expansion, reducing NHS run costs 
Potential delivery challenges: (1) Achieving adoption and national rollout in complex NHS and LA landscapes. Mitigation: Build relationships and user community to co-design and deliver successful business change. We'll draw on our experience from the Food Standards Agency and NHS Wayfinder, where we successfully rolled out digital services to 387 LAs and 100+ trusts, respectively. (2) Medical device status impacting timelines and ways of working. Mitigation: Work with Huma’s experts to embed medical device assurance, documentation and reviews into the delivery plan to avoid delays. We'll draw from experience creating NHSE’s COVID-19 Clinical Risk Assessment tool, where we developed clinical safeguarding, risk & quality management (ISO 13485:2016, DCB0129/160, IEC 62304:2006/Amd 1:2015) and achieved MHRA class 1 medical device status.  
T6.1.6 Priced assumptions we’ve made in regard to the overall response. 
(1) Incumbent has produced complete documentation and spends necessary time with us according to transition plan/timelines. (2) Gold/silver standard services have good documentation, and incumbent has required knowledge transfer assets and schedules to support shadowing. (3) Buyer will support pre-mobilisation efforts for seamless transition and quicker value. (4) Our leadership group will have access to the right forums to share experiences and provide thought leadership across DPSP.

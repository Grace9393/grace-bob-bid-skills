To deliver DCFH, we need to support Health & Social Care data connection and interoperability. We will protect and manage Sensitive Personal Data (SPD) by:
•    Implementing Architectural “Privacy by Design” Principles, using a layered architecture
•    Automating deployment, data pipelines, security monitoring, and testing using synthetic data
•    Integrating a robust Data Management Strategy into a comprehensive Security Management Plan
•    Implementing a proven threat management solution to detect cyber-threats and respond to security breaches and cyber-attacks
•    Continuously improving processes and procedures based on best practices and lessons learnt
We will apply appropriate controls by:
•    Governing and managing data from creation to destruction
•    Implementing GDPR-compliant data protection policies on data classification, access controls, encryption standards, and incident response protocols
•    Conducting audits/risk assessments across the data life cycle, implementing controls for each dataset commensurate with the level of risk
•    Tagging data with additional metadata to facilitate access controls
We will ensure appropriate access by:
•    Ensuring that only authorised users can access SPD through role-based access controls, Multi Factor Authentication, and regular reviews of user permissions 
•    Implementing data access risk mitigation strategies
We will store SPD securely by:
•    Encrypting data at-rest and in-transit, with SPD only decrypted when needed
•    Implementing policies for securely transferring data, on-premise or in the cloud
•    Implementing and testing Data Backup & Disaster Recovery capabilities
IBM is ISO27001 & HIPAA certified and we’ve extensive experience protecting SPD, in accordance with regulations such as the Data Protection Act and GDPR. We have built and operated critical systems that house SPD such as the NHS App, using the approach described above, and we protect the SPD of hundreds of clients who have outsourced operations to IBM.
 
We will use our proven, NCSC-reviewed Security-by-Design (SbD) method to safeguard IBM and Buyer systems and information assets against cyber-attacks, unauthorised access and data leaks, through:
•    Robust Governance to proactively prevent cyberattacks: Creating a dedicated Security Executive, with clear guidelines and processes for security implementation, aligned with relevant standards and regulations, e.g. NIST/NCSC
•    Embedding cybersecurity resources in outcome teams
•    ‘Shift-left’ security: Building in security from the start of development, e.g. threat modelling, risk assessments and secure coding practices
The robust measures and procedures we will implement include:
•    Deploying best practice security controls such as Attack Surface Management tools to provide a layered security approach
•    Automated testing: Implementing DevOps pipelines that continuously scan code for secrets and other vulnerabilities 
•    Continuous security monitoring and testing with real-time analysis to promote ongoing security
•    Regular software updates to prevent cyber-attacks exploiting vulnerabilities in outdated software
•    Using multi-factor authentication to prevent a stolen username/password from providing access to a system
•    Ensuring email systems are secure to prevent malware being delivered through email attachments
•    Implementing endpoint security on all devices, including anti-malware software, application controls and hard disk encryption
•    Ensuring regular backups to provide a recovery route in the event of a ransomware attack
•    Continuously monitoring and re-authenticating users and devices, though a ‘Zero Trust’ security model
Our approach is critical given DCFH’s goal to connect Health & Social Care Data and ensure interoperability. We are the cybersecurity supplier-of-choice in NHSE Cyber Operations, and we secure the NHS App where we identified and eliminated 40 vulnerabilities in live service and improved the Azure ‘SecureScore’ from 26% to 60%.
 
To enable data exchange and functionality across systems, including Health & Social Care settings, our integration strategy will focus on robust technical interoperability solutions ensuring our data and functionality exchange solutions fully support user requirements, facilitate re-use and minimise technical debt. They will be delivered using a GDS-aligned agile user-centric delivery model to ensure knowledge sharing and skillset transfer. We will build ‘Interoperability by Design’ into our development and support activities (such as service requests and incident resolution) and use industry standards like HL7, SNOMED and FHIR to facilitate seamless data and functionality exchange across systems.
We will employ APIs and middleware solutions to ensure the secure and effective exchange of data. Where necessary, we will implement secure web and mobile app integrations patterns, like we did for the NHS App.
This approach will improve data accuracy, enabling more informed decision making and improving quality of care.
To enable effective data and functionality exchange, we will deploy architectural patterns and other assets to accelerate delivery velocity and reduce risk. We will develop, test, assure and operate the most appropriate technical solutions to meet requirements for each Call-Off contract, e.g:
•    A common registry for data functions
•    Consolidating diverse systems onto a central platform
•    REST-APIs for exposing data and functionality
•    Data virtualisation
•    Message buses
•    NLP using Gen AI chatbots to generate queries and interrogate multiple systems 
•    Secure web portals for data access
•    Security Frameworks using SAML, OAUTH, etc
We deployed this approach for the COVID-19 Testing Service, building a cloud-hosted API-based integration platform, with a range of integrations to other NHS systems. We dramatically reduced test-result delivery times and greatly improved system reliability despite frequent updates due to short-notice policy updates.
We have a proven track record working with partners such as NHS England, UKHSA, Genomics England and their diverse stakeholders to deliver user-centred digital services. We will do this for our DCFH Health and Social Care partners by:
-    Taking an Agile, iterative approach, following the Agile phases as outlined in the GDS Service Manual
-    Running collaborative workshops with partners to establish shared goals and key performance metrics
-    Using our expertise in User Research and User Centred Design to gain deeper understanding of their motivations, pain points and needs
-    Designing prototypes based on needs identified, and testing early to gain feedback
-    Working closely with partners to prioritise backlog items for delivery
-    Promoting an open and collaborative culture, ensuring a continuous feedback loop and holding regular playback sessions
We will ensure our digital offerings overall continuously align with GDS and NHS Service Standards by:
-    Putting user needs at the heart of products through our research led approach, like the inclusive and accessibility strategies implemented by our NHS App teams
-    Designing for all users through adhering to the WCAG 2.2 AA accessibility standard
-    Understanding how digital solutions fit in the wider health/social care landscape and touchpoints between services and solutions
-    Building cross functional teams educated on standards, who champion them
-    Incorporating mock GDS assessments into our lifecycle, with our DHSC-trained Service Assessors, who also coach teams on best practice and meeting the agreed ‘definition of done’
-    Leveraging our wealth of knowledge from successfully delivering 15+ GDS compliant projects to address challenges and build on lessons learnt, through knowledge transfer sessions and buddying schemes
-    Developing close relationships with Clinicians and Clinical Safety Officers to manage risks and ensure compliance with regulatory standards, as demonstrated by our NHS App teams

IBM recognises that this service will be delivered in a fluid policy change environment, with people within DHSC and wider stakeholder positions who may be moving in and out of roles frequently to adapt to this change. Enabling real-time transfer of knowledge is essential to the success of this service delivery, and therefore it must be embedded in everything we do, day in and day out. An exit strategy is the final component to the continuous transfer of knowledge. The key to the success of this model is a close partnership between DHSC and IBM (internal & supply-chain resources) working as a ‘badge-less’ rainbow team. In this answer, we share:
 
IBM’s credibility
 
Transfer of knowledge and skills during the contract
 
Transfer of knowledge and skills at the end of the contract and exit strategy
 
 
IBM’s credibility
 
IBM have partnered with the DHSC to deliver NHS Test and Trace (T&T) services (e.g. International Arrivals and Enquiries, Feedback and Complaints) over the past year. IBM’s DHSC Test and Trace team have shared their experience to enable us to create plans for the transfer of knowledge and skills during, and at the end of the contract. Due to the fluid policy change environment, over the past year we have worked with you to create an approach to continuously transfer knowledge and skills which can flex and adapt to a rapidly changing environment. Additionally, we have continuously worked together to assess the readiness of the receiving parties and regularly measured the success of transfer to mitigate any knowledge and skills transfer risks. A particular example of this is with the knowledge transfer from delivery to support teams as part of our weekly releases into production.
 
 
Transfer of knowledge and skills during the contract
 
Establishing principles at the beginning of our engagement will be fundamental to ensuring that IBM and DHSC have a clear view of what knowledge and skills must, and will, be transferred. We recommend the principles should include:
 
Transfer of knowledge and skills should be embedded in all plans to enable real-time transfer.
 
Each activity and tool used across the delivery should have an individual knowledge transfer plan.
 
Transition represents an opportunity for the receiving party to learn from IBM over a fixed period of time.
 
Transition acceptance occurs at the agreed date, with ownership moving to the receiving party away from IBM (unless otherwise agreed).
 
 
We have already begun the knowledge and skills transfer process, with insights and approaches being gathered from the IBM DHSC Test and Trace team. An effective plan to transfer knowledge and skills during the contract will require several continuous activities throughout the service delivery, which combines technology, culture, measurement, and an infrastructure to share across teams and wider stakeholders. Below we define what continuous activities should be included in the plan, and how they will be achieved.
 
 
Due diligence
 
With you, we will carry out due diligence to ensure that our plans have a realistic view of how, and with who, we should be carrying out real-time knowledge transfer during the service delivery. We will achieve this through:
 
Agreeing the scope of what knowledge and skills must be transferred.
 
Understanding the availability of receiving resources to receive the knowledge and skills transfer.
 
Identifying and engaging key stakeholders.
 
Establishing a shared approach to knowledge management to enable ease of accessing and storing documentation.
 
Creating and updating a RAID Log which will be monitored and updated consistently through the approach mapped out below in the Transfer of Skills and Knowledge Governance approach.
 
 
Transfer of skills and knowledge planning
 
With you, we will co-create and continuously assess a plan which embeds real-time transfer of knowledge and skills into the delivery of the service. We will approach this by:
 
IBM have created a draft plan, through a pre-mobilisation transfer of skills and knowledge with the IBM DHSC Test and Trace team.
 
During week one of the service delivery, IBM and DHSC should review and update the plan to reflect the needs of the team and wider stakeholders.
 
Governing and updating the plan if required due to the nature of the fluid policy change environment.
 
 
Human centred approaches to knowledge and skills transfer
 
We will combine both the practical with the personal to enable the team to share and received knowledge and skills. To successfully transfer knowledge and skills, different approaches and methods may need to be amended to suit the needs of the team and wider stakeholders. A combination of these activities will allow for successful real-time knowledge transfer throughout:
 
A social contract to agree shared ways of working.
 
Playbacks and showcases across teams and wider stakeholders to align the product roadmap and backlogs.
 
Lunch and learns sessions on topics such as: HS APP, NHS.UK, Inclusive Service (119), Verifier App (Scanner).
 
Townhalls to share programme updates, and content relevant to all the team such as User Research findings and best practices.
 
Shadowing and reverse shadowing to transfer personal or experiential knowledge from one person to another.
 
Coaching from SME’s, on topics such as IBM Garage, Agile, Service design, Enterprise Design Thinking, GDS Standards, and tooling e.g. Jira.
 
Support and training on wellbeing best practices, such as resilience and mental health to ensure the team have the support required to deliver successfully and safely in the critical service delivery environment.
 
 
Transfer of knowledge and skills governance
 
Knowledge transfer decision points: At the beginning of the programme we will agree a series of decision points to assess the completeness of the knowledge transfer by the receiving party.
 
Establish KPI’s: we will work with you to establish the KPIs we will use to measure the continuous success of knowledge and skills transfer during delivery. This can include measures such as number of artefacts created, time spent in documentation, or an Agile maturity assessment.
 
Weekly programme management meeting: will be used to govern and review the success of the delivery of the plan through analysis of the KPIs and RAID log updates. This will also be the forum used to review and update the plan on a weekly basis.
 
Agile ceremonies: knowledge and Skills transfer will be embedded at the core of our delivery. Activities agreed in the plan will be brought into sprint, and will be managed through Sprint Planning, Backlog Refinement. Risks and issues can be raised, resolved or escalated in daily stand ups.
 
 
Transfer of Knowledge and Skills at the End of the Contract
 
These activities to transfer knowledge and skills during the contract, lead us to a final cutover process at the end of the contract. In the final stage of transfer, IBM and DHSC will have a clear handover approach, which will include handover documentation or a walkthrough on knowledge and skills such as:
 
Programme level: governance model, delivery model, cadence model, and programme risks and issues
 
Tooling
 
Service Design Blueprints, Pain Point Tracker and Product roadmaps
 
Demand Management process
 
Programme and scope change process
 
Current stakeholder map
 
Relevant work products at time of handover
 
With you, IBM will work on an exit plan that set out our exit management strategy to ensure a smooth transition. The plan will include a list of key personnel and staff that worked on the service; a program for the transfer process to be followed with DHSC (and a potential successor supplier); a communications plan to support the transition; a plan to ensure IBM provides sufficient coverage and support to DHSC for a set amount of time after the transfer if required.

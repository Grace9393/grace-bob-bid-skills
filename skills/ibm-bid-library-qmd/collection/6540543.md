A good roadmap should establish the intent at the beginning of the data transformation in an actionable roadmap that is scaled over a sustained period.  
Frequent realised value should be included through an optimised, governed, and measured roadmap, showing a clear pathway and identifying anticipated challenges with proposed solutions that are aligned to the technical and business objectives.  
Alignment to current investment decisions and prioritisation will sit at the core of a good roadmap, looking at the technical readiness and business commitments, and with a repeatable decision-making framework established that continues to reassess to maintain focus on value delivery.  
Other factors should include integration across the different functional data dimensions/capabilities, interdependencies, agreed milestones, defined critical path analysis, an understanding of internal and external constraints, skills enablement and development and adoption of new processes and roles. 
Intrinsically, we believe this, plus, a holistic stakeholder approach with well-defined success metrics is at the heart of good roadmap planning, along with establishing a phased ‘wave’ approach to where the data capabilities can continue to build on each other to achieve the future state. Every roadmap should include a mix of approval measures, i.e. success criteria, reviews/stage gates, QA review before releases to production etc. 
For this contract, the challenge will be how to balance the need to add new or change existing databases to address customer (and supplier) pressures whilst changing the underlying technology and processes; very much like changing the wheel of a car whilst driving down the motorway to an important meeting. 
However, if it is possible to clearly define the new technology and process strategy that underpins the data ingestion task, it will be possible to instantiate parts of that strategy as we go.

Establishing clear roles & responsibilities for the full team is crucial to avoid single points of failure, ensuring there are peers with appropriate skills who can cross cover as needed.  
Succession planning also helps guard against single points of failure. The IBM team structure includes a great cross section of experience, with more junior resources stepping up to provide support within or across teams when required (e.g., if someone is off ill), gaining valuable experience. We minimise dependency on individuals’ knowledge through our T-shaped skills team composition (which is outlined further below); squad ways of working (agile ceremonies); IBM Strategic Sponsor oversight; pair programmes; succession plans; escalation processes; and transparent tooling (e.g., JIRA). In NHS Test and Trace we successfully applied mentor and pair programmes which helped employees to learn on the job, transfer knowledge and increase job satisfaction. 
The Cross Functional (Rainbow) team model Our proposed team structure, allows us to pull from a pool of resources, helps to minimise the risk of single points of failure as other team members can pick up the work if needed. In addition, IBM fosters a culture of continual development and responsibility to others; this includes teaching your job to the person below you, and learning the job of the person above you, both to develop and enhance skills across the board and to provide cover if needed.  
DevOps, Automation and Pipeline management to utilise technology effectively. Use of automated Deployment Pipelines (on NHS Test and Trace we used Bitbucket and later Bamboo tooling) allows for regular deployments to production to respond at pace to change. This provides a mechanism for a wider number of people to execute tests and deploy without specific in-depth DevOps or tool experience. For resilience, multiple members within a given team should have a suitable level of access to tooling and repositories.

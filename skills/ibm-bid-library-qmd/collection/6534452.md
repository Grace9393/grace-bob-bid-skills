From our experience working with the NHS, other large government organisations and private companies, we have identified what challenges we believe are core to delivering user-centred design in the NHS: 
Capacity - Getting access to users, especially any front-line workers may be very difficult and possibly act as a blocker to conducting research and validating designs.  
Low innovation potential - Critical legacy systems take enormous investments of attention, resources and time to maintain, drawing focus away from innovation 
Heterogenous nature of the workforce - Working at the UK-wide level means handling the complexities of over 150 trusts, each with their own procedures and architecture. 
Shifting context - As the NHS shifts from Trusts to Integrated Care Systems, we see an overhaul of EPR systems meaning the NHS is operating on constantly changing ground.  
Operating in highly regulated spaces - Operating in healthcare comes with strict rules and regulations on information governance, clinical safety and device accreditation that in our experience it requires a tremendous learning curve from our clients.  
Change fatigue - Historically, change to technology in healthcare brings about pain and mistakes as users get to grips with new systems and processes.  
Increased usage of electronic health systems - With the pandemic drastically changing peoples habits there has been an upsurge in the use of your services and therefore the burden placed on your technology. 
We will address these challenges by involving our eco-system's team of industry experts to consult with design teams who’ll benefit from their many decades of experience in this space, help with the recruitment of users for research activities and facilitate stakeholders relationships at the highest levels as the new structure of the NHS emerges post-pandemic. Our designers will work closely with your technical teams to understand the impact of changes to the designs that affect backend systems, response times and more. We will use activities from IBM's Garage methodology such as value trees to understand the relative value to the users and the business of opportunities related to both maintenance of legacy systems and innovation so that your investments into innovation are reliable, predictable and provide demonstrable value quickly.

The key to ensuring a clear focus on ease of use is to adopt a user-centric approach based on real-world personas and user journeys. These personas and user journeys are established during the Discovery phase and care is taken to refine and elaborate each persona and journey as the specific user stories and requirements are established for each delivery sprint. This provides a clear linkage and “north star” for all members of the sprint team. 
Ease of operation and maintenance throughout the life of the solution can be ensured by establishing and adhering to clear architectural and design principles, covering aspects such as the correct use of tools/platforms for development; implementation of a test-driven development approach based on repeatable automated testing frameworks and adopting the correct architectural patterns. This approach will provide foundational aspects of the solution such as data/object management, user authentication and authorisation and coding standards.  
As an example, we have recently utilised both a user-centric approach and the application of clear architectural and design principles to enable Social Security Scotland to deliver their first benefit portal to launch 6 months early. This portal has a digital uptake of 90%, average application completion times of less than 5 minutes, and a 97% positive user satisfaction score for applications.  Following migration to “Run/Maintain” we would use Adobe analytics to track user behaviour, real time analytics and segmentation, reporting activity and link mapping, which will analyse if users are following the expected navigation paths for these services. This analysis will provide information to support focus areas for continual improvement in UI and user journeys.

Introduction
IBM partnered with DWP to transform the Covid impacted Apply for a National Insurance Number service, which brought the previously paper-based, clerical service to a standstill, creating a backlog of ~500,000 applications. DWP needed to re-establish the service, reduce office footfall, enable self-service, refocus staff, and achieve £108million savings over 10-years.
IBM formed a multi-disciplinary team working with DWP, rapidly onboarding experts in Agile delivery, experience design, research, development, DevOps, and business specialisms. The team re-designed the service, launching a new MVP digital service within 8-weeks to release immediate value, and deployed production enhancements building out from MVP to continuously release value every 2-weeks.
The digital transformation exceeded all targets, achieving £117million savings, 60% reduction in decision-making time and 88% customer satisfaction before fully transitioning the service to DWP by late 2023.
Prioritising User Needs and Delivering Value
Working in a joint DWP/IBM team, we collaborated across operations, policy, and digital teams to define an MVP that could quickly restart the service whilst delivering value within existing constraints. We created a product roadmap outlining the longer-term plan for unlocking value iteratively by understanding evolving citizen/agent needs, shaping solution ideas, sizing work, planning and tightly managing all dependencies. We prioritised the backlog using product management techniques, like the value/feasibility matrix with stakeholders to group features and assess the value impact against complexity for data-driven decisions. 
Every release was informed by continuous User Research and Data/Analytics shaping our approach, roadmap and priorities throughout tracking value on performance dashboards. Surveys, focus groups, usability testing, and analysis enabled deep understanding of user needs and pain-points, refining personas and journey maps to create an accessible service that meets diverse needs and aligns with the GDS Service Standard, completing Alpha, Beta, and Live assessments.
Innovation was embedded throughout, proactively seeking opportunities to drive efficiencies, automating manual tasks and improving user experience, reducing decision-making time by ~60%.

 
Engaging Stakeholders 
We adopted a multi-faceted approach to maintain strong stakeholder engagement and buy-in. Taking a one-team approach across Digital and Operations, we held daily stand-ups, fortnightly sprint planning and retrospectives and prioritisation sessions to ensure close alignment on plan, progress and risks.
For wider DWP and OGD stakeholders, fortnightly show and tells and regular workshops enabled co-creation and functionality alignment, facilitating effective implementation, tightly managing dependencies across teams to deliver shared goals. 
Managing and Mitigating Risks 
We maintained a dynamic backlog and risk/issues register, continuously reviewing priorities and risks, raising for early visibility and escalating to stakeholders when appropriate. Weekly sessions with senior leaders ensured priorities were aligned, risks/issues proactively addressed, and joint mitigation strategies developed. For example, with the Ukraine war creating a sudden influx of refugees in the UK, our agile approach enabled a new solution to be designed, built, tested and deployed within 3-days to handle sensitive applications at pace. This agile risk/issue management enabled rapid response to an evolving external landscape.
Solution Architecture, Scalability and Adaptability
Our solution was designed and built on AWS, utilising the cloud's scalability and adaptability to meet service demands. A microservices architecture, containerisation, load balancers and queues ensured high availability and scalability to support large case volumes following the service restarting.
Working with DWP Domain and Enterprise architects, we evolved the solution to modernise and remove reliance on legacy systems (e.g. CAMLite/DRS) sequentially, replacing them with new microservices. Our full-stack engineers worked with approved technologies and libraries, including DWP's CASA framework to accelerate delivery whilst ensuring maintainability. 
Engineering best practices and DevOps techniques included robust CI/CD pipelines and automated testing (including performance and accessibility) to release enhancements efficiently and reliably, as we scaled from private to public beta, with zero-downtime deployments, enabling 100+ production deployments over the engagement with 99.99%+ availability. 
We carefully balanced delivering new high-value functionality and addressing technical debt through regular backlog refinement, adopting latest technologies/frameworks where appropriate optimising service reliability.
Summary
With strong Agile and DevOps practices as a joint DWP/IBM team, we rapidly launched an MVP to iteratively build out a full GDS compliant and accessible service, achieving significant benefits for DWP. 

We delivered additional value by:
- Developing re-usable services, such as notification queues, re-used by other DWP teams.
- Proactively identified and addressed improvement areas including combatting Fraud and Error.
- Providing implementation support to operations, ensuring efficient delivery of business change.
 
This project successfully demonstrates valuable lessons that can be applied to future initiatives, including:
- Delivering a strict MVP with ongoing prioritisation 
- Embedding User-Centred Design throughout
- Fostering one-team collaboration with the wider organisation
- Embracing continuous improvement with regular releases

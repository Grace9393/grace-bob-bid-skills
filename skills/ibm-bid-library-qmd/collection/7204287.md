As outlined in our response to question 1 and based on your requirements, to work in an iterative deploy approach and include a proof of concept, we have not structured our project plan around a typical design, build, test and deploy waterfall approach. Instead, we propose three main phases over a 17-month project enabling us to work together in an iterative fashion to develop the final solution your teams require. These phases are Proof of Concept (PoC) development, Minimal Viable Product (MVP) development and solution enhancements.  
To support the overall implementation, the phases will be supplemented by additional parallel workstreams covering process validation, data transformation, infrastructure design with software installation and training.
At the end of the PoC and MVP phases, IBM are also proposing a “decision point” period to allow WPD to confirm their requirements, prioritise scope for the following phase and discuss with their business any changes they foresee are needed as priority. If the scope has deviated from the scope in this proposal, then IBM will ask to confirm our pricing or reprice the additional scope with the agreement of WPD.  This will be a mutual discussion before proceeding.

Discovery Phase
The purpose of the discovery phase is two-fold: 1) to mobilise the teams from each side and to establish common ways of working and define the end goals of the programme. 2) to finalise the scope for the PoC phase, both from a technical delivery element and a business one. Picking teams or work types that are suitable for the PoC is an example of this activity. This phase will also be used to make some key decisions on how Maximo will be configured and then trialled during the PoC.

Proof of Concept Phase
During this phase, IBM will perform the base configuration of Maximo and perform system testing only. We will also load configuration data for the target workgroups in scope for the PoC and test this configuration. IBM will create ‘dummy’ interfaces where we can take data extracts from your existing systems and push the information into Maximo through its integration framework simulating the future interface. Our functional consultants will run familiarisation sessions (show and tell training) for super users on the project. Access to the system will be provided to your project team so they can use the system to understand the system processes and capabilities.  At the end of this phase a decision point will be made on MVP functionality as outlined above.

In parallel to the PoC phase, IBM suggest running the process review phase where our process and functional consultants can work with you to validate your ‘To-be’ business process against the out of the box Maximo functionality and help you align your processes and the terminology within them. Where your new processes do not align to standard Maximo functions, we will help you evaluate the options and decide whether to adjust your process or enhance Maximo. Any changes can be fed into the MVP or Enhancement phases depending on priority. This phase is explained in further detail in question 17.

Proof of Concept (PoC) Evaluation Phase
This is one of the key stage gates in the project. It provides WPD an opportunity to review the PoC phase outputs and the work done developing Maximo plus take the out of the box solution to the wider user and stakeholder groups.  This will allow WPD to share demos and gathering feedback to ensure what has been built so far will work for them. In addition, identify any changes or new requirements can then be assessed, prioritised and taken forward into the MVP development phase. In this phase, we will partition the work for execution in sprints that will take place during the MVP phase. 
 
 
MVP Design & Build and Test Phases including E2E Testing
Once the MVP scope has been confirmed in the PoC phase, a decision point will be taken how to move forward and decide if the scope has had any resource or commercial impact.  If not, the MVP phase will complete the design, build and test of the functionality needed for the first go live and will include the build and test for the interfaces required. 
IBM proposes to run this in a series of 3-week sprints allowing a week each for design, build and test. During the build and test parts of a sprint, IBM will provide regular show and tell of the functionality which will help ensure we meet requirements.
At the end of the sprint phase, we have allowed time for WPD to complete E2E testing, which allows you to simulate ‘real-life’ business process across all the impacted systems to check they perform as expected.  If there are any major changes to the scope after each sprint, then a decision point is established again to decide how to move forward.  For example, some changes maybe business critical and nice to have scope will be moved to the enhancement phase of the project.

MVP Review
Similar to the PoC review stage, this is a stage gate in the project that allows for a review of the functionality built in the MVP and a prioritisation of requirements for the enhancement phase. A final decision point will be discussed on the scope of the enhancement phase and again IBM and WPD will discuss scope that is outside of this proposal or exchange scope to supplement new scope with existing scope elements.  The commercial and resource impact will be evaluated before moving to the next phase. 
Enhancements
This phase will focus on the design, test and build of additional ‘value add’ functionality that was not required for the release of the MVP. This is likely to include automation of workflows and processes, as well as providing additional reports and KPI development plus any new feature that have been explored during the previous phases.
We propose deploying all of this functionality into live at the end of the phase in one technical release, following UAT and regression testing. However, we could work with WPD to look at deploying items that would provide significant benefit as minor releases earlier if possible. During this phase, we will also provide hyper care for the MVP release.

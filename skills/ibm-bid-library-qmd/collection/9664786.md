A)	QA Design/Discovery
 
Fig D3.1 sets out our approach to quality through the service lifecycle. This starts with our understanding of the importance of the digital design/discovery phase to understand pain points, needs and validate configuration options based on user research. Our approach to ensure quality outputs are delivered to the Buyer includes:
•	User research: Identifying key user needs through user research with all service users. We ensure that all user types and their goals are represented.
•	Feasibility assessments: We start to prototype and validate configuration options based on user research
•	Forecasting value: We identify core value drivers through techniques like value trees to forecast value.
•	Mock service assessments: Conducting mock service assessment via peer panels, documenting decisions & reasons clearly.
•	Needs Statements: Collate a consolidated set of Needs Statements & work you to prioritise these into a set of hypotheses that can be tested during Alpha.
 
To ensure the right quality outputs, we put in place:
•	Quality measures for each activity and aligned to the overall objective
•	A multi-disciplinary team (see Fig D3.2) testing and validating options from different perspectives
•	A plan to engage the right stakeholders 
•	Independent external assessments. 
 
We demonstrated quality assured Discovery phases at Scottish Government (See fig D3.2). We implemented Discovery, aligned to the GDS service manual, ensuring we had quality measures for each activity, giving assurance of successful outcomes. We defined the overall objectives, broken down to sprint level criteria to allow assessment of outputs in each sprint. A checkpoint at every sprint assessed progress against the Discovery plan, measuring against original criteria. For the initial delivery, we assessed Discovery outputs with an external panel from Gartner. We worked with the right stakeholders to explore options so that we could develop a service that enabled Agency staff at Scottish Government to focus on high value tasks. This project won the UK MCA awards in 2020 and was a finalist in 2022.
 
B) Peer programming/configuration and life lifecycle testing
During Alpha & Beta we test solutions with users, prototyping ideas & expanding testing in a live lifecycle. Solutions are tested against business needs, requirements, use cases & user scenarios. Our live lifecycle approach maximises learning while minimising risk & disruption to users. New code is deployed alongside existing; access to new features is gradually enabled while monitoring for any issues. A/B testing, prototyping, and user research experiments are done in live.
 
Our approach is to use a combination of pair, peer, or individual programming based on the story need, identified by the team at the inception of each sprint, with a scorecard supporting selection. We demonstrated this mixed approach on our NHS Covid Response deliveries, which won the MCA 2022 Highly Commended Award. Pairing and peering optimised efficiency & quality, reduced incidents & technical debt. We used Pair programming (multiple developers working together) for stories with technical challenges, unknowns, or technical hotspots. We used Peer programming (developer and business/stakeholder working together) for complex business requirements & validating user experience. Individual programming was used for simpler fully understood stories.

C) Hypercare provision
Our approach to provide hypercare to deployed products/platforms after go-live includes:
•	A scope document that summarises what is being delivered as part of the Release and a walkthrough of the scope with key stakeholders
•	Agreed set of entry and exit criteria in line with required standards
•	Ensuring all Project documentation is held in a common repository to enable handover post hypercare
•	A resource profile for Hypercare covering all required skillsets
•	Having a joint plan with the client team with clear RACI and RAID
•	Support agreements and arrangements familiarised by all team members
•	Training sessions for the team taking over post hypercare
•	Testing any support agreements with vendors ahead of hypercare
•	Access to the common collaboration toolset for hypercare validated by all members
•	Clear escalation path for incidents with published ‘out of hours’ rotas
•	A clear route to live for defect resolution that covers functional, integration and performance testing to validate each release.
 
We demonstrated this approach for the delivery of Hypercare post go-live at Scottish Government for a Case Management solution. We devised our resource profile for Hypercare early in the project lifecycle, mapping skillsets required for the products & platforms against a skills profile. We ran entry gates to track the completion of pre-requisites in line with the standards that we had agreed with the Agency. To ensure a smooth transition post Hypercare, we held training sessions with the Live Ops teams leading up to go-live. During this hypercare period, we resolved defects found in Live, and identified enhancements to the services by establishing a feedback loop with the Product team. We proactively monitored the performance of the service to identify improvements and worked with the Product team to release the enhancements and performance improvements. The outcomes of Hypercare included a smooth go-live with zero Sev1&2 defects and successful achievement of the exit criteria in line with the client’s standards.
 
D) Service availability, optimisation & lower TCO
Designing for service availability is a key foundational aspect of our delivery. To ensure that we are delivering solutions that are following industry best practice, are scalable, maintainable & easy to support, our designs emphasise:
•	Exception handling, monitoring & alerting: in addition to handling exceptions, the right level of logging, monitoring & alerting enables rapid identification of root causes whilst maintaining availability
•	Systems & data recovery: consideration of technical design, processes, & infrastructure to rapidly restart systems or restore data and transactions from backups or secondary sources
•	Automated application management: designing solutions to automatically scale & recover on demand, manage load balancing, restart, or rollback to the previous version in case of repeated errors, & protect from denial of service & other attacks
 
We ensure that we leverage our extensive knowledge & experience from successful implementations around optimisation of our configurations. Our approach during design ensures that we are maximising OOTB components and functions; adopting configuration before customisation and reusing common components and frameworks.
 
Our approach to undertake monitoring and preventive maintenance leverages the following to identify & address issues proactively: 
•	Monitoring & alerting data APIs
•	Application and infrastructure monitoring
•	Regular anti-virus, patch, & security compliance checks
•	Vulnerability scanning 
 
We have demonstrated this approach for ‘The Disclosure and Barring Service (DBS)’. IBM delivered the digital transformation for this service, using cloud native services – all of which were configured to be resilient, highly available and auto scaling. The service received a 25% increase in user satisfaction, 3 times the number of applications and a reduction of 40% in operational and hosting costs.
 
E) Managing Risk to Live Services
Our approach to risk management for live service delivery includes:  
•	Establishing a governance process for managing risks to ensure risks (& mitigations) are transparently reviewed whilst tracking actions, progress & decisions
•	Ensuring that managing risk to live services covers fixes for live issues and the delivery of new features & enhancements
•	Validating fitness of architectural decisions to existing live services & mitigating any fitness issues.
•	Relentless focus on DevOps processes & CI-CD toolchains that can continuously assure the quality of the releases mitigating any regression risks to live services
•	Ensuring code health is continuously maintained & the quality of the releases is continuously assured leveraging test automation
•	Drawing on experiences of previous releases & in-depth root cause analysis to identify and design mitigating actions for potential threats to live e.g. regression. 
 
We have demonstrated this approach at Scottish Government where we are providing support to the Live Service, launching new services, and delivering enhancements to services in parallel. We have been rapidly iterating on a live service (providing break fixes/remediating faults) whilst maintaining and improving quality standards. To maintain and improve code health and enable these frequent deliveries we have a strong focus on Design Assurance, DevOps, CI-CD (Test Automation and deployment automation) for confidence and repeatability. We have a toolchain covering code Quality (SonarQube), Security (SonarQube), Unit Testing (JUnits), Regression Testing (Selenium) and orchestrated through Jenkins that ensures that risks to the Live services (including regression) are effectively mitigated. We also have a feedback loop into delivery enabling mitigating actions to be progressed early in the lifecycle for any recurring issues

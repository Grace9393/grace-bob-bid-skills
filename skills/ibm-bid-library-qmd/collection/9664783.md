A)	Reacting to the Buyer at pace and short notice, in the context of scaling for multiple clients/requests:
As a global consultancy, we balance demand and supply across many clients to ensure we meet their needs to commission services simultaneously and promptly.   To achieve this, we:
•	Maintain a rolling 12-month pipeline of opportunities on our ISC tool, indicating demand across our services
•	Capture roles starting in the next 6 months in IBM Marketplace, our internal staffing platform
•	Forecast demand on a rolling 12-month basis by resource profile/capability to identify high-demand skills
•	Maintain a rolling 12-month strategic resourcing plan, comprising re-skilling, hiring and our partner ecosystem of SMEs and contractors. This is supported by our Client Innovation Centres, offering technical and capability services once only available offshore. 72% of CIC work is Public Sector, so we are highly attuned to short-notice working
•	“Cluster” clients together to optimise and co-ordinate resources and communications from one place. At IBM, we have a cluster for HMRC-DWP-Scottish Government, and one for government defence agencies.
Where clients concurrently compete for resources:
•	Each Delivery Lead raises an open seat in IBM Marketplace, specifying skills required, SFIA level, and start date
•	The account’s Resource Manager (RM) matches skills against available resources, working with global RMs to source capabilities
•	Discipline Leads screen resources against experience and qualifications, conducting candidate interviews
•	If a resource is selected by two accounts, each RM works with their account to understand each scenario, the resource’s criticality, and role duration
•	If unresolved, escalation is made to Sector Leads, who are briefed on conflicting priorities and decide on assignment. A collective agreement is made regarding where the resource/s is placed, backfill, and where part-time/short term support is required between accounts.
This practice is embedded across all teams, offering scope for people to grow their experience on a programme and to rotate. We inject new skills in existing programmes and support retention through a variety of growth opportunities.
For example, a client requested a specific person from the DWP account. Having evaluated that a move would be low-impact, we agreed a roll-off plan and implemented a handover period, with the ability to reach out with questions/requests if necessary. Similarly, a Senior Delivery Manager was due to start at Scottish Government (SG). However, SG and DWP Account Leads placed them into DWP which had a greater need for specific Scaled Agile skills; a second resource with matching skills was placed into SG.
Reacting to Buyers’ requests at pace and at short notice:
HMRC’s £300m Securing our Technical Future programme (SoTF), contracted to CapGemini, Fujitsu, and Oracle, was experiencing delays, conflicts and disputes when IBM was called in. We stood up a new Delivery Excellence & Assurance (DEA) team within 4 weeks. We   partnered with HMRC, using the DEA framework to conduct workshops, validate costs, and introducing a RAID and comms process to set the project on-track. SoTF migrated 70 services to Azure, delivering all migration within one weekend, on time. It saved HMRC £18m, helping to meet Treasury approval targets, securing additional funding of c.£50m. 
B)	Driving KT to the Buyer whilst delivering the service, including methodologies, processes and tooling and enhancing the Buyer's Intelligent Client capability:
We will implement our KT process (Fig 1)   with the following iterative phases: Analyse, Plan, Train, Shadow, Reverse Shadow and Steady-state. Our approach is as follows:
•	A Training Needs Analysis, identifying capabilities and gaps
•	A focused Training Delivery Plan, mapping out topics, audiences, means of delivery, and outcomes for every aspect of the portfolio 
•	Train-the-trainer offers incremental learning and gives the confidence to train others
•	A Virtual Library captures governance decisions, design decisions, architectural designs, blueprints, data models, service specifications and more. Transition best practice, lessons learned, key decisions, and guidance are always up-to-date and available here. IBM policy and change consultants work with Buyer teams to confirm the Library approach and structure
•	Communities of Practice (CoPs), where groups of Buyer and IBM people share interests in working topics, coming together to fulfil individual and group goals
•	IBM learning on topics including Thought Leadership, Project/Agile Planning, Enterprise Design Thinking, Architectural Thinking, and Delivery
•	Shadowing and reverse shadowing, key to Intelligent Client growth, giving a human perspective to the project lifecycle
•	Learn and grow through regular sprint ceremonies and retrospectives
•	Ensure service continuity with clear and consistent standards. 
 
Enhancing Intelligent Client capability whilst delivering the service:
 
The following outcomes result from our effective knowledge transfer approach: 
 
•	Through the Training Needs Analysis, the Buyer understands the delta between the current and future state of its teams in terms of knowledge and experience absorption. This is reconciled through the Training Delivery Plan
•	The Virtual Library becomes a platform where high-quality, consistent knowledge and information is standardised, published and distributed to build collective intelligence. It is underpinned by IBM learning, building broader and deeper skills in personal and practical experience, individually and collectively
•	Collective intelligence is thematically segmented into CoPs, giving an even greater depth of practical knowledge and intelligence across client teams. Sprint events allow for further take-up of knowledge as Buyer and IBM teams deliver functionality and capability in an open, collective, Agile-led culture
•	This individual-to-team experience is reinforced through shadowing, in to directly distribute further intelligence into Buyer-side specialists as the contract evolves.
 
At the SG Social Security Directorate, we created Communities of Practice which reinforced and shared knowledge across teams. CoPs created confident, in-house teams with capabilities in analysis, low-code development, testing and DevOps. 6 such CoPs now exist, comprising over 350 people.
 
Our Virtual Library approach is used in the SG ORCI programme. The Library provides operating instructions, guidance notes and training packs using different media, facilitating complex KT with no downtime.
 
C)	Driving accessible KT towards the end of the contract, enhancing Intelligent Client capability:
 
KT empowers the Buyer to take on ownership through the following:
•	A thorough assessment of current capabilities to understand levels of knowledge, skills, and proficiency in this final contract phase; including a tailored training programme addressing these needs.
•	Project and transition plan. Supplier time, resources, and stakeholder engagement are carved out for KT. A detailed transition plan contains steps that the Buyer takes to assume responsibilities.
•	A virtual forum, sandbox environments, and chatbots where Buyer teams may put questions and discussions to IBM, or service their needs directly through a conversational interface
•	Hands-on workshops toward the end of this phase where Buyer teams actively engage in simulated and real-world scenarios, bridging the gap between theory and application.
•	1-1 mentoring and coaching giving direct access to IBM expertise towards contract end.
•	Certification and recognition for Buyer teams that demonstrate service/product proficiency; this is a motivating factor to invest in capability.
•	For post-contract support, we offer a period where our team may answer questions, provide guidance, and offer assistance. We tailor our approach to specific needs and learning preferences
•	Regular feedback, to refine and improve KT effectiveness and to map skills and further knowledge development.
 
At Nationwide Building Society, we co-created a delivery team to provide Agile training, with expert tutors and coaches to support teams once IBM had stepped away. This ensured that resources were supported as services moved to BAU.
Our work on the DWP API Gateway project included setting up an Enablement function. Through user research, we found that by providing support through training, guided learning, and call-out support, IBM could embed and drive maturity of the solution in an organic and collaborative manner. This was supported by a network of IBM and DWP cloud engineering specialists, who were advocates throughout the organisation (see Fig 2).
 
Enhancing Intelligent Client capability toward the end of the contract:
Enhancing end-contract capability is critical to a smooth transition. The result of our approach delivers these advantages to the Buyer:
 
•	Teams equipped with holistic, thorough knowledge and experience of the products/services and overall portfolio, having acquired hands-on, targeted training throughout contract lifespan
•	Teams ready to take on product/service ownership, having acquired confidence through targeted, immersive learning supported by the Virtual Library, sandbox training, and transition plan
•	Individuals working in an open, collective culture and empowered to take and show leadership as the Buyer takes on the products/services, through 1-1 mentoring and reverse mentoring
•	A partnership of equals, where both IBM and the Buyer constantly learn from each other in working style, culture, information and knowledge, through training, Q&A sessions, and the virtual forum
•	Fully qualified, up-to-date documentation and artefacts, revised and optimised throughout contract duration, and stored under controlled, agreed standards in the Virtual Library.

The Vaccination Programme has been overwhelmingly successful and has played a significant role in society returning to some level of normality in recent months. We are very excited at the prospect of working with you to the take the programme to the next phase of product development.  
User-centred design and user research are crucial to our programme-wide user-centred approach as they gain deep understanding of the COVID-19 Vaccination Programme’s users: members of the public eligible for vaccination, and the distributed workforce responsible for providing vaccinations. Equipped with this understanding, we will iteratively and innovatively design and evaluate our products and services to best fit the needs of those users, in alignment with the programme’s strategic objectives. Our approach will be centred on the following themes:  
User-centricity – our ethos is rooted in design thinking which is closely aligned to the NHS Digital and GDS Service Manuals through its consistent focus on users. We will co-design with representative and diverse user groups at all stages of the development lifecycle, promoting care, inclusion, usability and accessibility within our products and services. We will use qualitative and quantitative data about users, gained through analytics, data science, published literature and our own research, as input to our process and as evidence to promote user-centric decision-making. 
Standards-led – we have extensive experience designing products and services in healthcare and the public sector and of the standards that apply in those environments, such as NHS Service Standards, GDS Service Standards, and the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG 2.1). This includes, but isn’t limited to, working side-by-side with NHS D and various other NHS organisations, e.g., NHS Test & Trace (NHS T&T), Manchester University NHS Foundation Trust, and Guys and St Thomas’ NHS Foundation Trust. We will champion the NHS Digital Service Manual through our principles, methods, and mindset. We regularly pass GDS assessments and will incorporate mock assessments into our lifecycle with our NHSX Service Assessor, Flora MacLeod. 
Collaborative – we will be embedded across the cross-functional delivery squads as directed by the product management team, to promote a user-centred mindset in delivery. We will establish constructive working relationships with clinical experts, policy makers, and business stakeholders and proactively involve them in user-centred design to align and agree on user-centred outcomes. We will also collaborate outside of the programme with the wider NHS D user-centred design and user research communities on Slack, FutureNHS, and through meetups.   
 
Continuous improvement (MS01 & MS02): Our User Researchers will drive measurement to gain a comprehensive understanding of users’ current experience. The user research methods we will use include crafting user research plans (tailored to the service roadmap), conducting user interviews, co-design workshops, usability testing, accessibility assessments, A/B testing, and user analytics.  
Following the analysis of the insight gathered from the research reports, the team will craft user research and usability reports (with prioritised findings), define and prioritise problem statements, user needs, and metrics such as Staff Experience Score. Equipped with these findings, Service Designers, Interaction Designers and Content Designers will design and iterate the target experience using service maps, service blueprints, interaction flows, wireframes, prototypes, and content design and strategy. Our research and design methods will be aligned to the NHS Digital and GDS Service Manuals to root our work in best practice and accelerate rapid delivery. The insight that we gather through user research will be shared throughout the team to influence the future roadmap and overall strategy, referred to in T2.2. On top of using existing content, we will actively contribute any new patterns we develop and share these across the workstream and in wider communities we belong to, such as the NHS Service Manual Slack workspace. We will actively collaborate with our IBM and NHS colleagues working on Digital Transformation of Screening (DToS) and UKHSA Enterprise Data Analytics Platform (EDAP), consolidating research, and working together on patterns.  
An example of where we use co-design and user research to drive improvement of live services is our work with Scottish Government. Having successfully launched 7 new benefits over 4 years, there was a growing need to address evolving user needs and drive consistency across the services. The ‘Operational Readiness and Continuous Improvement’ team conducts ongoing user research to identify and prioritise opportunities to improve experiences across all benefit services and our ‘Service Patterns’ team crafts and maintains service design patterns to ensure consistency and reusability across services.  
Discovery (MS03 & MS04): We will run discoveries to understand and define new problem areas across the COVID-19 Vaccination Programme. Our User Researchers will conduct user interviews, run co-design activities, and work with our Service Designers to craft user research reports, vision statements, problem statements, user needs, personas, service blueprints, and hypotheses. The hypotheses will be tested, and the outcomes presented to senior leadership to support them in making decisions on the prioritisation of the service portfolio roadmap. This will establish an overall delivery model that is led by user needs and aligned to our strategic objectives. A recent example of this process was our discovery work with the Foods Standards Agency, which focused on identifying key user needs and existing pain points by conducting user research with a large range of user groups. These needs were collated into a comprehensive set of 'Needs Statements' and prioritised with Product Owners into a set of hypotheses to be tested during alpha. This discovery work resulted in the establishment of a food business registration service, replacing a vast range of paper processes for 387 local councils.  
Shaping the delivery model (MS05): To improve the products and services of the COVID-19 Vaccination Programme, we will continually aim to improve our ways of working and delivery model, as is detailed in T5.2. When making improvements to our delivery model, we will focus on three fundamentals:  
Understanding: adopting an evidence-based approach to understanding current issues. 
Measurement: measuring and calculating the impact of ideas aligned to our strategic objectives. 
Reflect: comparing the actual value created to the calculated impact and to our objectives. 
Any delivery model improvements we identify, will be filtered and prioritised as part of the roadmap and backlog prioritisation process, as outlined in T2.2. While adopting an ethos of continuous improvement throughout the Call-Off contract, we will have a specific focus on improving our delivery model throughout the first 3 months while we make the full transition.

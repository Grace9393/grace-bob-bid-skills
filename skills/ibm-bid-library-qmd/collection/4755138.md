We fully support longer term productivity improvements such as your requirement to reduce support T&M caps by 10% each year. However, we also think that there is a risk that this could drive behaviour that you do not want and that therefore this is an area we should discuss.
 
For example, if we were able to identify a 20% saving in year 1 this would be of higher value to you than a 10% saving in Years 1 and 2.  We would not want to find ourselves, essentially holding back an improvement so that we could be sure that we could achieve the next 2 years’ worth of required savings.  This is not to say that we would do that, it is simply an example of how some SLAs could inadvertently drive the wrong behaviours.  We have ideas for mechanisms that could resolve this example with constructs such as credit carried forward, or even ‘extra credit’ for early delivery of benefits that when projected over the life of the contract over-delivers compared to your 10% per year requirement.
 
We also note that because the pricing construct for ongoing support has been defined as Capped T&M, there is actually no incentive for a supplier to over-achieve.  Any cost optimisations or service improvements would yield savings for the Home Office but reduced revenue and no margin improvement for the supplier, and in fact a tighter T&M cap increases risk.  This could drive a behaviour of relaxation in teams once the annual saving has been realised.  A construct in which both parties benefit would encourage ongoing pursuit of savings and/or service improvement.
 
Lastly, to realise cost efficiencies, it is often necessary to update applications, perhaps to simplify or refactor some code or to address a root cause.  These updates will be in competition with enhancement backlogs and so we are keen to ensure that KPI interactions permit these updates to take their place in the workload so that the contracted efficiencies can be realised.

Our approach to knowledge transfer (KT) and capability building in Digital, Technology and Cyber projects is to integrate it into the lifecycle as a continuous process, rather than as an isolated activity at the end. This enables you to actively engage in learning and skill development throughout our projects, and start early. Our role is to enable you or your Service Provider to continue to improve and update your and their approaches to delivering Digital and Technology innovation and Secure IT. Our success measure is the ability to hand it back to a team which has built the experience and skills needed to manage the service effectively. 
 
We follow a structured process, specifically tailored to address the unique requirements of your CTO, Security and Operations functions, ensuring you and your Service Providers’ teams gain the essential knowledge and skills to enhance strategy and framework development, IT service delivery and security compliance in IT operations,  through strategic planning, risk management, process improvement, performance tracking, and project delivery, while ensuring compliance with the SEC and your licence conditions. The following process allows us to develop a joint KT plan within a structured framework, which we deliver in an iterative way. There are the steps we take with you:  
Assess the project domain, and identify and prioritise areas for knowledge transfer and upskilling 
Agree the knowledge areas, skill levels and resource planning that will be needed for each of your roles and define the relevant methods to progress from entry-level to thought leader for each. 
Evaluate: Request the DCC team members to complete a light-weight self-evaluation, to inform their personal skill development areas – this highlights the priority areas for team learning. Create personalised learning journeys with DCC or Service Provider, based upon their evaluations and preferred learning styles.  These plans guide and measure personal development to competence. 
Pairing your team members with IBM practitioners for knowledge exchange; and a gradual rebalancing of our involvement as the DCC team becomes more proficient. 
Mentoring is a key vehicle for knowledge transfer, and DCC/Service Provider team members will have regular meetings with IBM mentors to discuss their learning journey progress and effectiveness. 
Re-iteration – create and regularly update How-To guides, work instructions and supporting materials to reflect technology, security and digital delivery methods and tools developments and feedback. This will ensure they continue to be meaningful for the team, at the moment in time they need to access them. 
Tools, Methodologies and Skills to transfer knowledge and skills for long term success 
We use the IBM Knowledge and Skill Transfer methodology (outline of which is set out above) to ensure that knowledge and skills are passed on to DCC’s team, commencing from day one and to enable long-term skill acquisition. Building on the methodology, the techniques we use to ensure that knowledge and skills needed are passed on to DCC’s team include: 
High Quality, Accessible Content: producing high-quality materials on an accessible platform, is essential to nurturing your expertise. In our experience, timeliness of knowledge creation is extremely important when developing skills; those learning may need to reference materials more frequently. 
Blended Learning: We see capability building as more than just training completion, we advocate for a blended learning approach infusing regular training sessions with on-the-job activities and experiences. We rotate work within teams to build knowledge, provide variation and promote innovation. 
Continuously provide learning opportunities: through our daily engagement and regular creation of materials, but to actively support an individual’s capability development, we establish initiatives such as featuring individuals in monthly learning sessions, request their feedback on materials produced, asking them to add user stories for material re-use creation and capture colleague comments in Agile reviews. 
Community creation: we help to establish Communities of Practice where peers can come together to share experiences, learn from each other, overcome challenges and understand of development journeys in more detail. The CoP also fosters innovation ideas and hosts shared learning events. 
Recognition: We will recognise team members who advance their technical delivery, IT domain and cyber knowledge by celebrating their skill milestones. Our experience has shown that celebrating success fosters a culture of high performance and will positively impact on promoting skills across DCC. 
The tools we employ to ensure that knowledge and skills needed are passed on to DCC’s team include: 
Tailored AI learning using IBM-engineered Generative AI prompts to provide materials that are tailored to different learning styles / Myer-Briggs personality types. 
Where appropriate to individuals, we offer clients free access to IBM’s SkillsBuild training platform (https://skillsbuild.org), covering topics from data to emerging technology and AI to project management. 
Our IBM Garage approach offers an active learning environment with user-centred design with tools such as Jira, Confluence, Mural embedded. 
We support digital ‘badge’ credentials through Open Badges / Credly to support recognition 
We create centralised document libraries containing role-based user guides, personalised training plans, skills logs on your Sharepoint system 
Examples of our tools and methods in action, in the Knowledge Transfer and Capability Building in relation to delivery of advisory-centric Digital, Technology and Cyber services, are:  
We created and implemented the new learning curriculum for “Architect” and “Engineer” global populations within Barclays. These Practices had an unstructured approach to learning, so it was difficult to understand current skill levels and future aspirations across the team. We created an industry best practice learning curriculum on topics such as hybrid cloud architecture, by leveraging IBM Watson.ai Talent Framework to identify roles to skills, to learning resources, to gain perspective on future aspirations and improve their technical capabilities, following targeted learning pathways. We used a modularised, masterclass approach with key industry insights and guest speakers. Examples of our tools and methods in action, in the Knowledge Transfer and Capability Building in relation to delivery of advisory-centric Digital, Technology and Cyber services, are:  
We created and implemented the new learning curriculum for “Architect” and “Engineer” global populations within Barclays. These Practices had an unstructured approach to learning, so it was difficult to understand current skill levels and future aspirations across the team. We created an industry best practice learning curriculum on topics such as hybrid cloud architecture, by leveraging IBM Watson.ai Talent Framework to identify roles to skills, to learning resources, to gain perspective on future aspirations and improve their technical capabilities, following targeted learning pathways. Knowledge and performance increased with strong feedback, and ongoing client requests for new topics e.g. GenAI, FinOps, SRE   
Overall learner satisfaction was 95%, which was driven through the continuous feedback loop we had where we listened, acted and delivered. The process is now owned, run and managed by Barclays which was their objective, with IBM input on request.  
We partnered with a nuclear small-modular reactor startup, based in UK, Italy and France who were seeking a better solution for managing complex engineering documentation and data.  We led on the selection, design and deployment of the solution, which enables a single ‘point of record’, real-time reporting and integration, from requirements to the fulfilment of engineering project, ‘moving with the speed of the data’. A new range of IBM-built digital tools, such as Azure integration for the KM system, SuccessFactors, Concur, SDWrox and other core systems in an event-driven, rules-based workflow platform, are enabling the client to evolve from a research organisation to an Original Equipment Manufacturer, by being able to move modelled concepts through R&D to production. Our Advisory role was in business operational transformation, to move to a data-centric mode of working, equipping the team with the IT skills to support their transition into the manufacturing operating realm. Deliverables included: end to end KPI reporting on the reworked processes; capability and road-mapping prioritisation for programmes of both IT and business delivery; and governance structures to tightly couple business need to project delivery. All of these are now embedded ways of working in the organisation as we continue to engage within their innovation programme. 
 
A Global leader in Data and Analytics, Dun & Bradstreet, asked IBM to de-risk their journey to Hybrid Cloud and accelerate its adoption. Delivering 6 MVPs with ‘Hybrid by Design’ methods, covering GenAI with watsonx, FinOps, Security, DevOps in a joint Cloud Centre of Excellence team for GCP, embedded skills and ways of working have seen time to market improve ongoing by 73%, $110m savings realised on IT operations; the result of improving both developer productivity and end user satisfaction measures, across multiple analytics brands, with the MVPs forming the base for growth and the Centre of Excellence D&B team as ‘custodians’ of the product set using IBM Garage methods and tools to govern the AI+Analytics product roadmap and its secure deployment to cloud .

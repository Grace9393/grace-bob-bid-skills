a) AQG2(i). Identifying/deploying experienced individuals: we will review customer requirements (specification, outcomes, activities, etc.) and select a Project Lead to identify skills required using our Job-Role-Skills classification (JRS). Using our Expertise Finder'', which uses AI to curate skills from CVs and has a natural language interface to match people to roles, we will review potential CV's and check previous Project Assessments to determine 'best fit'. Internal interviews help in making a 'short-list', presented to the customer (if required) for final selection. Once individuals are allocated, we will hold joint pre-mobilisation meetings with the customer to start the process of team building and integration. Should any individual be determined to not be the right fit, we can identify others who may be a better fit. We ensure our consultants are appropriately skilled through global, digital developpment ecosystem that regularly carries out industry-based gap assessments (e.g. for Health & Social Care) developing timebound plans offering training, accreditations and global project experience so they can stay up to date with skills, industry knowledge and certifications.
Seamless integration: 
1) We will put a robust governance structure in place (including Project Charters describing expected joint team behaviours) at project commencement phase that facilitates a seamless integration/blending of consultants. IBM’s Client Relationship Management Framework helps with forward planning, resource/skills forecasts, business reviews etc. to ensure 'One Team' ethos between our and customer teams. 
2) Using our hiring, training and deployment processes ensure we offer candidates who are industry experts or have worked in customers’ sectors for several years, making it easier to blend seamlessly with customer SMEs.  This ensures effective communication/escalation management is in place and business benefits are continuously being reviewed/realised.
 
b) AQG2(ii). To ensure knowledge and expertise is successfully transferred, we will develop a joint knowledge transfer (KT) plan/approach to provide a structured framework and iterative method for executing KT from IBM to customer teams. Using our proven agile transition approach as an example, we will blend planning for product/sprint backlog with clear sprint execution plans and entry / exit criteria for each KT phase.
Each sprint cycle provides for incremental learning on processes, tools, workflows, specifications, designs, coding standards, code check-in / check-out etc. Each Sprint has its own 'Plan-Learn-Perform' stage where we ensure customer teams are becoming self-reliant. At the end of the sprint cycle, our customer’s teams are able to support, independently or under our observation, the activities that have been learned and performed in preceding sprint while continuing to learn the next set of activities in ongoing sprint. This proven method gradually frees up our experts as customer teams take ownership.
The project phase coincides with the 'Perform' phase of the sprint where we walk customer teams through new operational processes, decision workflows, etc. and steps necessary to successfully manage the project outcomes. We would leverage our experience from our work  to support Scotland's Social Security benefits services. As part of our work, we expect to have key responsibilities for mentoring and KT. We will develop upskilling plans per role-type and introduce Communities-of-Practice, which are informal networks designed specifically for efficient knowledge-sharing. We will also Introduce a capability tracking mechanism to jointly measure growth in skills of the customer's teams. We can then determine if the approach taken supports the capability build. If not, we agree adjustments, so together we are focused on enabling self-sufficient teams who can work across the full delivery lifecycle through transition to live operations.
 
c) AQG2(iii). Through a structured approach, we harness the learnings and knowledge accumulated from previous projects (both 'first-of-a-kind' as well as standard) to build reusable artifacts/assets, completed 'lessons learnt' templates, and accelerators which are used to bring efficiencies and benefits to future projects. To ensure lessons from previous customer engagements are embedded and readily available to delivery teams, we identify and store assets and knowledge of our customers’ sectors, systems, policies, processes, people, values and technologies and provide a thorough induction/training on these to all team members at project start.
Examples of reusable artefacts we draw on include:
- Project deliverables including proposals, work plans, risk logs, reports, presentations, designs, instructional materials and process maps 
- Tools used to implement a process such as checklists, surveys, models, templates, and automated tool capabilities, and 
- Software code, class libraries, models and architectures.
In new engagements, we bring these reusable artifacts and IBM best practices to bear in maximising reusability, cost savings and process efficiencies. The efficiencies and benefits delivered as a result of our approach includes:
- Encouraged creation and sharing of reusable assets between our and customer teams leading to time and cost efficiency in future projects 
- Improved categorisation of submitted assets making it faster and easier to search and improve the opportunity for reuse 
- A robust storage repository that is often shared between our and customer teams 
- Creation of a Knowledge Hub that hosts/maintains all assets centrally, and 
- Regular tracking and updates to all stakeholders.
As a result of our approach, we are able to demonstrate savings in time, effort and cost of 10%-25% on relevant customer engagements.

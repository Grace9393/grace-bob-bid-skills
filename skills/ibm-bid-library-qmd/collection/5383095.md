How we will support and actively engage with your strategic planning to enhance and enable budgeting, planning and forecasting 
We share your belief that it’s important for us to engage with you in the APRA planning process right from the very outset of the annual cycle. APRA will form a significant source of backlog items within the SLC Customer Garage. The whole engine is dependent on accurate forecasting, planning and value based prioritisation of work items, be those inputs from APRA or broader innovation initiatives. 
We’ll work with you during the Discovery and Inception phase to conduct a two-stage process. Firstly, a top-down prioritisation exercise to create a strategic roadmap, and secondly, detailed planning to add a degree of rigour and robustness so that it can be relied upon to enable budgeting. Because we’ll be working together much earlier in your APRA cycle than you do today, we’ll bring forward an improvement in the planning orientated metrics listed within your systems of measurement. In particular for ‘Increase in % of releases implemented to within 10% of business case estimate (end of Inception)’ where we think that it is possible to achieve this accuracy ahead of Inception. 
In determining our approach, we believe prioritisation should start from a strategic perspective, and that the pressure to deal with urgent tactical needs should come second. By adopting this approach, we can together ensure that your strategy is delivered and that we are not simply reacting to short term pressures. We will do this by assessing the value of each initiative using a common metric weighted in favour of strategic roadmap items. This will feed into the Garage framework and determine the prioritisation of work items on the relevant Garage backlog. Strategic imperatives on the roadmap will remain committed on the backlog and the agile characteristics of our proposed delivery model will allow us to scale capacity as required to meet tactical demand.
Our prioritisation approach will deliver on two aims: 
- to accommodate the regular heartbeat of new legislation that must be implemented within each APRA planning cycle, and 
- to focus delivery resources on the highest value items from across your portfolio of work. 
To really make this work your Chief Product Owner should regularly convene with other key members of the Garage Interface Squad to review and adjust backlogs, so that they account for factors like dependencies and funding, as well as other factors like availability of key skills, and strategic imperatives. To help identify what is of highest value to you, we will create a value framework that defines the drivers for generating value and their relative importance. This model will enable us to jointly compare initiatives of different natures in a consistent way. The values associated with each will be tracked using the mechanism described in the ‘benefits of our approach’ section of this response. 
When work items have been allocated a value and prioritised on your Apply, Assess, Pay or Repay Garage backlog, we will lay them out onto a roadmap whilst taking account of any interdependencies. For example, it may be that a strategic action, such as moving your Customer Portal to the cloud, can also address a pressing tactical need, such as the need for a hardware refresh. 
At this stage, we'll combine the backlogs to produce an overall Customer Enablement Systems Roadmap view that we'll be able to look at through different lenses (policy, technology, corporate, business optimisation or Evolve). This roadmap will be highly dynamic as priorities change and new problems or opportunities are worked through and incorporated. It will also be highly visible and will ideally live in physical form on a wall in the SLC Customer Garage location. 
A key step in planning is that of establishing the optimum delivery model, and delivery location, for each potential project. To ensure that the right delivery models are chosen for each piece of work, we will assess the characteristics of each and apply our experience and knowledge of delivery trade-offs to inform the right profile for each project. It’s how our IBM Garages around the world successfully deliver thousands of projects and programmes every year. 
When the delivery model is defined and agreed within the SLC Customer Garage, we will estimate duration and effort levels required. We already deliver a significant proportion of our projects and programmes, well over half, using fixed price / fixed outcome contracting mechanisms. This is an approach that we are used to managing and are confident that we can help you move to this way of working, in line with your stated objective. We have developed tools, techniques, and an estimating centre of competence that allows us to create estimates to a high degree of accuracy even at very early stages of the planning process. We will feed into this best practice from our global network of Garages, which all use similar approaches to estimating, and actively share ideas and insights. 
Finally, we will lay out the projects onto an overarching programme plan. Within the plan we will create decision checkpoints that will be used by the SLC Customer Garage Interface team and your ICF to measure progress. To maintain focus on value, we will create a Benefits Realisation view of the plan to monitor, manage and enhance the benefits delivered by each project and programme as it progresses through its lifecycle. The contents of this plan are described below under ‘the benefits of our approach, and how we will manage and validate these’.

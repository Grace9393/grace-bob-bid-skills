CASE STUDY ONE: European Electricity Network Provider Project: Lean Connections Programme  
Date: 2019 and 2021 - 2023 / Team size: IBM: 3-6 / Client: Core Team 6 
Operational sub-lots covered: Change Management | Business Process & Operational Planning | Forecasting and/or planning | Programme and Project Management | Value for Money Reviews | Performance monitoring and reporting 
Contract scope and objectives:  Review and optimise the ‘Application to Energise’ processes for renewable grid connections nationwide and establish an operational excellence core team.  
Objectives: For the national government to achieve their target of 70% renewable electricity generation by 2030, our client needed to significantly improve their change management processes and optimise how they planned and delivered projects and programmes to reduce lead-times, improve forecast accuracy and reporting, and deliver better value for money for energy consumers.   
Key outputs and deliverables: 
Halving the time-to-connect through improved process and operational efficiency. 
Meeting regulatory requirements to provide quotes for connection applications (ahead of auctions), whilst not increasing headcount despite a significant increase in volumes.   
Ensuring greater quotation accuracy and project cost control, governance and assurance to address historical cost-creep issues. 
Reduction in asset values on balance sheets through improved energisation and handover process. 
Established Operational Excellence training and certification programme for their project teams. 
Key deliverables included end-to-end process maps, training materials, Business Intelligence dashboards and virtual Project Studio workspaces for optimised project team collaboration. 
Managing scope creep, maintaining cost control, and ensuring quality: 
Scope:  We managed scope creep by conducting a detailed current state assessment, then designing the future state and developing a detailed implementation plan, which defined the scope.  This was agreed with the client’s senior management and communicated across the organisation.  Adherence to this plan was ensured by strict governance processes, with only authorised client staff able to approve scope/plan changes.   
Cost: We maintained cost control by agreeing and adhering to a clear plan, timescales, schedule, methods (e.g. format of workshops, use of ‘Mission Control’ to visualise progress to plan), and resource model. We engaged and consulted the client’s senior stakeholders to ensure teams had the required information and resources to avoid delays and rework.  We conducted monthly meetings to track progress, course correct as needed and plan ahead. 
Quality: By using our proven, structured and standardised approaches (rooted in Lean A3 Thinking) and adhering to the implementation plan (to ensure the correct pace), we ensured quality.  Ahead of change delivery, we ensured that key client personnel had all required information to make decisions.  Our follow-up procedure ensured that the target outcomes and benefits had been delivered and sustained (30/60/90 days after the change event). If not, we implemented course-correction activities to bring the change back on track. 
Specific methodologies or approaches used: 
IBM’s Lean operational improvement methodology underpinned the approaches used. We tailored this methodology to suit the client’s situation, i.e. the project’s environment and a regulated industry (with its inherent constraints, external oversight and need for approval/validation for some decisions). 
Key principles of this improvement methodology include: 
Collaboration and Flow: i.e. the right people working on the right tasks at the right time, making right-first-time decisions to reduce the time taken to respond to customers. 
Front-load Work: investing in discovery activities and using standardised designs to ensure accurate, informed inputs, not assumptions/estimated, were used to complete customer offers.  This avoided our client ‘picking up the bill’ for necessary works (e.g. substation expansion) that previously were frequently unearthed only after commencing construction work.  
Short Interval Project Control, visualising status in real-time and taking action (rather than reporting on past performance) to improve quality (by catching and addressing any issues quickly) and reduce the time to complete offers and energise new sites.   
Some of the specific techniques and approaches we employed were:  
Optimised project sequencing:  Vertical Value Stream Mapping approach used to design the ideal project flow, ‘working backwards’ from outcome to inception to optimise the sequence of the key stages of the project.  For each stage we determined ‘what the project needs’, and therefore which teams should gather and analyse information, make decisions and execute actions.   
Use of ‘cells’ (for planning, scoping and costing):  Establish teams with all of the required skills, knowledge and authority to make decisions ‘in flow’.  Design-out siloes and build-in collaboration. 
Project Studios: Collaborative spaces to visualise projects’ scope and status, ensure clear roles and responsibilities, tackle problems, take decisions and harness a Continuous Improvement mindset. 
Visual Management:  Building Power BI dashboards to compile and visualise all of the relevant metrics that teams needed to understand how they were performing overall and on specific projects. 
Level of responsibility our team held during the engagement: 
We were the client’s trusted strategic advisor, reporting into the client’s Senior Manager of Programme Delivery. The budget approval was signed off by the CEO, whom we kept regularly briefed. We were responsible for co-designing the Future State for the Connection Process, providing coaches to facilitate workshops and help the client solve problems and implement new ways-of-working.  We also trained and certified client staff, allowing them to continue to apply the work to other areas. 
Results achieved and value delivered:  Some of the outcomes we delivered for the client included: 
>50% improvement in auction application scoping: 2022 auction applications were scoped, costed and made complete before the 2021 applications (using the client’s old approach) were completed. This demonstrated significantly improved operational planning capability. 
100% of 2022 auctions offers made on-time: This unlocked € millions in regulator incentives, reduced the client’s operational costs and improved business processes and operational planning. 
47% reduction in overall “energise to technical completion” timeline, which reduced the client’s Assets under Construction balance sheet by over a quarter of a billion Euros.  The resulting reductions in monthly interest payments further increased the programme’s value for money. 
Completion 18 months ahead of schedule: The projects from early 2022 demonstrated the extent to which the client’s project and programme management capability had been improved. 
Non-viable projects withdrawn early: our improved monitoring and reporting (as well as improved cost accuracy) meant that decisions, using trustworthy data, could be taken sooner.  
Internal Lean Connections team trained and certified using IBM’s teaching and assessment programme, which is accredited by Cardiff University’s Lean Competency System. This demonstrated that internal operational excellence and change management capability had been established at the Client. 
 
Comparisons with DCC and industry: 
Our client operates in the same industry, and within a very similar regulated environment to the DCC. 
The need for active stakeholder engagement and communication within and outside the client environment is key for the DCC, and on this project we helped our client engage, consult and collaborate with the regulator and put the customer first, supporting them in communication events, and providing key showcase materials and reporting. 
Where possible to do so, using modular, standardised designs and processes to minimise costs, and avoiding “cost creep”, sticking to the project budget through robust project management, will be critical to DCC under its ex-ante price control framework. Our tight and active control of this client project and its finances are direct parallels. 
DCC needs to manage its portfolio of programmes tightly and introduce approaches to deliver greater consistency and optimise performance and outcomes – the nature of this project’s process optimisation initiative contains methods, approaches (such as project sequencing, vertical value mapping, and project studios) and learnings that could be directly applied to the DCC.   
 
 
CASE STUDY TWO:   
Client: HMRC / Project: NorthStar - Portfolio management review / Date: May 2021 / to October 2023 /  
Team size: IBM: 12 / Client: 15 / 3rd Party: 13 
 
Operational Sub-Lots covered during this Project: 2.3 Business Processes and Operational Planning, 2.5 Programme & project management, 2.6 Value for money reviews, 2.7 Performance Monitoring and Reporting 
 
Contract scope and objectives: 
IBM worked with HMRC and Moorhouse Consulting to shape and deliver HMRC’s Portfolio Management. HMRC has one of Europe's most complex change and technology portfolios, spanning border controls, personal tax, VAT, fraud and COVID support. HMRC’s Transformation Group sought a Portfolio Management Strategic Partner with proven expertise to lead the Transformation Portfolio and oversee delivery of HMRC’s Portfolio Management strategy in the medium term and create a sustainable ‘fit for the future’ in-house Portfolio Management function within 18-24 months. 
 
Key outputs and deliverables: 
There were several areas of focus and outputs required from this portfolio review 
Active management of a £1.2bn portfolio of 34 Programmes and 134 projects using a standard KPI reporting mechanism that provides a ‘single version of the truth’; with extra support for complex programmes 
Establishment of a single Portfolio Management Function with 40 staff in 4 delivery squads, (integrated Civil Servant / NorthStar teams), with the Senior Civil Servant 2 Portfolio Director reporting to the Director General of the Transformation and Executive Committee 
 
How IBM managed scope creep, maintained cost control, and ensured quality 
For the portfolio review, we established several key principles together with HMRC which were instilled to provide greater assurance and rigour across their entire programme portfolio. 
Portfolio risk was brought into tolerance for the 21/22 Financial Year and managed through specific management interventions and targeted escalations 
Advanced review: Prior to the Contract Management Board, we submitted draft deliverables for an initial review to HMRC. Comments were provided requesting clarifications or amendments within 2 days. We had a further 3 days in which to clarify or remedy any items.  
Using Governance to remove obstacles: At the formal Contract Management Board (CMB), each service level or deliverable was assessed against HMRC Commercial's quality scoring criteria. We also assessed risks, issues, escalations and planed the following Sprint. We also held a Sprint Retrospective to capture what worked well, could have been better and to highlight exceptional contributions across the joint team. Following approval of the work at the CMB, deliverables were accepted, and invoice payments were instructed. 
 
Specific methodologies or approaches used: 
We deployed Value Orchestration, IBM’s iterative methodology to drive end-to-end value from strategy formulation to execution with a governance approach being one of “Air Traffic Control” enablement, ensuring delivery of multiple programmes/workstreams from a range of suppliers across a portfolio of work.  
We created a NorthStar Academy to develop in-house capabilities in Portfolio Management through upskilling of resources to self-sufficiency (ongoing). Project NorthStar received positive feedback including a special focus at the 2022 Project Delivery Profession conference for:  
Establishment of a single Portfolio Management Function with 40 staff in 4 delivery squads, (integrated Civil Servant /NorthStar teams), with SCS2 Portfolio Director reporting to DG Transformation and Executive Committee 
Co-creation and co-execution of 17 processes embedded in 9 months including identification of 190 cross-portfolio dependencies, up from zero in the previous year 
We worked collaboratively with the Investment Planning Team to deliver HMRC’s £5bn Spending Review bid including re-shaping the portfolio to align with Settlement outcomes and a restructuring of Programme ownership within ExCom 
We established effective workforce planning for over 2000 staff working in multi-functional teams with specific interventions to address key resource shortfalls 
We maintained connectivity throughout this process through 121s between the NorthStar Programme / Deputy Programme Directors with the HMRC Programme Lead and their Commercial lead. Our Engagement Partner met with Moorhouse’s Engagement Partner and the HMRC Executive every two weeks to make sure that our work was fully reflecting HMRC’s priorities, also to collect feedback on performance, delivery and where any potential obstacles could be removed. 
 
The level of responsibility your team held during the engagement. 
IBM carried out the role of Portfolio Management Strategic Partner with proven expertise to lead the Transformation Portfolio and oversee delivery of HMRC’s Portfolio Management strategy. 
We also had the responsibility of ensuring that, in the medium term, we could create a sustainable ‘fit for the future’ in-house Portfolio Management function. Medium term being 18-24 months on from project commencement. 
 
Results Achieved and Value delivered: 
Some of the outcomes we delivered for HMRC were: 
Managed down a £170m portfolio resulting in a £50m net underspend due to increased scrutiny on programme design, work-packaging, planning, business sponsorship and resourcing. 
Working collaboratively with the Investment Planning Team to deliver HMRC’s £5bn Spending Review bid including re-shaping the portfolio to align with Settlement outcomes and a restructuring of Programme ownership within ExCom. 
Establishment of effective workforce planning for over 2000 staff working in multi-functional teams with specific interventions to address key resource shortfalls. 
Development of in-house capabilities in Portfolio Management, with a team that had been up-skilled effectively enough to become proficient and self-sufficient 
 
Comparisons with DCC and industry 
Like HMRC, DCC must work as the single option, mandated central body, in a highly regulated market sector. As with HMRC, for DCC, it is an imperative that delivery is of the highest quality, at the highest standard and that security is always maintained.  
 
Financial control is also key (which is critical to the DCC under its ex-ante and price control regime), if programmes do not have strong governance, and a strong framework in place they are placed at risk from scope creep, time overrun and consequently, an increase in cost. DCC have enormous transformational change ahead. The predictability of cost, and performance as DCC moves into its ex-ante forecasting model mean that consistent approaches and methods deployed by a skilled partner will be crucially important.

1. Our capacity and capability to support the transition from incumbent

We are a top-5 ranked Managed Security Services Provider (MSSP) globally (IDC MarketScape, 2024) with 400+ Tier 1/2 threat detection/investigation analysts. In the UK, our capacity and capability are further evidenced through our dedicated Cyber Security Services Business Unit, with 121 security SMEs. Below, we outline how we satisfy all 'must' and ‘should’ supplier Appendix B3 requirements relevant to this question (with references to such in square brackets).

Our capacity and capability to support transition from the incumbent is demonstrated through our track record of transitioning and supporting the UK’s most critical services and infrastructure, using our Plan-Learn-Perform methodology detailed in section 1a. Successful transitions include:
• Driver and Vehicle Licensing Agency (DVLA): Following the incumbent removal due to performance disputes, DVLA needed a new SOC service within 4 weeks. IBM mobilised a team on-site in 3 days, installing SIEM hardware, developing playbooks, and transferring operations successfully. Over the following months, we fine-tuned the SIEM solution, ensured log sources were operational, and performed a successful knowledge transfer to our SOC team.
• National Savings & Investments (NS&I): Mobilised a team of 16 analysts, 4 engineers, 1 focal analyst and an incident response manager in 4 weeks, covering protective monitoring, incident management and threat intelligence.
• The Home Office’s Biometrics Matcher: Our 25 security specialists successfully took over from Fujitsu in September 2023. The services include 24x7x365 protective monitoring, incident management, threat intelligence, adopting the LogRhythm SIEM, correlation engineering, vulnerability management, security risk management, and efficient service management.
• NHSE Cervical Screening: Mobilised a team of 80 and transitioned services in 8 weeks from contract start, taking over the digital transformation programme. Our approach to mobilisation has now become the NHSE standard for service transition. 
For the CSOC contract, we will leverage our SOC capability, which currently process 1 trillion+ security events monthly. Our Hursley SOC, a List X-certified facility, will serve as the hub for your service and we will co-locate with you, in Leeds, for the Transition. With a Service Manager and 20+ analysts well-versed in NHSE CSOC ways of working, we can accelerate/de risk transition from Day 1. Additional resilience and capacity are offered through our Leicester SOC, with Farnborough to follow in 2025.
 
a. Our Service Transition methodology, delivering to your requirements and timescales

Figure 3.1 illustrates our established 'Plan–Learn–Perform' service transition approach and its focus on relationship development, knowledge transfer, process documentation and planned transition. This proven approach has been incrementally refined and validated from hundreds of successful, complex transitions delivered by IBM over many years. For NHSE CSOC, we will tailor our proven approach to meet all your requirements. Our team, Jason Finlayson (Customer Liaison), Ruth Garry (Project Manager), Colin Hamilton (Threat Management Lead), Laura Martin (IBM CSOC Service Manager) and Doug Pooley (Technical Liaison), will be responsible for delivering a successful transition across the 3 phases of our method. The high-level phases are:
1. Plan – We will establish clear milestones and outline the roles and responsibilities of all stakeholders, including client teams and incumbent suppliers. Our planning phase ensures a comprehensive understanding of the project requirements and a solid foundation for transition. During ‘Plan’, we will collaboratively agree a governance structure to measure and track our transition and knowledge transfer completion.
2. Learn – We will ‘shadow’ both the NHSE and incumbent to gather knowledge, acquire skills and refine our approach. Our teams will undertake a series of onboarding activities to build on our existing understanding of NHSE’s culture, alongside detailed knowledge transfer activities such as self study to understand documentation; business knowledge on processes and backlog items; technical knowledge on the system architecture, designs, configuration and development; and best practices for working with the NHSE toolset.
3. Perform – We will ‘reverse-shadow’ (with the incumbent providing oversight) to execute our plan, monitor progress and adapt where necessary. During this period, our teams will gradually take on more responsibility from the incumbent and run regular simulations to ensure that they are comfortable and able to run the service independently; this de-risks transition significantly.
Figure 3.2 illustrates how we have tailored our Plan-Learn-Perform approach to align with your 8-week timescale, including weekly checkpoints to provide visibility and incremental confidence that we will deliver to your timelines and requirements. From learned experience, we know that the earlier we move to the Perform phase and commence ‘reverse-shadowing’, the better. Our plan
below (Figure 3.2) reflects this, highlighting that we will be operationally ready to undertake handover activities by week 4 [05] and commence ‘reverse-shadow’ from week 5. During the shadowing phase, we would co-locate with the incumbent and client at NHSE’s SOC in Leeds within business hours. After hours, we would work with the incumbent remotely. For reverse- shadow, we will again co-locate during working hours, but after hours, we will take the lead remotely, with the incumbent providing oversight/quality assurance

2. How we ensure the delivery timescales are met and continuity of service

To ensure we are meeting your delivery timescales, we will track progress of the plan in Figure 3.2 with a simple, but structured, governance process [01]. During the first week, we will deliver the following key enablement activities:
1. Establishing ‘Transition Board’ Governance – this weekly meeting will be attended by all parties (us, you, and the incumbent) to review transition status; key risks, issues and mitigations; and key decisions. This will escalate issues and sign-off of key transition gates.
2. Issue a Transition Plan and Readiness Template which we will draft and agree with your leadership team and the incumbent. It will be reviewed and will include:
• The transition process (including 4 Transition Gates for ‘Plan’, ‘Shadow’, ‘Reverse-Shadow’ and ‘Incumbent Exit’, with Acceptance Criteria (AC) for each).
• RACI (Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed) Matrix for stakeholders.
• Activity mapping with clearly defined ownership of key tasks.
 
3. Issue a transition RAID log – to be maintained and reviewed as part of the Transition Board.

By week 2, we will have created the KT team (including our IBM Focal Analyst, one analyst from NHSE, and one from the incumbent) who can upskill our Focal Analyst around the 4 key areas of proactive monitoring, incident management, threat intelligence and KPIs/SLAs. Our Hursley SOC service shall be configured to present a static publicly-routable External UK IP address for connectivity to you [46] and will provide our External IP Address for Allow Listing [47]. Week 3 will see our Focal Analyst working to shadow the KT Team and ready to start handover with our SOC. Weeks 4 and 5 will see our Focal Analyst take this information and relay it to key resources in our Hursley SOC. This will happen iteratively due to the nature of the team shift patterns. During weeks 5 to 8, the upskilled shift teams will commence reverse-shadowing, with the incumbent providing oversight and guidance. In week 7, we will conduct a Transition Exit Checkpoint, meeting
to confirm readiness to transition to Steady State delivery. Any actions will be addressed to ensure the 8-week transition is met.

Consistent with our transition experiences described in section 1, we propose the incumbent supplier maintains responsibility for the SLAs until ‘Incumbent Exit’. We will assure continuity of service by:
1. Ensuring we have the right resources with the right skills appointed.
2. Ensuring our staff are clear on the latest functionalities of your toolsets, how they fit into the CSOC processes and have all required access. Our Hursley SOC analysts are already well-versed with your architecture and tools (Sentinel, MS Defender, Teams, Confluence, Jira, ServiceNow) from working with NHSE and other government clients.
3. Agreeing with the incumbent their oversight is required for the entire 8 week transition period.
4. Conducting a thorough risk assessment and impact analysis, so all parties are aware and can proactively mitigate, track, and escalate potential threats.
5. Prioritising the information and KT workshops in week 1 to ensure identification and escalation protocols for Critical, High, Medium and Low-level alerts are understood, as well as seamless shift hand-over practices are maintained. Therefore, if a major incident occurs whilst we are reverse-shadowing, we are well prepared, and can support both NHSE and the incumbent.
6. Whilst shadowing, we will understand the documented process. Whilst reverse-shadowing we will identify, escalate and triage potential incidents in line with documented processes with the incumbent present (either in-person or remote using your collaboration tools).
7. Ensuring dual monitoring (in-person 9am-5pm and remote 5-9am) of the environment from week 5, with a defined (temporary) process to manage shift hand-over, and assign/manage alert queue between us and the incumbent (who will be ultimately responsible for the SLAs)
8. Developing a joint incident response Playbook and Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) with you by week 5, outlining clear procedures and processes for responding to major incidents that result in monitoring service impacts during the transition period.
9. As part of the Incumbent exit gate NHSE carry out a quality assurance review, in week 8, of the alerts and events managed by IBM to ensure that we are compliant to the process.
 
3. How we manage absences/change of personnel during implementation
 
In addition to the resources we have designated for NHSE CSOC (e.g., Laura Martin, Doug Pooley, etc), we are able to provide additional resilience through our wider team of 23 SC cleared analysts in our Hursley SOC. Through their work on similar public sector clients, this team have the required skills; are well-versed in your toolset/architecture; and hence, we can pull from this pool to mitigate any absences or changes to personnel during implementation. Whilst we perceive this as a low probability risk, we would plan to manage absences/changes as follows:
• Dedicated Resource Planning and Backup Assignments: We will assign key transition roles to personnel with overlapping skill sets and maintain an active roster of backup personnel for each critical role, ensuring replacements can step in without disrupting operations.
• Cross-Training and Knowledge Sharing: We will conduct cross-training sessions for team members during the onboarding phase to build redundancy in expertise across critical functions. Additionally, we will create detailed process documentation, such as SOPs and playbooks, to ensure the team can access necessary information to cover absences.
• Centralised Resource Coordination: Appoint a Resource Manager to monitor staffing levels, track availability, and respond to unforeseen absences in real-time. We already do resource forecasting in our SOC operations, so we are well-equipped in co-ordinating this.
• Proactive Leave and Absence Planning: Implement a freeze on non-critical leave requests during high-risk transition phases, ensuring adequate staffing levels are maintained. We will start early (i.e., now) by encouraging analysts to minimise their holiday over the 8-week period.
• Incident-Ready Staffing: Maintain our surge capability, with additional resources identified and pre-approved to step in as needed or when you are responding to a critical incident.
• Regular Status Checks and Contingency Drills: Conduct personnel status reviews and simulate absence scenarios to validate readiness and adaptability. We test backup procedures during tabletop exercises to ensure they work as intended under real-world conditions.
• Clear Communication and Role Reassignment Protocols: Establish protocols for rapid role reassignment (e.g., designate each team member a delegate from our wider resource pool). We will use your collaboration tools to provide real-time updates on role changes, responsibilities, and coverage plans during personnel transitions.
 
 

4. Smooth management of knowledge transfer and handover activities

Our transition approach extends beyond the technical and focuses on transitioning the historical context, business needs, and dynamic operating environment. Understanding the evolving needs
of the NHSE CSOC is key in meeting your objectives and driving continuous improvement. As a result, our approach places a clear priority on a robust KT process, designed to capture, retain and transfer essential knowledge [02]. This includes:
• Structured KT Plan: co-developed with you and the incumbent to outline the key knowledge areas (i.e. operational history, processes, tools and configurations). A phased and gated approach to ensure all critical information is fully captured and made accessible.
• Joint Knowledge Sharing Sessions: scheduling of collaborative workshops and simulations with the incumbent to understand different use cases and insights. Previous incidents and monitoring challenges will be the focus, ensuring we have the full context to handle the service.
• Comprehensive Documentation Review and Updates: including SOPs, playbooks, monitoring configurations, incident response protocol and threat intelligence reports to ensure they reflect the current operational environment and best practices.
• Tool and System Familiarisation: our team will undergo hands-on training with your current tools and systems, ensuring the monitoring, alerting and reporting processes are clear.
• Implement Transition gates: to manage the transition from one phase to the next phase and to ensure this can only happen once all required acceptance criterion has been signed off.

To ensure handover activities are handled smoothly:
1. The KT team will upskill our Focal Analyst by week 4 and we will establish clear communication channels, publishing roles and responsibilities for each stakeholder.
2. Our Transition Plan will be reviewed weekly to ensure all activities are completed before we take over the service at week 8.
3. Our Focal Analyst will iteratively upskill our Hursley SOC due to the SOC shift patterns.
4. As soon as the first shift team has received knowledge from our Focal Analyst and has been upskilled, they can commence ‘reverse-shadowing’ from week 5.

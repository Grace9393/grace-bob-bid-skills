Ensuring that inclusivity and accessibility are at the forefront of the design process
 
Inclusive design at IBM means we have taken into consideration accessibility, diversity and sustainability.
IBM places inclusivity at the forefront of the design and development process to ensure personal and adaptive experiences that are benefit both people and the planet.
By developing a deeper understanding of how physical and cognitive disabilities affect the use of a product or service, designers can deliver better human experiences on any device.
Accessible technology respects the uniqueness of every individual. It’s technology with a profound purpose. At IBM, inclusive design is everyone’s responsibility. IBM focuses on restless reinvention, and that means advancing all of the human condition, regardless of age and ability.
 
Design for accessibility
At IBM we believe that designing for accessibility is not just a good idea; we believe it is essential to good design.
 
Design for inclusion
•We focus on designing for inclusion which shows shows how crucial design is in creating a product that makes people's lives more productive, regardless of age or ability.
•Placing accessibility at the forefront of the design and development process ensures that all customer segments have more personal and adaptive experiences.
•Our view is to accessibility as a design challenge and not a constraint.
 
A unique partnership
•IBM Design and IBM Accessibility formed a partnership to help designers understand how physical and cognitive disabilities affect the use of a product, and help accessibility professionals understand creative elements that deliver better user experiences.
•IBM made accessibility an integral function of Enterprise Design Thinking and IBM Design Language, and embedded accessibility into its design boot camps where designers are introduced to accessibility principles, standards and regulations, methodologies, and technologies.
•We integrate accessibility into personas that describe specific user needs, the tasks that they want to accomplish, and their motivations and abilities.
 
Inclusive design
We have the right understanding, access, and opportunities to incorporate accessibility:
Empathy and understanding for different users Our designers understand all kinds of users, including people with disabilities, the aging population, and people who face a situational disability while they use a mobile device.
Tools and testing processes
We identify and correct accessibility conformance issues early in the process to create a more holistic design. 
Opportunities to gather stakeholder feedback
We ensure we get feedback from a diverse range of users. With real feedback, developers can ensure that newly designed solutions are flexible, adaptive, and work for all users in all situations, making everyone's daily routines easier and more manageable.
 
 
Inclusive Design at IBM –Coercive Control Resistant Design
One way we design to be inclusive is to ensure that our designs are resistant to coercive control to protect the safety of our users. IBM focuses on five design principles targeted at combating technology-facilitated coercive control.
 
- Promoting Diversity
IBM understands the importance of having a diverse team in order to broaden the exploration of different users, some of whom may leverage technology in unexpected ways.
- Guaranteeing Privacy and Choice
Users need to be fully in control of their privacy settings and be capable of making informed decisions. Terms such as “advanced settings” should be avoided so as not to intimidate the user into selecting default settings. 
- Combating Gaslighting
Gaslighting is manipulating someone psychology into doubting their own sanity. We realise it is important to have audit trails so users can confirm what actions have been made, by who and when.
- Strengthening Security and Data
It is vital that products only collect and share data that is necessary, to minimise ability for data to be used maliciously.
- Making Technology More Intuitive
Due to the complexities of domestic abuse victims’ lives, they may struggle to navigate new technologies. In order to minimise the power abusers have over victims, technology should be intuitive to use and understand.
